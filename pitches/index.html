OrdersThe brief

The owner gave the committee five base directives and asked it to be as creative as possible inside them.

  1. 1
    Historically accurate set between 1895 and 1925.
  2. 2
    A life sim / RPG of a spy the career, business and private life of a freelance agent as one interlocking resource system (Reilly, Ace of Spies).
  3. 3
    Incredibly deep systems travel (Bradshaw's Guide, 80 Days), deception / conversation / coding, items / artifacts, economy / business, politics / warfare.
  4. 4
    Pressure time-sensitive events in a world that moves without the player, and hidden movement: the player is hunted, as in Scotland Yard.
  5. 5
    An engine efficient and scalable, for dynamic events, decisions, actions and dependencies.

The prohibition

No proposal may be about persons, story, graphics or style. Every idea must be gameplay and systems mechanics, with an argument for why the game would be captivating and addictive to play. Historical institutions, technologies and macro-events may be used as inputs to mechanics; characters, plots, scenes and art direction may not.

C07 · whole-game concept · lead author P01 (Core Loop & Systems Designer)

The Departure Is the Turn

The world is a discrete-event simulation faced by the timetable and the diary; decision points are departures, and every verb sits in the slack between them.

Borda 188/200mean 8.60first-place votes 5ranked between 1st and 5th

Rank 1 of 20 · Borda 188/200 · mean score 8.60 · Lead author P01, audited by P03 (history), P02 (engine) and P09 (hook honesty)

One-line pitch

A spy's thirty-year life played as an itinerary: the world is a discrete-event simulation faced by the railway timetable and the diary, every decision point is a departure, and every verb — meet, cable, forge, trade, bribe, register — takes hours that must fit into the slack before a train that leaves without you.

The hook — what is new

There is no abstract turn and no free time. The world advances through one queue of timestamped events, and choices are made in the gaps between scheduled departures, so the connection — the interval between arriving and leaving, when you are stationary, visible and spending — is the atomic unit of tension. The board you plan on is not the real network but the subgraph you know from owned, dated guides, porters and posters, and it goes stale as the real network changes. Because the hunters ride and read the same timetable, time pressure and the hunt are the same object as travel.

Core loop

  • Every 30 seconds: Decide what fits before the departure you have provisionally booked — a bank drawing in business hours, the poste restante, a meeting, a cable, or waiting out of sight — and choose class, lodging and instrument, each priced in money, hours, health and trace. The diary shows the slack shrinking.
  • Every 10 minutes: Assemble a day of time-stamped slots against the local timetable: mail deliveries, bank hours, a 14:05 express, the hotel register that reaches the police tomorrow. A tight connection risks a seasonal delay; a loose one buys dead hours of exposure and expense. Interrupts — a telegram, a late train, a strike rumour in the evening edition — reopen the plan.
  • Every session (1–2 hours): One commission carried across two or three cities on a chain of connections, with the gauge break, the frontier hall and the night boat as set pieces. Every pause sits within sight of a departure, so sessions end aboard a night train with the world and the hunt advancing until next load.
  • Over a campaign (years of game time): The network thickens and accelerates — tunnels, through services, motor cabs, airships, airlines — while borders harden (1914), currencies break (1917, 1923) and your files thicken. Routes are learned, re-bought, sold and forgotten; the same verbs become faster and riskier; the diary becomes a life.

The systems and how they interlock

  • Travel (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): A date-versioned, multi-modal master graph of services (departure, duration, class, fare, supplement, calendar bitmap) built from monthly guides. The player routes on a known-edge overlay — learned date, source, confidence — while the simulator executes the truth and emits ghost-connection events when an edition has gone stale. Every leg writes records: a named berth is high-fidelity and persistent; a third-class day ticket reveals only mode and direction. Travel necessarily spends time, money and evidence, so every other system hangs from it.
  • Deception / conversation / coding (C04, C12, C20): A legend is a name whose records accumulate under this timetable; two legends stay apart only while no connection could have carried one body between their recorded appearances — the alibi test is a fastest-path query. Conversations run on a fine-grained clock nested inside a slot; a cipher takes hours by hand or minutes by bureau; a cable leaves an office copy. Every assertion is a record, with an authorship field, in the store the hunters read.
  • Items / artifacts (C06, C19): Papers, codebooks, luggage and crates hold itineraries of their own on the same queue — registered luggage follows the ticket, a crate moves at goods speed, a left-luggage receipt is a key. Documents are tested in the frontier hall, a scheduled dwell of fifteen to ninety minutes at a gauge break, and the stamps they collect are dated against the legend's itinerary.
  • Economy / business (C08, C18, C15): Money is instruments with hours and places — a letter of credit draws only at listed correspondents in banking hours; gold is anonymous and seizable; a telegraphic transfer is instant and copied. Rent days, maturities and quarter-days are queue events; burn is continuous and income lumpy, so the ledger forces you onto the network. Commissions arrive as located, time-windowed offers made mutually exclusive by travel time: accepting Baku forecloses Tangier.
  • Politics / warfare (C10, C09, C03): The world calendar writes rows into one dated parameter layer the timetable reads: passport requirements per edge, registration coverage per jurisdiction, liaison between police forces, requisition and suspension of services. Crises climb ladders with literal deadlines; at general mobilisation military flows claim the civilian graph and the Guide stops publishing. Politics is felt as which trains run, which frontiers ask for papers and who may read the register you signed.
  • Time pressure & the world clock: Native. Departures leave without you; windows close; the Meldezettel reaches the police within about a day; a frontier post receives its circular at wire speed but only in office hours and only where liaison exists. Three cadences beat against each other — the day of slots, the institutional rhythm of registers, gazettes and circulars, and the macro calendar of crises and seasons — and their alignments are unauthored set pieces.
  • The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters; C17, C05, C13, C16): Hunters never see you; they read records arriving on institutional clocks, hold weighted hypotheses that advance along the timetable you ride, cannot teleport, and cordon a city only where their men can be in time. Their notices move by cable, their bodies by train; your body moves by train, your description by cable — the night train outruns tomorrow's newspaper but not tonight's wire. Your own trail is always visible as a self-forecast from your own acts; what they have concluded is not.
  • The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends; C11, C04, C08): The diary is also the household's calendar — rent, remittances, club nights, a sanatorium season — and each kept appointment is a witness record. Health is spent by third class and long legs and recovered in weeks of world time that neither the clock nor the hunt pauses; ageing lengthens every timer; skills grow by use and rust by disuse; a year-end audit batches what quietly accrued and stamps the coming year's known closures onto the calendar.

A worked example of play

Berlin, late October 1905. A service's want (C18) pays for copied documents delivered to a handler in St Petersburg within six days; a three-month bill drawn under the same legend matures there on the seventh (C08). The diary shows the slots before the evening Nord-Express: the bank closes at four, so the letter of credit is drawn first; the hotel's Meldezettel will reach the Polizeipräsidium tomorrow (C17), after the train has gone. The planner offers the sleeper — fast, rested, a berth under the legend's name, passport presented by the conductor at the frontier — or second class to Eydtkuhnen, anonymous, a night sitting up, a third more hours: one cost vector, five prices (C02). The known-world overlay marks the Wirballen connection at high confidence from the October edition bought that morning; the September edition would have shown a withdrawn Sunday working. The evening edition carries strike rumours from the Russian lines — the disruption layer's forecast — so the plan buys a day of buffer and a fallback: a Stettin–Petersburg steamer edge learned from a shipping agent's cable, open for weeks yet before the ice. At the frontier everyone changes trains; the passport hall records the name presented and checks the Russian visa. The self-forecast (C13) shows the Berlin description reaching the post by wire only if the Prussian police have cabled, and only in office hours. Then the scheduled Tier-0 event fires: the October general strike closes the Russian graph edge by edge. The sleeper is stranded; the steamer sails. The player who bought the buffer reaches Petersburg by sea, meets the bill and finds the handler's window shut by a day — the lapsed offer reports that a rival delivered. The player who did not sees the bill protested and gazetted, and that name's heat rise in a city they are now locked inside.

Why it is captivating and addictive

The five champion statements converge on one word: honest. "The scheduler is the game" (P02); the timetable was "the period's most pervasive real constraint on a spy" (P03); C07 is "a frame rather than a feature" (P04); "the night-train session end is the pool's most honest 'one more leg'" (P09). The mechanisms are specific. Zeigarnik tension: a provisionally booked departure is an open loop until the errands that justify it are done. Near-miss: a connection made or missed by eleven minutes is the strongest reinforcement in 80 Days, and here every verb produces one. Goal gradient: effort rises as the visible departure nears, so the player rarely pauses. Mastery in the head: slack management, buffer sizing and the known network are skills the veteran feels, not stats. Approach–avoidance: the sleeper you want and the register you fear are one purchase. Information gap: the evening edition, the stale guide and the frontier's unknown demands are gaps the mind must close. Emergent narrative: alignments of the three cadences — a deadline, a frontier closure and a cordon in one week — are set pieces nobody wrote. The natural stopping point is the hotel, and it has an honest cost, the register; the alternative is the 23:40 through train reaching Berlin before the bourse opens, so the player stops aboard it and the session ends on a cliffhanger the schedule generated.

Historical grounding

Accuracy is the rules. The master graph is built from period guides — Bradshaw's monthly Continental Guide, the Reichskursbuch, the Indicateur Chaix, the Russian official indicator — whose editions change at seasonal changeovers (typically 1 May and 1 October) and were suspended in wartime; owning a stale edition is a mechanic. Times pass through local regimes: Russian railways run on St Petersburg time, Russian registers keep the Julian calendar until 31 January 1918, France keeps Paris time until 1911, Ottoman clocks count from sunset — a forged date in the wrong calendar is a tell. At the Russian and Spanish gauge breaks (Eydtkuhnen/Wirballen, Alexandrowo; Irun, Port Bou) passengers change trains in a hall of passport desks, customs and a telegraph office. Registration is a row per jurisdiction and date: Meldezettel in Germany and Austria, innkeepers' registers in France, passport desks in Russian hotels; in Britain nobody is registered before August 1914 (aliens from the Aliens Restriction Order of 5 August 1914, British subjects from the National Registration Act 1915, general hotel registers from 1920). The network is the progression: Simplon 1906, Tauern 1909, Lötschberg 1913; Trans-Siberian through running 1903–04, the Baikal gap closed 1904, regular Circum-Baikal service from 1905, the Amur line 1916; motor cabs c. 1905–10, DELAG airships 1910–14, the London–Paris airline 1919; the Orient Express suspended 1914, the Balkanzug 1916–18, the Simplon-Orient Express 1919. Disruption is dated: Baltic ice closes the Petersburg approach while Libau stays open; strikes in Russia (October 1905), France (1910) and Britain (1911, 1919); civilian trains suspended in Germany and France in early August 1914; the Haparanda–Torneå route with a sledge between the towns until the 1919 bridge. Passport-free Western and Central Europe before 1914, Russian and Ottoman passport regimes, universal controls from 1914 and the League's 1920 booklet are edge attributes with validity windows.

Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies

One global priority queue of timestamped events with total ordering (time, priority, insertion id). Trains, mails, bank openings, register deliveries, maturities, liaison dates, crisis rungs, hunter moves and the player's intentions are all events; entities subscribe to event types; handlers are data-driven rules. Every stochastic draw (delays, examinations) comes from seeded distributions, so a save is seed plus input log and replay is exact — which makes C16's autopsy and C10's inquest computable and doubles as the debugging tool. Timetables are monthly tables (edge, calendar bitmap, departure, arrival, class, fare, supplement); routing is connection-scan over the time-expanded graph; reachability sets are precomputed per timetable period so dozens of hunters cost microseconds. Level-of-detail scheduling runs the player's city and legs in flight at minute granularity and the rest at day granularity, with deterministic catch-up on arrival: a quiet winter passes in minutes, a closing cordon takes an hour. The known-world overlay is a sparse per-agent set of {edge, learned date, source, confidence}; the planner routes on it, the simulator executes the truth and emits discrepancy events. Node dwell — the connection — emits records consumed by C17 (lag, fidelity, readers), C02 (exposure) and C08 (expense). The communications graph (C13) is a second edge table of latencies, office hours and jurisdiction gating over which records reach their readers: C17's lag model, not a second mechanism. The dated parameter layer (C10) supplies edge requirements, registration coverage, requisition and liaison as rows keyed by (edge or jurisdiction, date, tier); a new decade adds rows, not code. The other nineteen candidates attach as event types and handlers on this queue — none runs without it, and it runs without any of them.

Progression and endings (as systems)

What grows: the known network (edges learned, confidence raised, corridors saleable), slack-planning mastery in the player's head, each legend's record-set and credit, standing per circle, skills exercised. What decays: editions at every changeover; unused skills toward a floor; unattended legends; health spent by cheap travel and recovered in world time. What accumulates: records in institutional ledgers, debts with maturities, dossiers per name, a file the alliance calendar may one day copy to a new reader — archives never decay. Endings are system states reached on the timetable (C11): retirement is arriving in a non-extraditing haven with wealth in a currency that still converts, before 1914, 1917 or 1923 slams a door; capture is a cordon closing on a city whose next departure you cannot make; ruin is a protested bill gazetted under the name you wear; succession keeps the world running and hands the next agent every debt and file, while routes and codes persist only in the player's head.

Risks and mitigations

  • "A skeleton, not a motive engine" (P07; echoed by P05, P06, P10). True by design: C07 is the clock. The motive engines are arranged around it — C18's windowed, exclusive commissions and C08's burn and maturities are the reasons to leave the hotel; C01's reconciliation dates are the campaign's reckonings. Design rule: the slack must always have claimants; no hour of the diary is free.
  • Spreadsheet feel (P01.01's own risk). Strong defaults — "next sensible departure", automatic edition purchase at stations, a porter for luggage — make planning depth opt-in; the diary, not a Gantt chart, is the face.
  • Punishing randomness (P04.02). Delay distributions are shown before commitment; a forecast channel (editions, agents' cables, insiders) always exists, so buffer is a priced choice.
  • Skipping one's life (P02.01). The diary shows what will pass before time jumps; interrupts stop the jump; conversation and pursuit run on a nested clock.
  • Off-screen death (P09.01). Deferred leg resolution never kills without warnings visible before boarding; the hotel pause stays cheap enough to be a real choice.
  • Unfair ghost connections (P04.01, P03.07). Confidence cues on every known edge; staleness bites only at changeovers and wartime suspensions; recovery options are always offered.
  • Dead time on long legs (P04.10). Sealed legs carry an action budget — ciphers, forgery, letters for the next port, conversation aboard — and news arrives only at ports or by wireless, so arrival is a reveal.
  • Data volume and accuracy. Model the trunk network exactly from period guides and abstract minor lines to regional defaults; the Historian audits dates as table rows, where errors are cheap to fix.

One-line pitch

Several intelligence services and commercial principals pay you at once, each holding its own model of who you are and what you know; the game is to retire before those models are reconciled.

The hook — what is new

Every paymaster is a faction with a belief store: facts about you, each tagged with provenance and confidence, built only from what you delivered, what it observed and what its other sources corroborate. Your product is the report, composed from tokens you actually hold plus fabrications, and every fabrication is booked as a consistency debt that falls due on a real date. Stores are compared only at dated reconciliation events — each service's audits, and the liaison channels the diplomatic calendar opens between services in 1904, 1907, 1914 and 1917 — so the hunt is born from your own inconsistencies on a schedule you can read in the newspapers. The loop closes on itself: every success credited to a service raises its annual secret-service vote, which funds both the prices it pays you and its counter-intelligence capacity, so your deliveries pay for the hunters who will one day read you.

Core loop

  • Every 30 seconds: In the slack between departures on C07's clock, read a requirement, decide which fragment goes to which service and which gets chickenfeed, pick the cipher that service reads (C20), and compose a report from held tokens — each a C17 record you own or a claim you invent — with every outstanding lie's maturity date shown beside the composer.
  • Every 10 minutes: A city day: meet a handler (the meeting writes a record under the legend that attends), deliver, and see the delivery scored for novelty and corroboration after a verification lag; take payment in a name-bound instrument (C08); service a lie that matures this week — put the partner you invented on the train you named — or roll it over and let the principal compound.
  • Every session (1–2 hours): One requirement cycle across two or three capitals: acquire a document, carry it through a frontier whose regime the date sets (C06), deliver it under one legend to one service and a marked copy under another to a second, then survive an audit whose questions leak what the store believes (C12, C16). Sessions end on a night train with a reckoning in sight.
  • Over a campaign (years of game time): The alliance calendar schedules the reckonings: 1904 and 1907 open channels between employers; October 1909 adds a British buyer and a British hunter; August 1914 fuses Entente counter-intelligence and turns enemy services into hunters with wartime powers; March 1917 abolishes a Russian service and spills part of its files; 1923 networks Europe's police. Budgets grow with your credited successes until a store flips to "double", pay stops and the hunt begins — unless you have already retired the legend or the life (C11).

The systems and how they interlock

  • Travel (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): Requirements are located and time-windowed, handlers sit in specific capitals, and liaison hubs move with the calendar — Paris and London before the war; Rotterdam, Copenhagen, Berne and Madrid inside it — so which services you can serve this week is a routing problem on C07's timetable. Every meeting and payment writes a C17 record under the legend that travelled; carried documents are examined at frontiers, above all at the change of train at the Russian gauge break and at every crossing once passports return in August 1914.
  • Deception / conversation / coding: Reports are token-based, never free text. Each service has its own cipher and legend; a reused keyword is an evidentiary signature that can link two legends. A lie is a liability with a principal (distance from truth), a holder and a maturity (the world-clock event that tests it): service it, roll it over, or buy it back in rapport. Handler conversations are C12's claims and calls, and their questions are the only direct window on what a store holds.
  • Items / artifacts: Deliverables are documents with provenance (C06): originals, photographed copies carrying distinguishing marks, forgeries that pass until cross-checked. Each class has a per-faction value curve (C18) rising toward the crisis it concerns and collapsing when superseded or published — construction tables in the naval race, deployment timetables in the Balkan Wars, shipping schedules in wartime, every secret treaty on its publication in November 1917. Selling twice pays twice and leaves two marked copies in two stores.
  • Economy / business: Retainers, bonuses and audited expenses are paid in instruments drawn under a legend's name (C08); a commission house is a first-class paymaster whose contracts excuse your presence in a city and whose two books launder service money into trade income (C15). Payment follows corroboration: a fabricated delivery is paid provisionally and reverses as credit loss and suspicion when contradicted. Each service's annual vote is a Tier 1 output of the Tier 2 outcomes credited to it, scaling the prices it pays and the watchers and cables it can afford (C05).
  • Politics / warfare: Services and ministries are factions whose requirement generators read their worries from the world state — naval construction tempo 1906–1912, deployment plans 1912–1914, neutral shipping and censorship evasion from 1915 — and whose goals are confined to Tiers 1 and 2 (C10): no service can prevent a war; every service wants the contracts and the credit inside one. Liaison edges open partially and late when states align (C03), close when they fall out, and pass incomplete files to successor regimes; war mode (C09) changes what is bought and who hunts.
  • Time pressure & the world clock: Three rhythms beat against each other: C07's day clock of departures and bank hours, each service's audit cadence, and the liaison calendar whose dates are Tier 0 and therefore foreseeable. Requirements expire with the crises that generate them; a lie's maturity and the yearly vote are scheduled events. The reckoning is in the papers before it is in your ledger.
  • The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters): The hunter is the patron. A store that flips becomes a C05 hunter whose dossier is everything you ever told it, funded by the budget your deliveries built. Reconciliation between two services that know you under two names is itself a linkage event (C04) when the stores share a signature. Hunters read only C17 records, so capture is auditable in C16's autopsy: you see which report hanged you.
  • The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends): Each service relationship is bound to a legend with its own household, accounts, cipher and burn (C04, C08); retainers are the life's income and lapse when a service's worries move on. Thirty years accumulate stores that never forget while ageing (C11) lengthens every recovery. Retirement is the ending the loop is built toward: a stable currency, a non-extraditing haven and a name no store has flipped, before the next reconciliation.

A worked example of play

Spring 1913, between the two Balkan Wars. The player holds a retainer from the Foreign Section of the British Secret Service Bureau under one legend, one from the French Deuxième Bureau under another, and a commission agreement with a Belgian société anonyme brokering Balkan artillery orders under a third. In Vienna, through a clerk cultivated in a railway directorate, he obtains a fragment of Austro-Hungarian deployment tables, a document whose value has climbed since October 1912. He sells the original to the French and a photographed copy, carrying the original's marginal stamp, to the British — having told the British he had no Vienna access, while his hotel's Meldezettel under the British-facing legend reached the Polizeidirektion within a day. Late May 1913 brings a supersession event: a compromise of Austro-Hungarian plans becomes public, the Evidenzbureau revises them, every held copy's value falls to zero, and both services now hold the same report from two sources; the marked copy raises suspicion in both stores. The quarterly British audit asks about Vienna; the lie matures; he rolls it over with a supporting lie about a courier. His credited 1913 successes raise the Bureau's 1914 vote, which pays for an officer to check its freelancers' hotel registers. In August 1914 the Entente channel fuses the two stores: the marked copy and a Playfair keyword reused across both ciphers link the two legends; the British store flips to "double", pay stops, a hunter with Aliens Restriction Act powers takes the file, and the Belgian firm's books are sequestrated in the occupation of Brussels. The remaining moves are the system's: reach Rotterdam and buy back the debt with a corroborated truth, or retire both legends and live on the third.

Why it is captivating and addictive

The engine is sustained dramatic irony: the player always knows more than any one faction and must keep each faction's picture coherent — the pleasurable cognitive load of spinning plates whose collisions are foreseeable but never fully preventable. P09's standard is met because the near-misses are under the player's control: a leak that almost crossed a threshold came from a lie the player chose, so it motivates mastery rather than gambling. Each added master is a self-set difficulty slider raising income and tension together. Dread and relief alternate honestly — dread of the cross-check at each audit and liaison date, relief at each paid, corroborated report — and loss aversion attaches to trust built over years with a patron. The maturing-lie ledger and the dated reckonings are open loops: Zeigarnik tension without an authored cliffhanger, because the calendar that threatens you is history. Mastery lives in the player's head — which services talk, when, and what each checks — and the emergent narrative is betrayal by one's own inconsistencies, so every exposure is a story the player wrote. The natural stopping points are the relief after a paid delivery and the night before an audit; players push through because the next reckoning is visible, the lie that matures tomorrow is theirs to service, and C07's night train turns stopping into anticipation.

Historical grounding

The career is the touchstone's own: freelance and semi-official agents paid per report by several services, often doubling as commission agents, drawing retainers and audited expenses from annual secret-service votes. The buyers are the period's institutions: before October 1909 Britain's Admiralty and War Office intelligence departments, then the Secret Service Bureau with its Home and Foreign sections; the French Deuxième Bureau; the Austro-Hungarian Evidenzbureau; the German General Staff's Abteilung IIIb; the Russian General Staff's intelligence and the Okhrana's Paris bureau, which worked closely with the French police until the Okhrana's abolition in March 1917, when its Petrograd files were partly burned and partly published. The reckonings are real dates: the Entente Cordiale of 8 April 1904 and the Anglo-Russian Convention of 31 August 1907 as the alliances along which cooperation grew, partially and late; Entente intelligence liaison from August 1914; the Aliens Restriction Act of 5 August and the Defence of the Realm Act of 8 August 1914 as the hunters' new powers; the international police commission at Vienna in 1923. What is bought changes inside the rules: dreadnought construction tables during the 1906–1912 race and the 1909 scare, deployment timetables during the Balkan Wars, shipping schedules and censorship routes from 1915, and nothing for a secret treaty once the Bolsheviks publish it in November 1917. Travel obeys the errata: passengers change trains at the Russian gauge break; Western Europe is passport-free until the decrees of late July and August 1914; nobody is registered in Britain before August 1914, so London is where a legend is invented and Vienna, with its Meldezettel, is where one dies. Money is gold-standard until 1914; a retainer in marks is worthless by 1923.

Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies

The faction is one entity type: a belief store, a budget, a requirements generator, an audit cadence and liaison edges. The store is a C17 view — tuples of (subject, predicate, value, confidence, source, time, authorship, readers) — holding only facts about entities of interest: the player's identities, documents and claims. A report appends claim tokens with authorship marked and confidence set by trust; world records (a hotel slip, a cable copy) enter the same store through C17's lag-and-reader model. Consistency debt is a priority queue: each claim carries a test event bound to the world clock, and when it fires the claim is compared with the world log; truth-distance above threshold emits suspicion scaled by the product of the claim's and the contradicting record's confidences. Reconciliation runs only at events — audits, and liaison edges opened by Tier 0 alliance rows in C10's parameter layer — iterating shared subjects between two stores, so cost is linear in shared facts and events number dozens per campaign; between events the stores are inert. Two reconciled stores holding facts about different legends with matching signatures (copy marks, cipher keywords, handwriting) emit a linkage candidate to C04. The budget loop is a yearly event per faction: vote = f(credited Tier 2 outcomes, scare level), allocating requirement prices and C05's watcher and cable budgets; a store flipping to "double" instantiates a C05 hunter whose dossier is the store itself. Everything is deterministic from seed plus input log on C07's global queue, so C16's autopsy shows the exact report, audit and liaison date that produced a flip. Designers author services as data rows — requirement templates keyed to world-state predicates, audit cadences, cipher classes, liaison edges with open date, delay and fidelity, value curves per document class — so adding a service is a row and some edges, and the expensive reasoning, hunter inference, stays in C05 and budget-bounded.

Progression and endings (as systems)

What grows: the number of live masters, trust and retainer level per service, the prices a rising vote pays, the network of handlers and cover addresses, and the player's knowledge of who talks to whom and when. What decays: trust without deliveries, a service's need for you as its worries move, and every document's value along its curve. What accumulates and never decays: the stores — everything you ever told each service — rolled-over consistency debt, credited successes turned into hunter budgets, and signatures shared across legends. The end states are C11's, reached mechanically: retirement when an unflipped legend holds wealth in a stable currency in a non-extraditing haven, the valuation visible beforehand (roubles in 1916 are a readable mistake); a flip followed by capture, survivable — expulsion before 1914, a court-martial inside the war — and explained in the autopsy; turning, when the service that caught you runs you as its double and play continues from the hunter's chair (C05's inverse mode); ruin when retainers lapse faster than the burn; death or succession, the heir inheriting households, debts and a two-subject file. Every year the loop asks the touchstone's question: one more report, or stop while the ledgers are still unreconciled.

  • Legibility of belief states without exposing them (P07.01, P02.10): C16's windows — audit questions, liaison dates in the press, interrogation patterns — and an estimated beliefs view that is never ground truth.

One-line pitch

Legends are the unit of account: each cover identity owns its own flat, bank account, debts, standing and police file, keeps running in its city whether or not you are wearing it, and the hunt advances only by linking two names.

The hook — what is new

The player is never the unit the game accounts for; the name is. A legend is an entity that owns a fact-graph of claims whose evidentiary weights decay unless fed by acts under that name, together with a residence, accounts, obligations, a monthly burn, a standing in each social circle and one dossier per hunting service. Hunters never see a body, only records under names, so pursuit is a linkage problem rather than a chase, and the canonical link is decidable: two names whose records could have been produced by one body on the real timetable are a link candidate, while records that no connection could reconcile keep the names apart. Failure is therefore granular. You lose a name, not the game.

Core loop

  • Every 30 seconds: Choose which name acts, and how. Sign the hotel slip as A or B; pay from A's letter of credit (a specimen signature at a named correspondent bank) or in anonymous gold; post from B's accommodation address or by hand. A linkage-risk indicator, computed from your own records, warns before you arrive as A on a ticket B bought.
  • Every 10 minutes: A day in one city under one name. Feed its record-set (a bank drawing, a club lunch, a registration renewed), collect what piled up at its address, read the own-trail view of what this name has written lately, and decide whether it stays live, rests or is ripe for retirement.
  • Every session (1–2 hours): A circuit. Plan by timetable so that unattended lives do not go stale (rent on the quarter-day, unanswered mail, a lapsed registration) and so that the last record of one name and the first record of the next are provably incompatible for a single body. Arrive and face what the simulation materialises: callers, fines, a creditor's letter, an offer at the club.
  • Over a campaign (years of game time): Legends appreciate (record-set depth, credit, standing) and accumulate exposure (dossier size, links). August 1914 makes nationality a life-or-death attribute; February 1915 puts photographs on British passports and binds one face to every name it is issued under. Names are burnt, retired, sold or linked; the files thicken; the campaign ends when one clean name reaches a haven (C11).

The systems and how they interlock

  • Travel (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): the circuit is a timetable plan on C07's scheduler. Verification latency is routing: a Berlin employer answers a hunter's inquiry in a day, an overseas registry in weeks, so a claim is safe for as long as the mails take. The timetable is also the alibi: C14's habit profiles and a fastest-path query between two presence records decide whether two names can be one body. Class must match the legend (C02), and every frontier tests the papers of the name presented (C06).
  • Deception / conversation / coding: every assertion in a room (C12), on a hotel form or in a report (C01) is a claim written to the acting legend's fact-graph; claims and world records are one C17 tuple differing only in authorship. The legend ledger shows, per name, the claims made, who holds them, their verification cost and corroboration. Each name reports in its own cipher (C20); a captured cipher is a link.
  • Items / artifacts: papers, visiting cards, letters of introduction, monogrammed luggage, tailors' labels and typewriters belong to a name and can betray it: one hand in two registers, one trunk under two names, one typeface in two correspondences. Options folded from the panel's flags: a seizable free-text notebook as the only private record in hard mode (P05.12), and a bounded donor identity, the institutional record-set of a vanished person plus a small hot set of acquaintances with expectation events (P05.11).
  • Economy / business: one ledger per legend (C08). Each name has a class with a monthly burn and a credibility that tracks spend against class; underfunded names decay, overfunded ones attract thieves. Credit is sized to record-set depth, so a thin name cannot borrow enough to make burning it pay, and a burned name's creditor becomes a budgeted private hunter seeking the link to your other names. Households anchor a legend: rent, staff with discretion and loyalty values, a safe house, mail drop and cache, and a talk rate that writes witness records. A firm (C15) is the visible means of support a name must show.
  • Politics / warfare: nationality is a legend attribute read by the regime layer (C10): internment of enemy-alien men of military age, call-up papers, property under custodians from 1914, expropriation from 1917. Liaison is a linkage source: when allied services compare files at a dated reconciliation (C01, C03) and know you under different names, the comparison itself can join them; 1917 spills a fallen service's archive.
  • Time pressure & the world clock: records age and witnesses forget, so links decay; verification clocks run only while someone checks; rent and bills fall on calendars; registration slips reach the police on institutional lags; the document regime flips on fixed dates (passports in Germany and France in the first days of August 1914, British photographs from February 1915 and compulsory passports from November 1915, the League booklet of 1920).
  • The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters): each service holds one dossier per name as a filtered view of C17, and C05's hypotheses propagate per name. Linkage events: a witness who has seen both faces, the same hand in two registers, a draft endorsed from one name to another, the same trunk under two names, a photograph matched across files, gossip crossing a bridging institution where one person sits in two circles, a liaison reconciliation, or presence records the timetable can reconcile. Burning a legend zeroes its heat but forfeits its accounts, contracts, households and friendships and hands every fact it asserted to the hunters; destroying the specific linking record can sever a link instead.
  • The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends): standing is held per name per circle (an officers' mess, a bourse, an émigré colony, a service's desk), decays toward neutral with time and distance, and gossip crosses bridges at a rate, so a scandal under one name surfaces elsewhere weeks later. Names do not age; the one body behind them does (C11): portrait-parlé fields that never change (height, ears, scars) are the body's leak across names, and a wound (C02) is an identifier in every dossier at once. Retirement (C11) needs a name clean enough to live under.

A worked example of play

The hook is honest in P09's sense: every pressure comes from an institution's real clock, never from a manufactured timer. Endowment and the IKEA effect do the work: a legend is built over months of play from registrations, references and trades, so burning one nurtured for twenty hours is a genuine loss the hunt can inflict without ending the game, and choosing which name dies is rich in anticipated regret. Dread of linkage is an information gap, since only C16's windows say which records a service has read. A new legend offers the clean-slate appeal that makes roguelikes compulsive, inside one continuous career. Natural stopping points are arrivals, when a city's mail materialises, and the end of a circuit; players push through because a departure is already in sight (C07), a verification clock is running on yesterday's claim, and an unattended life is a day from going stale. In the panel's words: "identity is the join key of this entire game; failure is granular, lose a name, not the game, which is what lets forty hours of pursuit stay fair" (P08); "the life sim and the hunt become one object" (P09).

Historical grounding

Accuracy is the rule set. Where a name can be invented is a property of the registration regime: Britain is where people are invented, Germany where inventions die. German and Austrian Meldewesen send a slip to the police within about a day and keep resident cards; French innkeepers keep police registers; Russian hotels carry the passport to the police desk and yard-keepers report to the precinct; in Britain nobody is registered before August 1914, aliens only from the Aliens Restriction Act and Order of 5 August 1914, British subjects from the National Registration Act 1915, general hotel registers only from the Aliens Order 1920. So a British legend can be invented until 1915 and a foreign one only until August 1914. Hunters audit legends with the period's own tools: commercial inquiry agencies, professional registers, consular and residence registers queried by post or cable, portrait-parlé description cards, fingerprint files from 1901, typewriter typeface comparison, laundry marks and tailors' labels. Money is name-bound: letters of credit and circular notes draw only at listed correspondents in banking hours against a specimen signature; gold is anonymous, heavy and conspicuous after 1914. The document regime changes inside the rules: passport-free Western Europe against Russian and Ottoman visas before 1914; German and French passports in the first days of August 1914; British photographs from February 1915 and compulsory passports from November 1915; the 1920 League booklet. Internment is staged (selective from August 1914, general in Germany from November 1914 and in Britain from May 1915), enemy property passes to custodians, and 1917 expropriates every Russian household and account. Per the Charter errata, alibi arithmetic uses a change of train at the gauge break and Trans-Siberian through running from 1903–04.

Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies

The identity is an entity with components: a fact-graph of claims (C17 tuples with weight and timestamp), residences (household entities with staff, cache and talk rate), instruments and obligations (C08), registrations, a nationality, a standing vector per circle, papers (C06 items) and a derived heat per service. Every act emits identity-tagged records into the one append-only store; a dossier is an indexed filter over it, so hunters pay nothing to keep files. Linkage is a rule pass run only on new records against indexes: shared handwriting or typeface ids, shared object ids (a trunk, an endorsement chain), witness co-presence, photograph matches, circle-overlap propagation, liaison reconciliation, and timetable compatibility as a fastest-path query on C07's time-expanded graph, bounded and cached per city pair and month. Unattended identities run at low level of detail: their obligation events fire with aggregated outcomes, and on arrival the engine materialises specifics deterministically from the seed (which letters came, who called, what lapsed), so cost follows attention, not the number of lives. Verification clocks are lazy, spawned only when a hunter checks a claim, with latency from the communications and travel graphs. Standing propagates along bridging institutions with rate and decay, breadth-capped per tick. Burning cascades through a dependency graph (contacts, accounts and households bind to the name), and every link event carries provenance, so C16's autopsy replays exactly which record joined two names. Content is declarative: fact types, link rules, registration coverage and lag per jurisdiction and date (rows of C10's parameter layer), class burn tables, institution templates and donor record-set generators. Determinism is inherited from C07: seed plus input log replays the whole portfolio exactly.

Progression and endings (as systems)

What grows: record-set depth, credit, standing, households, and the player's knowledge of each country's disclosure texture. What decays: unfed claim weights, standing toward neutral, links as witnesses forget, registrations as they lapse. What accumulates and never decays: dossiers, archives and the body's unchanging description fields. A legend has its own end states: rested (cheap, decaying), retired cleanly (slow, expensive, a closed record-set), sold (a clean legend is an item with provenance), burnt (instant; every fact becomes evidence) or linked (its dossier merges into another's). The life's end states are C11's, each a query over the portfolio: retirement holds only for a name whose heat is below threshold in every service with reach into the chosen haven and whose assets sit in a stable currency; capture is a dossier that reconstructed the body behind the names; succession passes the standings held by names an organisation knew, not by the person.

Risks and mitigations

  • Cognitive load. Cap live identities at three to five; onboard with one life and add a second only when the first has a record-set worth protecting; the legend ledger must be the best interface in the game.
  • Upkeep as chores. Maintenance must generate opportunities (callers, offers, introductions arriving at a fed address) and be delegable to staff who leak; bookkeeping is aggregated per name.
  • Legend hoarding and borrow-then-burn. Burn by class, reactivation events for dormant names, credit sized to record-set depth, creditors who become hunters.
  • Hunter omniscience or arbitrariness. Links come only from records, reachability is noisy, every capture has an autopsy, and pre-photograph face matching must stay a filing operation weaker than a photograph.
  • Pre-1914 may feel easy. Russia and Ottoman lands are high-paper, high-value regions, and archives make early carelessness expensive later (C03).
  • The panel ranked the clock first. Rightly: this concept assumes C07 as the scheduler and C17 as the record store and attaches to both without modification.
  • Taste. Donor identities stay institutional record-sets, never authored lives.

The Paper Hunter: One Evidence Engine

One-line pitch

You are never observed, only recorded: every act of a spy's life writes a record into some institution's book — hotel slip, berth sheet, cable copy, bank counterfoil, frontier stamp — and the services hunting you can read only what reached their desks, so the whole game is deciding what the paper is allowed to say.

The hook — what is new

Pursuit games show the hunter a position; this one shows the hunter a filing cabinet. Every record in the world is one tuple — subject, predicate, value, confidence, source, time, authorship, readers — and a service's dossier on you is nothing more than the records that reached it through its own jurisdiction and liaison channels. Because lies, reports and forged fields are the same tuple with a different authorship, deception and the hunt become one system; because every deduction is derived from the log, every capture can be replayed, audited and learned from. The player's craft is not stealth but bookkeeping under fear: which name signs, which book it lands in, how long it lives there, and who will ever open it.

Core loop

  • Every 30 seconds: The convenient act writes a record; the awkward one writes none. First class and a grand hotel put your name on a berth sheet and a police slip; third class and gold coin write nothing and cost sleep. A cable leaves a copy at the office for months; a letter leaves nothing and takes three days. Beside every fare the planner shows a self-forecast — computed from your own acts and the published lags, never from the hunters' files — of where each record will be, and when.
  • Every 10 minutes: A day or a leg is planned against the hunters' probable knowledge, not their actual knowledge: the Munich slip reaches the Polizeidirektion tomorrow; the Zurich bank drawing sits behind a warrant; the trunk still carries the Vienna hotel's label. You thin the trail (a bribed clerk, a stolen register page — each a record in itself), choose which legend signs the next book, or lay a decoy: a through-ticket never used, an accomplice signing a distant register, a cable sent from a station you leave by cab.
  • Every session (1–2 hours): A commission carried across two or three cities on C07's timetable; a reveal rhythm you can hear — registers collected nightly, gazettes weekly, circulars monthly; a near-miss when a description lands one node behind you; perhaps a dossier burgled, a page removed, a false sighting inserted; perhaps an arrest, and the autopsy that shows exactly which record did it.
  • Over a campaign (years of game time): The log never shrinks. Dossiers copy along alliance edges in 1907 and 1914, pass with losses to successor regimes in 1917, and reopen under archive reviews; registration regimes thicken from 1914 to 1920, so the same verbs write more paper every year; your early carelessness is still filed when you are fifty. A veteran's dossier is thin because the veteran learned which books to stay out of.

The systems and how they interlock

  • Travel (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): Every mode leaves a different mark (P04.05). A named sleeping-car berth is high-fidelity and persistent; a third-class day ticket reveals mode and direction only; a steamer files a passenger list at the port, and in Britain only steerage appears on the Aliens Act 1905 lists; a cab is remembered for a day. A missed connection (C07) forces an overnight stop and a compulsory register. Frontiers are the richest record-writers, and their requirements are rows of C10's dated parameter layer: nothing in Western Europe before 1914, passports and visas in Russia and the Ottoman lands throughout, everywhere afterwards. Hunters ride the same graph (C05): a record's lag is a scheduled delivery on the same priority queue as the trains.
  • Deception / conversation / coding: Which legend signs is the deception verb (C04); a dossier is kept per name, so the hunt advances only when two names are linked — a witness who saw both faces, one hand in two registers, a draft endorsed from one name to another, one trunk under two names, a shared cable address, a timetable that could not have produced both records, or two services comparing files at liaison. An Assert in conversation (C12), a report token to a paymaster (C01) and a forged field on a passport (C06) are records with authorship set to you; a lie's truth-distance is the gap between that claim and the world log, and the world's clock will test it. Cables are copied at the office and letters pass censors (C20); both are records with readers.
  • Items / artifacts: An item's provenance is the log filtered by item id — issued, bought, stolen, copied, crossed a frontier at a time, pawned, planted — and an inspector's knowledge is its institution's view widened by liaison (C06). A labelled trunk is a record on legs; a notebook is the most dangerous object a player can carry; a stolen dossier is an item whose pages are records, and removing one makes a hunter's deduction regress.
  • Economy / business: Money has a body and a signature (C08): a letter of credit draws only at listed correspondents, by the signing legend, against a specimen signature; a telegraphic transfer leaves a cable copy; gold leaves nothing and weighs. Protested bills and gazetted bankruptcies are public records in the same store, which is how a burned legend's creditor becomes a budgeted private hunter. Decoys cost money and accomplices, and accomplices have trails. Hunters have budgets too: every ledger query is a cost-bounded action funded by a service's annual vote (C01), so your successes pay for the clerks who will read you.
  • Politics / warfare: Readers are political. A record's reader set comes from the cooperation and extradition matrix (service × jurisdiction × offence class × date, C03): a Sûreté lead dies at the Belgian frontier unless Brussels is asked and agrees; London shelters offences that can be framed as political under the Extradition Act 1870's exception, while ordinary crimes are extraditable to Russia under the 1886 treaty — so how a service characterises your act decides who may read your file. War rewrites readers overnight: censors read current cables as routine, enemy archives close, allied ones open, and a fallen regime's files are inherited with a destruction fraction.
  • Time pressure & the world clock: Lag is pressure. A Berlin or Vienna slip reaches the police within about a day; a consular inquiry takes weeks; a cable copy sits for months; a review of retained copies is a rare event the war regime schedules. Scotland Yard's reveal cadence becomes institutional rhythm: hypotheses reweight when registers are collected, gazettes printed and circulars issued — never on a visible countdown. The world clock mutates the tables under you: 31 July 1914 (German passports), 5 August 1914 (British alien registration), February and November 1915 (photographs; compulsory passports), 1920 (the League booklet; general hotel registers in Britain).
  • The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters): C05 reads this store and nothing else. Each service holds weighted hypotheses over each legend's place, time, mode and habit, propagated along the real timetable and reweighted only by delivered records; it profiles you (class, hotels, banks, cable addresses — C14's habit model), queries ledgers where weight concentrates, sits on fixed points, and when weight collapses on a city forms a cordon over its real exits. A fabricated record the timetable makes impossible is discarded and raises that service's scepticism against the evidence class, so decoys are an arms race, not an exploit. What the player sees is governed by C16: the own-trail view, always available, computed from your own records and silent about what was read; and the windows onto inference — institutional cadence, the press, the stolen dossier, the interrogation room, and the autopsy that replays C05's reasoning over these records after capture.
  • The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends): The life writes paper. A sanatorium season hides you and registers you at once; a limp or a scar is a portrait-parlé field that never changes; a household's servants are witnesses with a talk rate; standing per circle crosses bridging institutions as gossip and can link names (C04). Age thins the face in the 1905 photograph but not the height on the card. Capture never ends the life (C11): remand, expulsion, exchange or a sentence that spends years, and the file follows a successor as a two-subject dossier.

A worked example of play

March 1912, pre-war rules. Under legend A — a British machine-tool traveller — the player copies a shipyard tender in Hamburg during a window posted by an arms firm (C18). The act writes nothing; the week around it writes plenty: a Hamburg hotel registration form (high fidelity; with the police within a day; readers: the Hamburg police, and Abteilung IIIb on request), a commercial-code cable to a London cable address from the Hauptbahnhof office (copy retained for months; readable under warrant), a second-class berth on the night boat-train to the Hook of Holland (berth sheet under A), and a first-class landing at Harwich that no Aliens Act list records. In London nobody registers A: in 1912 Britain writes nothing. Ten days later the theft is noticed (a Tier 2 event); IIIb's new dossier holds a porter's fading memory and, after a budgeted query of the Hamburg registers, A's name and the hotel's luggage label. Hypotheses propagate along the March timetable to the Hook, Antwerp and Copenhagen; a second query returns the berth sheet and weight collapses onto the Harwich route. A description reaches London by consular channel, but with no register to query and no address it yields a note and nothing more; the dossier is shelved. The player's own-trail view meanwhile shows the danger they created themselves: the London cable address is also used by legend B, a Swiss national, for bank cables — one tag under two names, a linkage candidate (C04). They retire the address and move B to letters. On 5 August 1914 the Aliens Restriction Order obliges every alien to register: A, British, writes nothing until the National Registration Act of 1915; B must present papers at a police station, and from February 1915 the papers need a photograph. In 1916 a wartime review of retained cable copies — rare, scheduled by the war regime, run by a service now at war with IIIb and curious about A for its own reasons (C01) — surfaces the 1912 form: A's name above the address that B's 1913 bank cables carried. The autopsy, when it comes, shows exactly that page, the two names it joined, and the letter that would have kept them apart.

Why it is captivating and addictive

The argument is honesty, not stimulation. First, attributable consequence: because hunters act only on records, the player always knows which comfort betrayed them — it was the laundry mark — and fairness attribution is the strongest predictor of persisting after loss (P09: "every deduction derivable from one log makes forty hours of pursuit auditable"). Second, loss aversion over something the player built: a clean trail and a thin dossier are possessions, and every register is a small decision to spend them. Third, the information gap without a minimap: the player cannot see what the hunters concluded except through costly windows, so the evening edition and the gazette are sought voluntarily and relief when they are silent is real; dread survives forty hours because it is never resolved for free. Fourth, Zeigarnik tension on a period clock: a slip in transit to the Polizeidirektion is an open loop that keeps the player thinking between sessions. Fifth, theory of mind: modelling the hunter's model, feeding it a through-ticket you never used and watching a cordon form on the wrong city is the richest agency in the pool, and the scepticism learner keeps it an arms race across a career. Sixth, mastery that lives in the head: knowing that a German inn's Fremdenbuch is collected nightly while a British boarding house keeps none, that a berth sheet outlives a cab's memory. The natural stopping points are the hotel register (saving costs cover) and the quiet week before a gazette; players push through because a record is in transit, an edition is hours away, and the night train that outruns tomorrow's slip is on the board.

Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies

Data model: one append-only record store whose rows are the canonical tuple; legends, institutions, services, places, items and messages are keys into it, and every other candidate's store — C01's belief stores, C03's faction files, C04's legend fact-graphs, C06's inspector facts, C12's NPC belief sets, C14's witness log, C19's cargo records, C08's gazettes — is a filtered view, never a copy. Reasoning: a dossier is an indexed filter, essentially free (P02.05); C05's hunters propagate bounded, weighted hypotheses on a hierarchical graph that expands resolution only where weight concentrates, reweight at institutional cadence rather than per tick, and spend a budget on queries; bitset reachability on the time-expanded graph keeps dozens of hunters at microseconds (P02.08); link detection is a declarative pass over co-occurring tags; C14's pattern mining runs at ticks and is cached; a per-service scepticism learner weights evidence classes. Lazy materialisation: only ledgers a hunter actually queries expand into records; the rest remain counts — the world simulates books, not clerks. Determinism: seeded lags and total ordering make a save seed plus input log and replay exact, which is what lets C16's autopsy replay a hunter's inference step by step with provenance (record id → reweight → action) — the same instrument designers use to balance the hunt. Persistence: archive nodes store event ids, not copies; macro-events schedule reviews with per-archive destruction fractions; the event record is never pruned, and compaction touches derived state only (A-P02-1). Authoring is declarative throughout: emission tables, coverage tables, liaison matrices, consolidation cadences, link detectors, scepticism priors. Cost is linear in acts, and acts are what the player and the hunted do.

Progression and endings (as systems)

What grows: the log; each legend's record-set depth, which is also its credibility and its credit limit; the hunters' dossiers; each service's learned scepticism; the player's competence at disclosure textures, visible as a thinner dossier. What decays: witness memory by persistence, hypothesis weight without fresh records, hunter budgets without results, and dormant dossiers, which are shelved rather than deleted and wake on a linkage or a world-clock change. What accumulates: links between names, alliance copies of files, archive reviews. End states are read from the record: capture produces the autopsy and continues the life (interrogation, remand, expulsion, exchange, a sentence that spends years); a burned legend ends a name, not the game; retirement holds only for a name no dossier contains, so the file's thickness is the retirement score (C11); turning hunter is C05's inverse mode; succession hands the successor a two-subject file.

Risks and mitigations

  • "A substrate with few verbs of its own" (P04, rank 10; P07: "infrastructure rather than a defining concept"; P06: "says less about what the spy does all day than about how it is recorded"). True of the store, not of the game on it: the verbs are sign, pay, cable, cross, clean, forge, steal the file, lay the decoy, read the windows — and every verb of C02, C04 and C07 resolves into records here. The mitigation is build order and framing: C17 is the engine's first deliverable because it is also save, replay and the debugger (P01: "an append-only log that is save, replay and dossier at once"); the career comes from C01 and C18, the clock from C07.
  • Thin on economy and politics as a game by itself (P10, P02, P03: "the player still needs a career to run on it"). Agreed; the pitch makes them readers and writers of the store rather than claiming their loops.
  • Spreadsheet feel: the own-trail view must be a map of records with lag timers, never a table; heat is shown only as a derived summary, never as the thing hunters act on.
  • Tuning: persistence and lag set hunts between evaporating and inescapable; hunters too dumb or too lucky. The autopsy is the balancing tool, and redacted autopsies after survived near-misses teach without requiring failure.
  • Legibility discipline: if the self-forecast ever leaks what was read or inferred, dread dies; the own-record/window boundary (A-P01-2, A-P08-1) must be enforced in code, not convention.
  • Cheap decoys: bounded by the scepticism learner, by accomplices who carry their own trails, and by the timetable check that discards impossible fabrications.
  • Table volume: jurisdiction × date coverage tables are large; minor jurisdictions take regional defaults, and readers are gated by C10's layer rather than hand-authored per pair.
  • Players ignoring paper until punished: the first autopsy is the tutorial, and the inverse mode (C05) lets them read a file from the hunter's chair before they have one of their own.

Faster Than the Wire: The Information Front

Your body moves at train speed, news of you at cable speed: race your own description, and trade in the gap before the bourse hears.

Borda 145/200mean 7.50first-place votes 0ranked between 2nd and 14th

Rank 5 of 20 · Borda 145/200 · mean score 7.50 · Lead author P04, audited by P03 (history), P02 (engine) and P09 (hook honesty)

One-line pitch

A spy life-sim with two maps, the railway you ride and the wire that carries news of you, in which every act becomes a race between your body and your description, and knowing a thing an hour before the bourse is both your income and the evidence against you.

The hook — what is new

The period had two networks that ran at different speeds, and this game simulates both as graphs with real latencies. Every fact about an identity or an event is an entity that travels at its medium's speed: minutes between telegraph offices while they are open, a night by mail train, five days by liner, an edition cycle through the news agencies, a week for a photograph by post. The player's body travels on the timetable. The hunt, the newspaper, the bourse and the player's own forecast of where their description has reached are one propagation model read from different seats, so dread and opportunity are the same quantity, latency, and the player can always see their own front but never the hunters' minds.

Core loop

  • Every 30 seconds: choose how an act travels (in person, by courier, by letter, by cable in commercial code) and read the planner's self-forecast beside the fare and the duration; buy or skip the edition and the ticker; decide whether a headline without details is enough to act on.
  • Every 10 minutes: a day of edition times, bourse sessions, office hours and mail collections. A fact acquired at 16:00 opens a window that closes when the agency wire lands at the bourse tomorrow; a visible act writes records whose readers receive them hours or days later; a leg is built so the body arrives where the description has not; a hotel register, a frontier post or a bank counter is a checkpoint against what has arrived there.
  • Every session (1–2 hours): one commission generates two fronts, the secret's news and your own description, moving at different speeds through different channels. You route body, money and messages through the seams: offices that close at night, jurisdictions whose police do not talk, the sea beyond wireless range. The climax is a checkpoint the forecast rates at 40 per cent, and tomorrow's edition tells you whether you were right.
  • Over a campaign (years of game time): the communications graph rewires on the world clock: cable redundancy after 1902, wireless pushing the wire out to sea from the mid-1900s, censors and cut cables from August 1914, Russia going dark in 1917. Mastery is knowing the latencies in your head; the archive of your office copies never shrinks; the prescience accumulator judges how often you were right too early.

The systems and how they interlock

Describe each as rules/resources/states and, crucially, how it feeds the others:

  • Travel (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): the transport graph is C07's, time-expanded and timetabled; a leg costs hours, a fare and a class, and class sets trace fidelity (C02). C13 adds the second graph: telegraph offices at stations, post offices, coast stations, agencies, newspaper offices, bourses, banks, consulates and police posts, joined by channel edges that carry a latency distribution, office hours, a capacity, a jurisdiction gate and a copy-retention rule. The two graphs share nodes, so routing is a latency play: a night express beats tomorrow's morning edition but not tonight's wire; a wire needs an open office at both ends and, across a frontier, a liaison agreement (C03); a sea leg beyond wireless range is dark; a Russian provincial line is slow in both graphs. The same choice runs the other way when you want your letters of introduction to precede you.
  • Deception / conversation / coding: a wanted notice is a cable: a description in portrait-parlé fields travelling as a fact with a confidence value; a photograph travels by post and arrives days later. Because the fastest description is words, the cheapest defence is to change the thing described (C04: switch legend where only the old name has arrived). Commercial code compresses at so much a word; private cipher conceals but attracts censors (C20); every cable leaves an office copy whose readers list (C17) grows under warrant. Planted news enters as a provisional fact that reverts when contradicted; a forged release telegram or a poisoned liaison request is a fact with false authorship in C17's authorship field; what you say in a room (C12) propagates as rumour across C04's gossip bridges at post speed.
  • Items / artifacts: a telegram exists three times, as handed-in form, office copy and delivered copy, each an object with its own readers; editions are dated information objects whose value decays by the next edition; code books, a broker's book, ticker tape and the hotel's registration slip are items that are also records. Luggage and crates (C19) carry trade goods whose city prices (below) are themselves facts that travel.
  • Economy / business: the news-latency market. Each bourse (London, Paris, Berlin, Vienna, St Petersburg, New York; Constantinople for Ottoman paper) holds a fact set and reprices an instrument at its next session only when the relevant fact has arrived: bonds, arms-firm and railway shares, oil, shipping, marine insurance, and after 1914 currencies. A fact known before the agency wire opens a window of hours. Profit is bounded by three emergent rules, not a cap: depth (a position above a fraction of an instrument's daily turnover moves the price against you and emits a notable-dealing record), evidence (every trade is a timestamped record that a service can correlate with later news, C17), and prescience (C10's rule, below). City price vectors for goods, shaped by tariff walls and season, run the luggage trade through C19. Market income pays the burn (C08) and must be laundered through a firm's books (C15) when it is large; it never replaces commissions.
  • Politics / warfare: the diplomatic calendar sets which police wires cross which frontiers (C03's matrix is a gate on the channel edge); regime rows in C10's parameter layer switch censorship on, close offices, cut cables and reroute traffic through neutral copy points; crises (C09) multiply cable volume so queues lengthen exactly when windows are richest. C10's prescience rule bounds foreknowledge: trades or moves correlated with an upcoming Tier 0 event, rather than with a fact whose acquisition is on record, raise a per-faction prescience score; thresholds trigger inquiry, frozen accounts or a recruitment approach; cover reasons discount it; Tier 1 jitter keeps the bet a bet.
  • Time pressure & the world clock: everything here is a scheduled event in C07's queue: edition times, bourse sessions, office hours, mail trains, the arrival of each fact at each reader. The world emits news with a propagation profile per event type, so the player always acts on information that is hours to weeks old (the lag is the pressure), and "wait for the evening edition" is a slot decision like any other.
  • The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters): C13 is the wire half of C05: a notice reaches a post when its fact arrives; C05's hunters reweight hypotheses only on records that have reached their institution and move their watchers by train. Checkpoints query local knowledge only. The player's instruments are a self-forecast computed from their own act log and the published channel parameters, never from faction stores, and C16's windows (editions, agony columns, the interrogation room, the autopsy). Tactics: outrun the front, slow it (a bribed clerk, a frontier crossed before the relay, a wartime cut), change the described thing, or feed it (a false sighting cabled from a station you leave by cab, discounted by C17's scepticism learner).
  • The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends): the life rides the same wires. References and remittances travel by post and telegraphic transfer at the same latencies, so a legend's record-set must arrive before its body to be usable; standing (C04) propagates by gossip at post speed and arrives in a city before you do; a telegraphic transfer (C08) is instant and leaves a copy; your households receive the editions and read about you; being always ahead of the wire costs sleep and health on C02's dial; the office copies you left in 1903 are still there for C03's archive reviews in 1919.

A worked example of play

Constantinople, the second week of October 1908, days after Bulgaria's declaration of independence and the annexation of Bosnia have frozen police liaison between the Porte and Vienna. A want on the board (C18): a Vienna bank will pay for the Porte's decision on a railway concession tender before it is gazetted. The player obtains it at 16:00 on day 0 and two fronts start. The first is the decision itself: gazette tomorrow morning, the agency correspondent's cable at about 10:00, the Vienna agency by 11:00, evening editions, and the Vienna bourse repricing the concession company's shares at the day 1 session. The second is the player's description, which begins to move only when the clerk's absence is noticed at 08:00 on day 1: an Ottoman police circular reaches the Bulgarian frontier post by wire within two hours, but no request goes to the Bulgarian police at all, and the request to Vienna must travel by embassy, which the C03 matrix prices at two days this month. At 17:00 on day 0 the player hands in a fourteen-word commercial-code cable to a Vienna broker; its office copy is now a C17 record whose readers are the cable company and, under an Ottoman warrant weeks away, the police. The broker buys at the opening of the day 1 session, sized under the depth threshold so no notable-dealing record is written, two hours before the agency wire reprices. The body boards the Orient Express that evening: the Bulgarian frontier passes at about 06:00 on day 1, four hours before the post is hot; Serbia has been asked nothing; Vienna is about forty hours out. The self-forecast puts the Vienna police's chance of holding the description on arrival at 15 per cent, rising past 60 for a second night, because a registration slip is read within a day and the embassy request lands on day 3. The player chooses: alight at Budapest, where the request would arrive later still; take payment by telegraphic transfer (instant, copied) or by a letter-of-credit drawing (a signature record); register the Vienna night under the second legend, since the description names the first. On day 3 the evening edition carries an agency paragraph about a theft at the Porte with a description, which is C16's window; the prescience accumulator is untouched because the fact's acquisition is on record; the cable copy sleeps until a review (C03) someday reads it.

Why it is captivating and addictive

The tension/relief cycle is the checkpoint: a register, a frontier, a counter, each a query against what has arrived, resolved by institutions on their own clocks, so near-misses are earned rather than rolled (P09's ballot: "a complete, legible race ... near-misses are earned, not rolled"). Dread is legible without being omniscient (P01.06's phrase): the self-forecast turns anxiety into planning while the hunters' real holdings stay behind C16's windows, which P03 and P08 both name as what keeps the race honest. Information-gap curiosity runs the news half: a headline without detail is a gap the mind must close (P09.05), so the evening edition and the next ticker strip are irresistible, and the market makes the gap pay in numbers while the hunt makes it cost in sleep. Mastery lives in the player's head: knowing that a provincial office closes at eight, that the agency reaches Paris before Berlin on a Latin story, that a liner is dark for three days, is competence in the self-determination sense, and a veteran plans a crossing in seconds. Meaningful choice is the speed-permanence trade on every message: the cable is instant and copied, the letter slow and private, the courier a body with a timetable. Emergent narrative is the record itself: the description that arrived an hour after you left is reconstructed, when it matters, in the autopsy. Natural stopping points are the end of a day's editions and a bourse's close; players push through because a session ends aboard a train with the forecast showing where the description will be at arrival, and because the window you are inside closes at a published hour.

Historical grounding

Accuracy is the latency table. The submarine cable network was dense by 1895 and redundant after the All Red Line closed in 1902; a transatlantic cable cost about a shilling a word and companies kept office copies, readable under warrant; commercial codes (Bentley's from 1906) compressed meaning for anyone holding the edition. News moved through the Reuters–Havas–Wolff ring, each agency relaying its territory, into multi-edition dailies in the capitals and a day later into the provinces; Exchange Telegraph tickers had fed London since 1872. Wireless entered the model on the world clock: coast stations and shipboard sets from the first years of the century, regular liner news bulletins from 1904, and in 1910 a wanted notice overtook a transatlantic liner for the first time; before that a ship at sea was five dark days. Post ran at night-mail speed with several deliveries a day in cities and pneumatic tubes in Paris, Berlin and Vienna; there was no transatlantic telephony until 1927. Police knowledge travelled by telegraphed circular and by posted description cards, fingerprint cards from 1901, through liaison that followed treaties and alliances (C03). The rules change inside the period: the November 1905 Russian postal-telegraph strike blacks out the wire; on 5 August 1914 the Channel cables to Germany are cut and German traffic to the Americas passes through neutral copy points; cable and postal censors read current traffic and admit only deposited codes; a Press Bureau filters editions; most bourses close at the end of July 1914, London from 31 July to 4 January 1915; Russian registers and editions date in the Julian calendar until 31 January 1918 and Russian railways keep Petersburg time (C17's clock rule); the intercepted cable of January 1917, published on 1 March, is the archetype of an office copy that fights back.

Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies

Data model: two graphs over shared place nodes. The transport graph is C07's time-expanded timetable. The communications graph has institution nodes and channel edges with a per-date parameter row: latency distribution, office hours, capacity, jurisdiction gate (C03's matrix), censorship state and copy retention, all rows of C10's single parameter layer written by macro-events. A fact is C17's tuple (subject, predicate, value, confidence, source, time, authorship, readers); propagation is not a second mechanism but C17's lag-and-reader model computed on this graph: an act or event emits a fact with a propagation profile (which channel types carry it, with what degradation), and the scheduler posts arrival events at reader institutions into C07's priority queue. Evaluation is lazy and hop-bounded: a fact's arrivals are materialised only when a reader queries (a frontier post checking papers, a hunter reweighting, a bourse session repricing), so cost scales with queries, not with facts in flight. Facts exist only for subjects of interest (player identities, tracked events, instruments), and readers who never query hold nothing. Hunters (C05) and NPC institutions never read ground truth; they query their own node. The self-forecast reuses the propagation code on the player's own act log with published parameters and no faction state, so it is cheap and cannot leak. Markets are functions: each bourse's price for an instrument is a deterministic function of the facts that have arrived at that bourse plus a seeded mean-reverting term and a depth response, so there is no per-tick market simulation; trade records are C17 rows with the broker and bourse as readers, and the correlation rule (who dealt before the news) runs only when a service investigates. Determinism: every latency draw is seeded, every arrival event is totally ordered in C07's queue, so a save is seed plus input log and C16's autopsy replays the exact chain. Authoring is tabular: offices and hours, channel latencies by year, agency territories, edition times, censorship rows, event-type propagation profiles and structured headline templates; adding a decade adds rows. Scale: a few hundred institution nodes, thousands of facts per campaign, each with a handful of arrivals.

Progression and endings (as systems)

What grows: the player's latency knowledge, held in the head and unrecorded; a network of assets with reservation prices and leak risk (telegraph clerks, agency stringers, brokers, a cable address, a cover address), each an institution-bound node that can be bought, burned or turned; cover reasons for being early, which discount prescience. What decays: facts' confidence and readers' attention; a cold description stops being queried, though it is never deleted. What accumulates: office copies and trade records in archives that C03's reviews may reopen; the prescience score; the market's depth response to a known name. End states are C11's, reached through this layer: retirement holds when no live front reaches the haven's readers; capture is a checkpoint querying a fact that arrived first; ruin is a frozen account after a prescience inquiry or an exchange closed with your position open; turning is C05's inverse mode run from the wire's side; death at a checkpoint is possible but the autopsy makes it legible, and the second agent inherits a world whose archives remember the first.

Risks and mitigations

  • Collapse into flight (P01 ranked it 9th: "narrows the life toward flight and arbitrage"; P05, P06 and P10 name the same risk). Mitigation: most fronts are opportunities, not threats; the same layer is the player's only window on the world, so most play is reading and using latency, not running from it; the self-forecast usually reports that no reader holds you yet, so standing still is the common correct answer; running has C02 and C04 costs (health, abandoned households).
  • The life is absent (P10 ranked it 14th: "after amendment its substrate is C17 and the life is absent"; P02: "a spine for the pressure half of the brief"). Honest answer: C13 is the pressure-and-opportunity spine and is pitched coupled to C04 and C11, whose legends, standings and remittances ride these wires; it does not replace them.
  • A stock minigame (P02.09's own risk). Mitigation: bounds are emergent (depth, trades as evidence, prescience) and income is tied to C08's burn and C15's laundering; no exogenous cap, no exploit-free profit.
  • Latency tuning: if the wire always wins the race is unwinnable; if offices never matter it is trivial. Mitigation: office hours, liaison gates, capacity queues in crises and the sea are the designed seams; the MVP exists to tune the margins (P05.04's note that post speeds near express speeds narrow races).
  • The minimap exploit (P01.06's first risk). Already closed by A-P08-1: the self-forecast reads only the player's own log; hunter inference appears only through C16.
  • Replay foreknowledge of the macro-calendar (P03.08). Mitigation: Tier 0 dates are immovable but Tier 1 jitter and contingent micro-events keep timing approximate, and C10 prices prescience as evidence.
  • Teaching the network without a lecture (P02.02). Mitigation: editions and the autopsy teach latencies by showing them; the inverse mode of C05 lets the player run the wire from the other chair.
  • Procedural news text. Mitigation: structured headlines and tables, never articles.
