# Round 1A Proposals — Simulation Engine Architect (Panelist P02)

## Panelist statement
I build simulation engines, and I read the five directives as one requirement: a continent-sized world that keeps moving whether or not the player is looking, cheap enough to run thirty years of it on an ordinary machine, and authorable by designers as data rather than code. The game must get one thing right: a single unified event scheduler through which trains, telegrams, markets, crises, debts and hunters all flow, so that every consequence is a real scheduled event, every deduction by the hunters is derived from real trace data, and nothing is faked behind a curtain. Each proposal below is player-facing gameplay that only exists because of that architecture.

## P02.01 — Elastic Time
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** No turns, no fixed tick. The world is a discrete-event simulation: time jumps from the player's present to the next event that touches them while everything else advances through the same queue. A quiet winter passes in minutes; a closing cordon takes an hour.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The diary is the scheduler's visible face: commitments and expected arrivals in order (a departure, rent due, an awaited cable, tonight's hotel registration reaching the police). Choose an action; the engine advances to the next relevant event, resolving the world between. Waiting costs: expenses accrue, hunters move, offers expire. Granularity follows danger: minutes near a reveal, days at sea.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Travel: departures are queue events. Deception: cover stories must fit a checkable timeline. Economy: debts fall due. Politics: ultimatums and mobilisation timetables. Hunt: pursuers' moves share the queue, unseen but felt.
- **Historical grounding:** Bradshaw's monthly guides, several daily postal deliveries, multiple newspaper editions, hotel registration deadlines, the 1914 mobilisation timetables that made war's outbreak a railway problem.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** "One more event" compulsion: each advance delivers a result and a decision. An opaque queue makes arrivals variable-ratio reinforcement. Tension/relief cycles emerge from event density, not scripting; the world's indifference makes agency precious.
- **Engine implications:** A global priority queue of timestamped events with dependency edges; entities subscribe to event types; level-of-detail scheduling runs distant entities coarsely, nearby ones finely, with deterministic catch-up from seed plus aggregate state on arrival. Total ordering (time, priority, insertion id) makes saves "seed + input log" and replay exact. Handlers are data-driven rules.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players may feel they skip their own life; the UI must show what will pass; conversations need a fine-grained "now" within the frame.

## P02.02 — Faster Than the Wire
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The spy's life is a race between the player's body on the transport network and news of the player on the communications network. Facts travel at their medium's speed: a telegram reaches any cable office in minutes but carries only words; a photograph takes days by post; rumour walks.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Act (steal, broker, bribe). A notoriety map shows what each place knows about each identity. Route to beat the slow channels and land where the fast one does not yet carry your face: a night train outruns tomorrow's newspaper, not tonight's wire. Bribe a telegraph clerk to delay the wire; switch identity where only the old name arrived.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and hunt primary. Deception: identities are the subjects of facts. Coding: ciphered police cables act slower. Economy: bribes, cable costs, speed. Politics: wartime censorship and 1914 passport controls add checkpoints.
- **Historical grounding:** Telegraph against post latency; wanted notices in daily editions; photographs by post; Scotland Yard fingerprint cards from 1901; the 1910 arrest of a transatlantic fugitive by ship's wireless.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** A legible risk model rewards planning; near misses ("the description arrived an hour after I left") are the strongest hook. Loss aversion over identities; mastery of two overlaid networks; the rush of being just ahead.
- **Engine implications:** Two graphs: time-expanded transport, and an information network of channels with latency distributions and capacities. Facts are entities (subject, content type, confidence, timestamp); propagation schedules arrival events at institution nodes; NPCs query their institution rather than holding knowledge; hunters search only facts that reached theirs. Hop-count bounded.
- **Risks / open questions:** Must not collapse into pure flight; latencies need tuning so the race is winnable but tense; teaching the network without a lecture.

## P02.03 — The Default Timeline
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The thirty years run as history did unless the player pushes on a precondition. Macro-events are nodes of a dependency graph with default dates, preconditions and elasticity bounds; each playthrough jitters them by days to weeks; player and faction actions push them further within bounds. The player's life is a diff against history, revealed at the end.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Newspapers leak the default timeline. Commissions touch preconditions: an arms delivery before a mobilisation, a stolen memorandum that shifts a ministry's belief. Ride history (profit from approximate foreknowledge) or lean on it; every push draws counter-intelligence attention. At death, capture or retirement, an inquest shows which events moved, by how much, traced through the graph to the player's acts.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics/warfare and time pressure primary. Travel: wars close routes and rewrite the graph. Economy: foreknowledge trades. Deception: influence operations. Hunt: services escalate against whoever perturbs their plans.
- **Historical grounding:** The macro-event clock; the July 1914 chain of ultimatums; mobilisation timetables; the 1907 Anglo-Russian Convention; railway concessions as diplomatic causes.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Agency against a known script: the fantasy of "could I have changed it" and dramatic irony as a resource. The inquest gives systems-generated closure. Jitter keeps foreknowledge approximate, so replay stays uncertain.
- **Engine implications:** Macro-events as a DAG (preconditions as world-state predicates, default date, elasticity, consequences) as data tables; a constraint propagator reschedules dependents when a node moves; every state change carries provenance (causing event id), so the inquest is computed, not written. Deterministic given seed; jitter drawn from it.
- **Risks / open questions:** The Historian may object to any movement; butterfly effects must be bounded by elasticity; players may feel railroaded if bounds are tight. Risky but potentially brilliant.

## P02.04 — Several Lives at Once
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player maintains several identities, each an entity with residence, bank accounts, acquaintances, police registration, mail, debts and obligations in a different city; all keep running whether or not the player wears them. Switching lives means travelling there and facing what piled up.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Spin plates. An unattended legend goes stale: unpaid rent brings eviction and a police inquiry; unanswered mail drops a contact; a lapsed registration draws a fine and attention. Each identity has its own notoriety and assets. Plan a circuit by timetable; delegate upkeep to a housekeeper or clerk who reports by cable, at cost and leak risk. The hunters win by linking two identities.
- **Directive coverage:** Life-sim primary. Travel: the circuit. Deception: legends as maintained entities. Items: papers and letters of credit per name. Economy: accounts in several currencies. Politics: a German-national identity in London is interned in 1914. Hunt: hunters track identities, not the player.
- **Historical grounding:** Households in several capitals (the touchstone career); hotel registration; letters of credit issued to a name; enemy-alien internment from 1914; nationality-bound visa regimes thereafter.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Plate-spinning anxiety and relief; sunk-cost attachment to each life; "I must be in Petersburg before rent day" appointment compulsion; combinatorial strategy; the dread of a link being made.
- **Engine implications:** Identity as an entity with components (residence, accounts, registrations, relationships, obligations). Unattended identities run at low detail: obligation events fire with aggregated outcomes; on arrival the engine materialises specifics deterministically (what mail came, who called). Hunters deduce over identity entities; shared evidence (handwriting, photograph, luggage) triggers a link. Obligation templates are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Cognitive load; cap at three to five identities; maintenance must generate opportunities, not chores.

## P02.05 — The Dossier Is Real
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Each hunting service keeps a file on each player identity, and that file is literally a filtered view of the game's own event log: only events whose evidence reached that service. Steal it and you read exactly what the hunters know, gaps included.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Actions emit evidence records with a channel (witness, register, ticket, cable copy, fingerprint) that reach services through the information network; a service's deduction runs only over its dossier. Obtain it by burglary, a bribed clerk or a double agent, then act: remove a page (deduction regresses), insert a false sighting, or protect the gap you now know exists.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt primary. Deception: forging your own file. Items: the dossier is an artifact with provenance. Travel: gaps are where you move freely. Economy: files are bought and sold. Politics: 1907 and 1914 liaison channels merge files; 1917 spills a service's archive.
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana card indexes and Paris bureau files; Special Branch registries; fingerprint cards; wanted notices; the 1909 Secret Service Bureau's registry; wartime Allied liaison.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Information-gap curiosity (what do they know?) resolved into relief and dread; agency over your own history; legible deduction builds trust that the hunt is fair, making being hunted exhilarating rather than arbitrary.
- **Engine implications:** Append-only event log with data-driven evidence emission rules per event. A dossier is an indexed filter over the log, essentially free. Deduction is a rule engine over the dossier producing weighted hypotheses. Dossier edits are themselves events, so tampering is replayable and discoverable. One log drives save, replay and the hunt. Old evidence compacts.
- **Risks / open questions:** Readability of a raw file; exposing too much state; deduction must feel human, not perfect.

## P02.06 — The Operations Board
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player plans an operation as a dependency graph of tasks with time windows and conditional branches, then hands it to the same scheduler that runs the world and watches it execute, intervening when it breaks.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Lay tasks on a board: obtain an Ottoman visa by Thursday; take Friday's through-train from Vienna to Constantinople; meet the contact within two days; copy the plans (camera, one night); leave by Tuesday's Smyrna steamer; else fall back to Salonika. Tasks declare prerequisites (items, money, identity), duration and risk; sub-agents can take tasks. The engine validates against the timetable and shows slack. In execution the world intrudes (strike, cordon, late train); the board shows the cascade and the player repairs it.
- **Directive coverage:** All: travel (timetabled legs), deception (cover per task), coding (scheduled cable check-ins), items (prerequisites), economy (cost and cash flow per task), politics (operations are commissions), time pressure (slack is the key resource), hunt (each task emits evidence).
- **Historical grounding:** Period operations were logistics: visas, letters of introduction, cover firms, cable check-ins, through-train connections, consular registration.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Planning pleasure, anticipation while the plan unfolds, and flow when repairing under pressure. The board makes deep systems legible, so mastery grows from single legs to multi-agent operations.
- **Engine implications:** Plans are declarative DAGs with time windows; critical path and slack are computed against the time-expanded transport graph; the plan compiles into scheduled events with dependency edges; failures trigger re-planning of affected subtrees. NPC factions and hunters use the same plan format, so one planner serves all agents. Task templates are authored as data.
- **Risks / open questions:** UI complexity; drifting into project management; keeping moment-to-moment agency while the plan runs.

## P02.07 — Every Object Has a History
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every item is an entity with a provenance chain, and its safety is a live property computed from what the world has since learned. A forged passport is good until its forger is arrested; days later every document from his hand is a known forgery at every frontier post the news has reached.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The inventory shows provenance and estimated heat. Is this revolver traceable through a dealer's ledger? Are these blueprints from a batch now reported missing? Launder items (re-register, re-stamp), sell them on (heat transfers to the buyer), or hide them in luggage compartments. Stolen plans are valuable only while the theft is unknown and the buyer lacks them.
- **Directive coverage:** Items primary. Deception: forged papers. Travel: customs inspections check heat. Economy: provenance sets price; stolen-goods markets. Politics: a document's value depends on which service lacks it. Hunt: recovered items feed the dossier. Time pressure: heat moves.
- **Historical grounding:** Serial-numbered firearms and dealer ledgers; consular passport registers; banks circulating notices of stolen bonds; Lloyd's registers; customs stops at every frontier.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Loss aversion and the endowment effect: a trusted passport turning hot is felt as a wound. Risk assessment becomes a skill; a ticking inventory creates urgency; laundering plays are satisfying; items accumulate stories.
- **Engine implications:** Items are entities with a provenance log linking to other entities and events. Heat is lazily evaluated on inspection by walking provenance links against the fact store of the inspecting institution; no per-tick updates. Burn propagates as fact events through the information network. Item types declare which provenance facts they emit.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players ignoring provenance until punished; chain depth must be capped and summarised.

## P02.08 — Hunters Ride the Same Trains
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Pursuers are agents on the same timetable as the player. They cannot teleport: they wire ahead and board the next departure. Cordons form only where their agents can physically be in time: the hunt is a race across a Bradshaw's graph.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each movement leaves a mode trace (ticket, hotel register, frontier stamp). Hunters receive traces with latency and compute the player's possible positions. They post watchers at chokepoints they can reach first (frontier stations, ports, the Russian gauge-break) and wire local police. Reveals (a registration, a sighting) collapse their set. The player exploits night trains that outrun the morning dispatch, chokepoints hunters cannot reach in time, and mode changes that split their set.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary. Deception: false registrations poison the trace. Items: tickets and stamps are traces. Economy: first class leaves sharper traces. Politics: war adds checkpoints and changes who hunts. Time pressure: constant.
- **Historical grounding:** Continental timetables; frontier stations and gauge breaks; cabling police ahead; passport-free Western Europe before 1914 against Russian and Ottoman controls.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Scotland Yard's deduction tension made geographic and legible: "can they reach the frontier before me?" is computable by the player too, so outwitting feels earned. Near misses on platforms.
- **Engine implications:** One time-expanded transport graph per timetable period serves player routing, hunter routing and reachability. Hunter belief is a set of possible positions propagated as bitsets per time slice; cordon planning is a cut over that set within the hunters' own reach; reachability is precomputed monthly. Dozens of hunters cost microseconds. Trace emission is data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Exact reachability makes hunters too strong, so belief needs noise; overlaps with travel and hunt designs; merge.

## P02.09 — Trading Ahead of the Wire
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Prices on each bourse change only when news arrives there, and news travels at cable speed. Espionage puts the player hours or days ahead of the markets; profit is informational arbitrage, and it leaves a pattern hunters can read.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Hold bonds, arms-firm shares, commodity contracts and currencies across London, Paris, Berlin, Petersburg and New York. Events emit news; each bourse reprices on arrival. Knowing a contract award, a mobilisation order or a drilling result before the wire opens a trading window. Every trade is a timestamped record; a service that correlates "who traded before the news" acquires evidence; sudden fortunes must be laundered through front businesses.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy primary. Coding: ciphered commercial cables to brokers. Travel: being at a bourse, or instructing a broker by cable. Politics: crises are the news. Deception: fronts and nominees. Hunt: trading patterns are evidence. Time pressure: windows of hours.
- **Historical grounding:** Gold-standard parities; cable-linked bourses; commercial code books; arms firms' shares; Baku and Persian oil; the exchange closures of July 1914; the rouble's collapse; the 1923 mark.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The thrill of an edge; variable rewards; greed against exposure; mastery of reading news flow; the fantasy of funding espionage with espionage.
- **Engine implications:** Prices are entities repriced by news-arrival events from the same information network as P02.02; each bourse holds a set of known facts, so price is a data-driven function of known facts; no per-tick market simulation. Trade records are evidence; a pattern rule runs lazily when a service investigates. Scales to dozens of bourses and hundreds of instruments.
- **Risks / open questions:** Exploitability and balance; profits must tie to life costs and commissions, or it becomes a stock minigame.

## P02.10 — Serving Several Masters
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every intelligence service holds its own belief model of the player. The player can be paid by several at once; exposure comes not from script but from liaison events, when two services exchange information and their beliefs are compared.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Take commissions from several services; feed each a version of the truth through reports, forged documents and observed behaviour. A beliefs view estimates each service's model, imperfectly. Watch the political calendar: the 1907 convention opens a channel between two employers; 1914 fuses Entente counter-intelligence; 1917 shatters the Russian service and leaks its files. When a liaison fires, contradictions become suspicion events: interrogations, cut-offs, or a hunt.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics and deception primary. Hunt: suspicion escalates into pursuit. Economy: several salaries. Travel: handlers in different capitals. Coding: a separate cipher per service, and a captured cipher links identities. Items: reports as artifacts. Time pressure: liaisons are dated.
- **Historical grounding:** Freelance agents serving several services; Entente intelligence liaison, especially 1914–18; Okhrana files seized, partly burned and partly published in 1917.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Greed against dread with a visible political calendar of reckonings; the craft of consistent lying; betrayal emerges from systems rather than authorship, so every exposure is the player's own.
- **Engine implications:** A belief store per faction: facts as (subject, predicate, value, confidence, source, time). Liaison events, scheduled by the macro timeline or emergent, run a reconciliation rule over shared subjects; contradiction yields a suspicion event scaled by the product of confidences. Comparison happens only at liaison, bounding cost. Beliefs cover only entities of interest. Update rules are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** An honest beliefs view without full disclosure; liaison frequency; complexity for new players.

## P02.11 — The Archive Remembers
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Nothing sent or signed is ever gone. Telegraph companies keep cable copies; hotels, banks and censors keep registers, ledgers and files. These archives persist; later events (a war, a new codebreaking office, a revolution seizing files) schedule reviews that can discover the player's acts years later. The past catches up by mechanism.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Early careers are careless (commercial-code cables, real names in registers) and the archive accumulates. Later, world events trigger reviews: 1914 censorship offices read back cable copies; a service breaks an old cipher class and decrypts stored traffic; 1917 revolutions publish police files. Pre-empt: destroy copies (burglary, bribery, fire), buy the archive, or choose media that leave none (courier against cable), trading speed for permanence.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and time pressure primary, on a fuse of years. Coding: cipher strength sets how long the archive stays dark. Items: archived documents. Economy: ledgers. Politics: regime change opens archives. Travel: going back to burn a register.
- **Historical grounding:** Retained cable copies; wartime cable censorship at relay stations; the Okhrana archives seized and partly published in 1917; the Zimmermann telegram.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Dread of the long fuse rewards foresight; the touchstone arc of the past catching up emerges from systems; clean-up operations arise from one's own history; the catharsis of burning the last copy.
- **Engine implications:** Archive nodes store event ids, not copies: negligible cost. Macro-events schedule reviews with rule-based selection (which archives, period, cipher classes). Decryption is a probability over cipher class and elapsed time. Discoveries enter the information network as evidence. Deterministic, indexed by subject. Archive policies are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players may feel punished for early play, so foreshadowing and mitigation matter. Risky but potentially brilliant.

## P02.12 — The World Posts Its Own Jobs
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** No authored missions. Factions (services, arms firms, ministries, committees) derive goals from the world state and plan to achieve them; when a plan needs a human courier, thief, broker or forger, the engine emits a commission, and the player is one candidate among NPC agents.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each identity sees a market of commissions filtered by reputation, location and what factions believe about it. Accept, execute (the Operations Board), get paid; reputation and beliefs shift, changing future offers. Factions compete for the same object (a concession, a set of plans), so taking one job closes others and creates enemies. Being slow means an NPC agent takes the job and the world moves on without you.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics (faction goals) and economy (commissions, concessions) primary. Travel: jobs are located. Deception: jobs need cover. Items: jobs are about objects. Time pressure: offers expire; NPC rivals compete. Hunt: counter-intelligence factions post jobs whose target is the player.
- **Historical grounding:** Arms-commission agents; concession brokering in Persia, Manchuria and Ottoman lands; services hiring freelancers; the Boer War arms trade; Persian oil from 1908.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Coherent infinite content; the sense of a living economy of intrigue; rivalry with NPC agents; agency in choosing clients; long arcs because factions remember.
- **Engine implications:** Goal-oriented planners per faction over a shared world model decompose goals into tasks; tasks needing an agent become commissions in a bid market; NPC agents are coarse entities executing tasks as scheduled events with probabilistic outcomes. Factions re-plan only when subscribed state changes, keeping planning rare. Goals and task templates are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Generated jobs feeling samey; needs a wide template library; emergent degenerate loops.
