# Round 1A Proposals — Simulation Engine Architect (Panelist P02)

## Panelist statement
I build simulation engines, and I read the five directives as one requirement: a continent-sized world that keeps moving whether or not the player is looking, cheap enough to run thirty years of it on an ordinary machine, and authorable by designers as data rather than code. The game must get one thing right: a single unified event scheduler through which trains, telegrams, markets, crises, debts and hunters all flow, so that every consequence is a real scheduled event, every deduction by the hunters is derived from real trace data, and nothing is faked behind a curtain. Each proposal below is player-facing gameplay that only exists because of that architecture.

## P02.01 — Elastic Time
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** No turns, no fixed tick. The world is a discrete-event simulation: time jumps from the player's present to the next event that touches them, while everything else advances through the same queue. A quiet winter passes in minutes of play; a frontier crossing with a cordon closing takes an hour.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player's diary is the visible face of the scheduler: a time-ordered list of commitments and expected arrivals (07:15 departure; rent due Friday; cable reply within three days; tonight's hotel registration reaching the police). Choose an action; the engine advances to the next relevant event, resolves the world in between, and presents what arrived. "Wait" is an action with costs: expenses accrue, hunters move, offers expire. Granularity scales with danger: minutes when a reveal is imminent, days on a steamer to Bombay.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Travel: departures are queue events. Deception: cover stories must fit a timeline the diary makes checkable. Economy: debts and deliveries are scheduled. Politics: ultimatums and mobilisation timetables are queue events. Hunt: pursuers' moves and reveals sit in the same queue, unseen but felt.
- **Historical grounding:** A period run by printed schedules: Bradshaw's monthly guides, several postal deliveries a day, multiple newspaper editions, hotel registration deadlines, bank settlement days, and the 1914 mobilisation timetables that made war's outbreak a railway problem.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** "One more event" compulsion: every advance delivers a result and a fresh decision. The queue is opaque, so arrivals act as variable-ratio reinforcement. Tension/relief cycles emerge from event density rather than scripting; the world's indifference makes agency precious.
- **Engine implications:** A global priority queue of timestamped events with dependency edges; entities subscribe to event types; level-of-detail scheduling runs distant entities on coarse weekly events and nearby ones on fine ones, with deterministic catch-up from seed plus aggregate state when the player arrives. Total ordering (time, priority, insertion id) makes a save "seed + input log" and replay exact. Hundreds of thousands of pending events are cheap; handlers must be data-driven rules.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players may feel they are skipping their own life; the UI must show what time will advance past; conversations need a fine-grained "now" inside an elastic frame.

## P02.02 — Faster Than the Wire
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The spy's life is a race between the player's body on the transport network and information about the player on the communications network. Every act emits facts (a name, a description, a photograph, a fingerprint card) that travel at the speed of their medium: a telegram reaches any cable office in minutes but carries only words; a photograph goes by post in days; rumour walks.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Act in a city (steal, broker, bribe). Watch a notoriety map: what each place knows about each identity, and when it learned it. Choose a route that beats the slow channels and lands where the fast channel does not yet carry your face: a night train outruns tomorrow's newspaper but not tonight's wire. Lie low to let facts decay; delay the wire by bribing a telegraph clerk or cutting a line; switch identity where only the old name has arrived.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and the hunt primary. Deception: identities are the subjects of facts. Coding: ciphered police cables take longer to act on. Economy: bribes, cable costs, speed costs money. Politics: wartime censorship and 1914 passport controls add checkpoints that read the arriving facts.
- **Historical grounding:** Telegraph against post latency; wanted notices in multiple daily editions; photographs circulated by post; fingerprint cards at Scotland Yard from 1901; the Okhrana's Paris bureau circulating descriptions; the 1910 arrest of a fugitive aboard a transatlantic liner by ship's wireless, when the wire first beat the ship.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** A legible risk model rewards planning, and near misses ("the description arrived an hour after I left") are among the strongest engagement hooks known. Loss aversion over identities; mastery of two overlaid networks; the rush of being just ahead.
- **Engine implications:** Two graphs: a time-expanded transport graph and an information network of channels with latency distributions and capacities. Facts are entities (subject identity, content type, confidence, timestamp); propagation schedules arrival events at institution nodes, and NPCs query their institution rather than holding knowledge. Decay is lazily evaluated. Hunters search only over facts that have arrived at their node. Facts are sparse; propagation is bounded by hop count and relevance.
- **Risks / open questions:** Must not collapse into a pure flight game; latencies need tuning so the race is winnable but tense; explaining the information network without a lecture.

## P02.03 — The Default Timeline
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The thirty years run as history did unless the player pushes on a precondition. Macro-events are nodes of a dependency graph with default dates, preconditions and elasticity bounds; each playthrough jitters them by days to weeks, and player and faction actions can push them further within bounds. The player's life is literally a diff against history, revealed at the end.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Newspapers leak the default timeline into the present. Commissions touch preconditions: an arms delivery before a mobilisation, a stolen memorandum that changes a ministry's belief, a concession that shifts a crisis. The player decides whether to ride history (profit from approximate foreknowledge) or lean on it, knowing every push draws counter-intelligence attention. At death, capture or retirement an inquest shows which events moved, by how much, traced through the graph to the player's acts.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics/warfare and time pressure primary (crises have deadlines that move). Travel: wars close routes and rewrite the graph. Economy: foreknowledge trades. Deception: influence operations. Hunt: services escalate against whoever perturbs their plans.
- **Historical grounding:** The charter's macro-event clock; the July 1914 chain of ultimatums; mobilisation timetables; the 1907 Anglo-Russian Convention; railway concessions as diplomatic causes.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Agency against a known script: the fantasy of "could I have changed it" plus dramatic irony as a resource. The inquest gives narrative closure generated entirely by systems. Jitter keeps foreknowledge approximate, so replay stays uncertain.
- **Engine implications:** Macro-events as a DAG (event, preconditions as world-state predicates, default date, elasticity, consequences) authored as data tables; a constraint propagator reschedules dependents when a node moves; every state change carries provenance (the causing event id) so the inquest is computed, not written. Deterministic given seed; jitter drawn from the seed.
- **Risks / open questions:** The Historian may object to any movement; butterfly explosions must be bounded by elasticity; players may feel railroaded if bounds are tight. Risky but potentially brilliant.

## P02.04 — Several Lives at Once
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player maintains several identities, each a full entity with residence, bank accounts, acquaintances, police registration, mail, debts and obligations in a different city; all keep running whether or not the player is wearing them. Switching lives means physically travelling to where that life is and facing what piled up.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Spin plates. An unattended legend goes stale: rent unpaid leads to eviction and a police inquiry; mail unanswered drops a contact; a lapsed registration draws a fine and attention. Each identity has its own notoriety and assets; burning one loses them. Plan a circuit by timetable; delegate upkeep to a housekeeper or clerk who reports by cable, at cost and leak risk. The hunters' win is linking two identities.
- **Directive coverage:** Life-sim primary. Travel: the circuit. Deception: legends as maintained entities. Items: separate papers and letters of credit per name. Economy: accounts in several currencies. Politics: a German-national identity in London is interned in 1914; visas after 1914 bind to nationality. Hunt: hunters track identities, not the player.
- **Historical grounding:** The touchstone career's households in several capitals; hotel registration; residence permits; letters of credit issued to a name; enemy-alien internment from 1914; nationality-bound visa regimes thereafter.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Plate-spinning anxiety and relief; sunk-cost attachment to each life; "I must be in Petersburg before rent day" appointment compulsion; combinatorial strategy; the dread and thrill of a link being made.
- **Engine implications:** Identity as an entity with components (residence, accounts, registrations, relationships, obligation schedule). Unattended identities run at low level of detail: obligation events fire with aggregated outcomes; on arrival the engine materialises details deterministically (what mail came, who called). Hunter deduction operates over identity entities with a link operation triggered by shared evidence (handwriting, photograph, luggage). Obligation templates are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Cognitive load; cap at three to five identities; maintenance must generate opportunities, not chores.

## P02.05 — The Dossier Is Real
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Each hunting service keeps a file on each of the player's identities, and the file is literally a filtered view of the game's own event log: only events whose evidence reached that service. Steal the file and you read exactly what the hunters know, including the gaps.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Actions emit evidence records with a channel (witness, register, ticket stub, cable copy, fingerprint). Evidence reaches services through the information network, and a service's deduction runs only over its dossier. The player can obtain the file by burglary, a bribed clerk or a double agent, then act: remove a page (deleting evidence sets deduction back), insert a false sighting, or protect the gap they now know exists. Services circulate files to allies on a political schedule, so a gap in one may be filled by another.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt primary. Deception: forging your own file. Items: the dossier is an artifact with provenance. Travel: the gaps are where you can move freely. Economy: files are bought and sold. Politics: 1907 and 1914 open liaison channels that merge files; 1917 spills a service's archive.
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana card indexes and the Paris bureau's files; Special Branch registries; fingerprint cards; wanted-notice circulation; the 1909 Secret Service Bureau's registry; wartime Allied liaison.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Information-gap curiosity (what do they know?) resolved into both relief and dread; agency over your own history; a legible deduction system builds trust that the hunt is fair, which makes being hunted exhilarating rather than arbitrary.
- **Engine implications:** Append-only event log with per-event evidence emission rules (data-driven). A dossier is an indexed filter over the log, essentially free. Deduction is a rule engine over the dossier producing hypotheses with confidence. Edits to dossiers are themselves events, so tampering is replayable and discoverable. The same log drives save, replay and the hunt. Old evidence compacts to summaries.
- **Risks / open questions:** Readability of a raw file; exposing too much state; deduction must feel human, not perfect.

## P02.06 — The Operations Board
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player plans an operation as a dependency graph of tasks with time windows and conditional branches, then hands it to the same scheduler that runs the world and watches it execute, intervening when it breaks.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Lay tasks on a board: obtain an Ottoman visa by Thursday, take Friday's through-train from Vienna to Constantinople, meet the contact within two days, copy the plans (requires a camera, one night), leave by the Smyrna steamer on Tuesday; else fall back to Salonika. Tasks declare prerequisites (items, money, identity), duration and risk; sub-agents can take tasks. The engine validates against the timetable and shows slack. During execution the world intrudes (strike, cordon, late train); the board shows the cascade and the player repairs it.
- **Directive coverage:** All directives are exercised: travel (timetabled legs), deception (cover per task), coding (scheduled cable check-ins), items (prerequisites), economy (cost and cash flow per task), politics (operations are commissions), time pressure (slack is the key resource), hunt (each task emits evidence).
- **Historical grounding:** Period operations were logistics: visas, letters of introduction, cover firms, cable check-ins, through-train connections, consular registration.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The planning pleasure of crafting, anticipation while the plan unfolds, and flow when repairing under pressure. The board makes deep systems legible, so mastery grows from single legs to multi-agent operations.
- **Engine implications:** Plans are declarative DAGs with time windows; critical path and slack are computed against the time-expanded transport graph; the plan compiles into scheduled events with dependency edges; failures trigger re-planning of affected subtrees. NPC factions and hunters use the same plan format, so one planner serves all agents. Designers author task templates as data.
- **Risks / open questions:** UI complexity; drifting into project management; keeping moment-to-moment agency while the plan is running.

## P02.07 — Every Object Has a History
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every item is an entity with a provenance chain, and its safety is a live property computed from what the world has since learned. A forged passport is good until its forger is arrested; days later every document from his hand is a known forgery at every frontier post the news has reached.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The inventory shows provenance and estimated heat. Is this revolver traceable through a dealer's ledger? Are these blueprints from a batch now reported missing? Will this letter of credit be honoured after the bank hears of the fraud? Launder items (re-register, re-stamp), sell them on (heat transfers to the buyer), or hide them in luggage compartments. Stolen plans are valuable only while the theft is unknown and the buyer lacks them.
- **Directive coverage:** Items primary. Deception: forged papers. Travel: customs inspections check heat. Economy: provenance sets price; stolen-goods markets. Politics: a document's value depends on which service lacks it. Hunt: items are evidence, and a recovered item feeds the dossier. Time pressure: heat changes over time.
- **Historical grounding:** Serial-numbered firearms and dealer ledgers; consular passport registers; banks circulating notices of stolen bonds; Lloyd's registers; customs stops at every frontier.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Loss aversion and the endowment effect: a trusted passport turning hot is felt as a wound. Risk assessment becomes a skill; a ticking inventory creates urgency; laundering plays are satisfying; items accumulate stories.
- **Engine implications:** Items are entities with a provenance log linking to other entities and events. Heat is lazily evaluated on inspection by walking provenance links against the fact store of the inspecting institution; no per-tick updates. Burn propagates as fact events through the information network. Items far from the player unload to cold storage. Item types declare which provenance facts they emit.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players ignoring provenance until punished; chain depth must be capped and summarised.

## P02.08 — Hunters Ride the Same Trains
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Pursuers are agents on the same timetable as the player. They cannot teleport; they wire ahead and board the next departure. Cordons form only where their agents can physically be in time, so the hunt is a race across a Bradshaw's graph.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each movement leaves a mode trace (ticket, hotel register, frontier stamp). Hunters receive traces with latency and compute where the player could be. They dispatch watchers to chokepoints they can reach first (frontier stations, ports, the gauge-break at the Russian border) and wire local police. Reveals (a registration, a sighting) collapse their set. The player exploits night trains that outrun the morning dispatch, chokepoints beyond the hunters' reach in time, and mode changes (steamer against rail) that split the hunters' set.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary. Deception: false registrations poison the trace. Items: tickets and stamps are traces. Economy: first class leaves a sharper trace than third. Politics: war changes who hunts where and adds checkpoints. Time pressure throughout.
- **Historical grounding:** Dense continental timetables; frontier stations and gauge breaks; cabling police ahead; passport-free Western Europe before 1914 against Russian and Ottoman controls; ship wireless reaching liners mid-ocean by 1910.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Scotland Yard's deduction tension made geographic and legible: "can they reach the frontier before me?" is a question the player can compute too, so outwitting feels earned. Near misses on platforms; the pleasure of a well-chosen chokepoint.
- **Engine implications:** One time-expanded transport graph per timetable period serves player routing, hunter routing and reachability. Hunter belief is a set of possible positions propagated as bitsets per time slice; cordon planning is a cut over the reachable frontier within the hunters' own reach; reachability tables are precomputed per month. Dozens of hunter agents cost microseconds. Trace emission is data per transport type.
- **Risks / open questions:** Exact reachability makes hunters too strong, so belief needs noise; overlap with pure travel and hunt design will need merging.

## P02.09 — Trading Ahead of the Wire
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Prices on each bourse change only when news arrives there, and news travels at cable speed. Espionage puts the player hours or days ahead of the markets; profit is informational arbitrage, and it leaves a pattern hunters can read.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Hold bonds, arms-firm shares, commodity contracts and currencies across London, Paris, Berlin, Petersburg and New York. World and local events emit news that propagates; each bourse reprices on arrival. Knowing a contract award, a mobilisation order or a drilling result before the wire opens a trading window. Every trade is a timestamped record; a service that correlates "who traded before the news" acquires evidence; sudden fortunes must be laundered through front businesses.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy primary. Coding: ciphered commercial cables to brokers. Travel: being at a bourse, or instructing a broker by cable. Politics: crises are the news. Deception: fronts and nominees. Hunt: trading patterns are evidence. Time pressure: windows measured in hours.
- **Historical grounding:** Gold-standard parities; cable-linked bourses; commercial code books; arms firms' shares; Baku and Persian oil; the exchange closures of July 1914; the rouble's collapse; the mark in 1923.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The thrill of an edge; variable rewards; greed against exposure; mastery of reading news flow; the fantasy of funding espionage with espionage.
- **Engine implications:** Prices are entities repriced by news-arrival events from the same information network as P02.02; each bourse holds a set of known facts, so price is a pure data-driven function of known facts and no per-tick market simulation is needed. Trade records are evidence; a pattern rule runs lazily when a service investigates. Scales to dozens of bourses and hundreds of instruments.
- **Risks / open questions:** Exploitability and balance; profits must tie to life costs and commissions, or it becomes a stock minigame.

## P02.10 — Serving Several Masters
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every intelligence service holds its own belief model of the player. The player can be paid by several at once; exposure comes not from script but from liaison events, when two services exchange information and their beliefs are compared.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Take commissions from several services; feed each a version of the truth through reports, forged documents and observed behaviour. A beliefs view estimates each service's model, imperfectly. Watch the political calendar: the 1907 convention opens a channel between two employers; 1914 fuses Entente counter-intelligence; 1917 shatters the Russian service and leaks its files to whoever seizes them. When a liaison fires, contradictions become suspicion events: interrogations, cut-offs, or a hunt.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics and deception primary. Hunt: suspicion escalates into pursuit. Economy: several salaries. Travel: handlers in different capitals. Coding: a separate cipher per service, and a captured cipher links identities. Items: reports as artifacts. Time pressure: liaisons are dated.
- **Historical grounding:** Freelance agents serving several services; Entente intelligence liaison from 1907 and especially 1914–18; Okhrana files seized, partly burned and partly published in 1917; wartime Allied counter-espionage bureaus.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Greed against dread with a visible political calendar of reckonings; the craft of consistent lying; betrayal emerges from systems rather than authorship, so every exposure is the player's own.
- **Engine implications:** A belief store per faction: facts as (subject, predicate, value, confidence, source, time). Liaison events, scheduled by the macro timeline or emergent, run a reconciliation rule over shared subjects; contradiction yields a suspicion event scaled by the product of confidences. Comparison happens only at liaison, so cost is bounded. Beliefs cover only entities of interest. Update rules are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** An honest beliefs view without full disclosure; liaison frequency; complexity for new players.

## P02.11 — The Archive Remembers
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Nothing sent or signed is ever gone. Telegraph companies keep cable copies, hotels keep registers, banks keep ledgers, censors file letters. These archives persist, and later events (a war, a new codebreaking office, a revolution seizing files) schedule retrospective reviews that can discover the player's acts years later. The past catches up by mechanism.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Early careers are careless: commercial-code cables, real names in registers. The archive accumulates silently. Later, world events trigger reviews: 1914 censorship offices read back cable copies; a service breaks an old cipher class and decrypts stored traffic; 1917 revolutionaries publish secret police files. The player can pre-empt: destroy copies (burglary, bribery, fire), buy the archive, or choose media that leave no copy (courier against cable), trading speed for permanence.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and time pressure primary, on a fuse measured in years. Coding: cipher strength sets how long the archive stays dark. Items: archives hold documents. Economy: ledgers. Politics: regime change opens archives. Travel: going back to burn a register.
- **Historical grounding:** Retained cable copies; wartime cable censorship at relay stations; the Okhrana archives seized and partly published in 1917; the Zimmermann telegram as the archetypal read cable; hotel registers and bank ledgers as records of presence.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Dread of the long fuse rewards foresight; the touchstone arc of the past catching up emerges from systems; clean-up operations arise from the player's own history; the catharsis of burning the last copy.
- **Engine implications:** Archive nodes store event ids, not copies, so cost is negligible. Review events are scheduled by macro-events with rule-based selection (which archives, which period, which cipher classes). Decryption is a probability over cipher class and elapsed time. Discovered facts enter the information network as new evidence. Everything is deterministic and indexed by subject identity. Archive policies are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players may feel punished for early play, so foreshadowing and mitigation are essential. Risky but potentially brilliant.

## P02.12 — The World Posts Its Own Jobs
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** No authored missions. Factions (services, arms firms, ministries, committees) derive goals from the world state and plan to achieve them; when a plan needs a human courier, thief, broker or forger, the engine emits a commission, and the player is one candidate among NPC agents.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each identity sees a market of commissions filtered by reputation, location and what factions believe about it. Accept, execute (the Operations Board), get paid; reputation and beliefs shift, changing future offers. Factions compete for the same object (a concession, a set of plans), so taking one job closes others and creates enemies. Being slow means an NPC agent takes the job and the world moves on without you.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics (faction goals) and economy (commissions, concessions) primary. Travel: jobs are located. Deception: jobs need cover. Items: jobs are about objects. Time pressure: offers expire; NPC rivals compete. Hunt: counter-intelligence factions post jobs whose target is the player.
- **Historical grounding:** Arms-commission agents; concession brokering in Persia, Manchuria and Ottoman lands; services hiring freelancers; the Boer War arms trade; Persian oil from 1908.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Coherent infinite content; the sense of a living economy of intrigue; rivalry with NPC agents; agency in choosing clients; long arcs because factions remember.
- **Engine implications:** Goal-oriented planners per faction over a shared world model decompose goals into tasks; tasks needing an agent become commissions in a bid market; NPC agents are coarse entities executing tasks as scheduled events with probabilistic outcomes. Factions re-plan only when subscribed state changes, keeping planning rare. Goals and task templates are data.
- **Risks / open questions:** Generated jobs feeling samey; needs a wide template library; emergent degenerate loops.
