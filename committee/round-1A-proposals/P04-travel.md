# Round 1A Proposals — Travel & Logistics Designer (Panelist P04)

## Panelist statement
Travel should be the primary verb of this game: it is the one action that necessarily spends time and money, moves the player through jurisdictions, and leaves evidence for the hunters, so every other system can hang from it. The game must get three things right: the network is a real timetable, not a map with distances; routes are knowledge the player acquires, loses and sells, not a menu; and the pursuer is bound by the same timetable and the same telegraph as the pursued. If those hold, "will I make the connection?" becomes the heartbeat of a thirty-year spy life.

## P04.01 — The Known World: Your Timetable Book Is Your Character Sheet
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player never sees the true transport graph, only the subgraph they currently *know*, each edge stamped with the date and source of the knowledge. Progression is the growth, maintenance and decay of this known world across a thirty-year career in which the real network keeps changing.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Plan journeys on the known graph; buy, borrow or steal current guides (monthly editions), read station posters, question porters, concierges, commercial travellers and agency clerks to add edges; discover that stale edges fail at run time ("ghost connections": a service withdrawn, a ferry ice-bound, a line cut by a front). Session loop: learn, plan, travel, update. Career loop: the known world shifts east, west or overseas as commissions and the hunt push you; knowledge of corridors becomes a saleable asset to clients and services.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel primary. Deception/conversation: route knowledge is extracted socially and can be faked (planting false connections for a pursuer). Items: guides, maps and tickets are dated artifacts with provenance. Economy: knowledge has a price; a known smuggling corridor is a business. Politics/warfare: wars and treaties rewrite the real graph, rotting your knowledge. Time pressure & hunt: hunters hold their own known graph; routes they do not know are your escape.
- **Historical grounding:** Bradshaw's Continental Guide (monthly), national guides (Reichskursbuch, Indicateur Chaix); new lines (Simplon 1906, Tauern 1909, Lötschberg 1913, Circum-Baikal 1904–05, Amur 1916); Orient Express suspended 1914, replaced by the Balkanzug 1916–18 and the Simplon-Orient route from 1919.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Information-gap curiosity plus collection/completion drive; competence that is literal (you can go where others cannot); loss aversion as hard-won knowledge rots; emergent stories from the gap between the believed and the true graph.
- **Engine implications:** Date-versioned master graph (edges with validity windows); per-player overlay of known edges with {learned-date, source, confidence}; the planner routes on the overlay, the simulator executes on the truth and emits discrepancy events. Truth is shared data; overlays are sparse bitsets, so it scales.
- **Risks / open questions:** Frustration when a ghost connection strands the player; needs legible confidence cues and recovery options.

## P04.02 — The Connection: A Timetable Clock Where Waiting Is Exposure
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The world clock is the timetable. Every journey is a chain of scheduled departures, and the atomic unit of tension is the connection: the gap between arrival and next departure, during which the player is stationary, visible and spending.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player reads departure boards and builds chains; each leg has departure time, duration, class availability and reliability. Tight connections risk being missed (delay drawn from line and season); loose connections create dead hours at junctions where hunters' telegrams catch up and money drains. Missing the last connection of the day forces an overnight stop, which writes a compulsory hotel registration. Sunday and seasonal timetables matter; night trains buy both distance and anonymity.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and time pressure primary. Hunt: dwell time at a node is what converts a vague trace into a sighting. Deception: dead hours are also when meetings, cable-reading and ticket swaps happen. Items: through tickets with break-of-journey validity are stored slack. Economy: every waiting hour costs fares, hotels, cabs. Politics: wartime and strike timetables shrink the options.
- **Historical grounding:** Bradshaw's connection tables; Sunday and seasonal services; last-train and night-boat patterns (Harwich–Hook of Holland, Dover–Calais); compulsory hotel registration in Germany, Austria, France and Russia; railway telegraph offices at stations.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Tension/relief cycles at a cadence of minutes; near-miss effect when a connection is just made; Zeigarnik pull of an unfinished chain ("one more leg"); a mastery curve from naive planning to deliberate buffer management.
- **Engine implications:** Time-dependent routing over service patterns (calendar bitmaps per service, as in GTFS), connection-scan style queries; delay as seeded distributions so replays are deterministic; node dwell emits events consumed by hunter and economy subsystems.
- **Risks / open questions:** Over-punishing randomness; delay probability must be legible before commitment.

## P04.03 — Luggage, Weight and the Customs Gamble
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Inventory is luggage: discrete pieces with weight, volume and visibility, split between hand luggage (with you, searched at frontiers) and registered luggage (in the van, following the ticket, sealed through or examined). What you carry shapes where you can go and how fast.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Pack before each journey: compartments, false bottoms, concealed documents, trade goods, gold. Each frontier and port applies an examination probability modified by class, nationality on papers, war status and the official's incentives (bribes). Registered luggage can be sent ahead or by slow freight to arrive later and cheaper, decoupling you from incriminating items; left-luggage offices serve as caches and dead drops with receipts as tradable keys; hotel and steamship labels accumulate on trunks and are read by observers.
- **Directive coverage:** Items/artifacts and travel primary. Deception: luggage must match the cover (a commercial traveller carries samples). Economy: 80 Days-style trade bounded by weight and excess charges. Politics/warfare: wartime contraband lists change what is seizable. Time pressure & hunt: a seized trunk is evidence with your labels on it; a trunk sent by another route splits the trail.
- **Historical grounding:** Weight-based excess charges with free allowances varying by country and class; frontier customs halls; sealed through luggage; grande vitesse versus petite vitesse consignment in France; consigne/left-luggage offices; concealment compartments in travelling trunks.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Risk-reward decisions with visible odds; endowment effect and loss aversion around carried artifacts; packing as a puzzle; the customs gamble is a repeated micro-climax with real stakes.
- **Engine implications:** Item containers as graph entities that travel independently on edges (luggage has its own itinerary and arrival events); examination as a rule table keyed on {edge, date, class, papers}; cheap to scale.
- **Risks / open questions:** Inventory fiddliness; needs sane defaults and a "the porter handles it" option.

## P04.04 — The Frontier Station: Regimes as Time-Dependent Edge Attributes
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every border-crossing edge carries a regime record valid for a date range: passport requirement, visa, exit permit, customs intensity, currency control, gauge break, quarantine. Crossings are dense decision nodes, and the regime layer flips with history (August 1914 is the great flip).
- **How it plays (core loop):** Approaching a frontier the player chooses crossing point, time of day, class and identity. Gauge breaks (Russian broad gauge at Wirballen/Eydtkuhnen or Alexandrowo; Spanish gauge at Irun or Port Bou) force everyone off the train into a hall with passport desks, customs, money changers and a telegraph office: fifteen to ninety minutes in which papers are checked, trunks opened and names recorded. Before 1914 most Western crossings are near-formalities while Russia and the Ottoman Empire demand passports and visas; after 1914 every edge demands papers and exit permits; 1920 standardises the booklet.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel, deception and politics primary. Deception: the identity presented must hold a valid document set for this regime; forgeries have a per-regime detection chance. Items: visas and stamps accumulate on a passport as a dated trail. Economy: compulsory exchange and currency controls. Warfare: fronts delete edges; neutral corridors (Switzerland, Netherlands, Scandinavia) become bottlenecks. Hunt: frontier records are the richest trace the hunters receive.
- **Historical grounding:** Passport-free Western Europe before 1914; Russian and Ottoman passport regimes; wartime exit permits; 1920 League passport conference; gauge breaks; frontier customs halls.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Anticipatory dread then relief; mastery of a rule set that changes under you; a clean crossing with contraband is a repeatable peak experience.
- **Engine implications:** Edge attribute layers with validity windows, evaluated by a rules table at crossing time; identity documents as entities checked against the regime; macro-events patch the layer.
- **Risks / open questions:** Data volume for every crossing; abstract minor crossings into regional defaults.

## P04.05 — The Trace Ledger: Every Mode Leaves a Different Mark
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Each travel action writes trace records with four properties: what it reveals (mode, class, name, destination), latency before an institution forwards it, fidelity, and persistence. The hunters never see the player; they consume the ledger through institutional channels at institutional speed.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player picks moves knowing their trace profile. A named sleeping-car berth is high-fidelity and persistent; a third-class day ticket reveals mode and direction only; a hotel form is forwarded to police overnight; a steamer passenger list is printed and filed at the port; a cable receipt sits in the telegraph office's copy book for months; a hired motor-cab is remembered at the rank for a day. The player sees an estimate of their own trail and can spend money and time to thin it (buy tickets leg by leg, change class mid-journey, use a porter as cut-out). Reveals occur when a record is matched to the wanted description: Scotland Yard's cadence, but driven by paperwork cycles rather than turn counts.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary. Deception: aliases on tickets and registers are the main lever. Items: receipts and labels are both trace and evidence. Economy: anonymity costs money or comfort. Politics/warfare: wartime registration thickens every record; revolutions destroy archives. Coding: cables are readable by whoever holds the copy.
- **Historical grounding:** Hotel police forms; sleeping-car berths reserved under a name; printed passenger lists; retained telegraph copies; Bertillonage and fingerprint files; press wanted notices.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Managed paranoia: inferring what the unseen hunter knows is a theory-of-mind puzzle; control over one's own visibility is deeply satisfying; near-misses when a reveal lands one node behind you.
- **Engine implications:** Append-only event stream with per-record delivery time; hunter AI computes possible-position sets from delivered records only (cheap reachability on the time-expanded graph); cost scales linearly with actions.
- **Risks / open questions:** Legibility of the player's own estimate without making the hunter omniscient or trivial.

## P04.06 — Two Speeds of Pursuit: The Warrant Travels by Wire, the Hunter by Train (risky)
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Pursuit propagates at two physical speeds. Information moves at telegraph speed but only between offices, in office hours, through jurisdictions that must agree to act; bodies (detectives, agents) move on the same timetable the player uses. The hunt is played in the gap between the two.
- **How it plays (core loop):** When a trace is matched, a wanted notice is cabled along the wire network to posts that have an office and a liaison agreement; each hop has delay and loss. Simultaneously hunter agents plan journeys with the same planner as the player, suffering the same connections, strikes and frontiers, and needing permission to operate abroad. The player reads this: stay ahead of the wire by crossing into a jurisdiction without liaison, travel where offices are sparse (Ottoman interior, Russian provinces, at sea before shipboard wireless), move when offices are closed, exploit routes the hunters do not know (P04.01), or delay a clerk with money.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary. Coding: wanted notices are cables; intercepting or forging them is possible. Deception: liaison requests can be poisoned with false descriptions. Economy: bribing telegraph clerks and station police is a budget line. Politics: liaison follows alliances (the Franco-Russian alliance underwrites the Okhrana's Paris bureau; 1917 severs it). Items: a forged release telegram is an artifact.
- **Historical grounding:** Telegraph network with retained copies; Okhrana foreign bureau, Special Branch and Sûreté cooperation and its limits; shipboard wireless spreading from the mid-1900s; extradition and consular liaison as the slow human layer.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Outwitting a fair opponent bound by visible physics; racing your own description produces high-arousal planning; the asymmetry is legible, so victories feel earned rather than lucky.
- **Engine implications:** Two propagation models on two graphs (wire graph with office hours; transport graph with timetables); hunters as agents sharing the planner; possible-position reasoning updated per delivered notice.
- **Risks / open questions:** Hunter AI legibility and cost; needs strong debugging views and caps on hunter count.

## P04.07 — Fares, Classes and the Price of Anonymity
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Class is a four-way trade between speed, comfort, society and visibility. Expresses and trains de luxe carry only first and second class at a supplement and record names; third class is slow, exhausting and anonymous. Money is not one number but instruments liquid only at certain nodes and hours.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Fare tables price every leg by class and distance, plus supplements for express, berth and reservation. First class yields conversation access to officials, officers and businessmen (deals, rumours, routes) but writes your name into lists; third class costs fatigue (health) and time but leaves almost no record. Cash is spatial: letters of credit and circular notes cash only at correspondent banks in business hours; gold is universal but heavy and seizable; frontier money changers are dear; after 1914 exchange controls and depreciating paper make currency a route-planning constraint. Through tickets with coupons and circular-tour tickets permit breaks of journey (stored flexibility); buying leg by leg conceals the destination.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy and travel primary. Deception: class must match cover. Hunt: anonymity is purchased with discomfort. Items: tickets, coupons, notes. Politics/warfare: wartime fares and controls. Time pressure: bank hours and Sundays delay cash.
- **Historical grounding:** Gold-standard parities until 1914; Cook's circular notes; Wagons-Lits supplements; German express surcharge; many Continental expresses first/second class only; circular-tour tickets; rouble collapse 1917; mark hyperinflation 1923.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Constant small optimisation with visible trade-offs; scarcity converts routine choices into meaningful ones; social opportunities in first class make spending feel like investing, which fights hoarding.
- **Engine implications:** Fare function per edge (distance band × class × supplements × date); money as typed instruments with node-and-hour liquidity; conversation opportunity tables keyed to class.
- **Risks / open questions:** Fatigue model must stay simple; tune so third class is tempting, not merely punishing.

## P04.08 — Disruption Weather: Strikes, Ice, Requisition and the Forecast Market
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The network has weather: a disruption layer that is partly deterministic (seasonal closures), partly scheduled by the world clock (war, revolution), partly stochastic (strikes, accidents, floods), and all of it forecastable at a price through newspapers, agency cables and insiders.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Before committing to a route the player checks forecasts: strike rumours in the press, a cable from a shipping agent, frost reports. Deterministic closures shape the calendar: St Petersburg's sea approach ices for months while Libau stays open; Lake Baikal is crossed by icebreaker ferry, by rails laid on the ice in winter 1904, and by the Circum-Baikal line from 1905. Scheduled disruptions follow history: the October 1905 Russian strike halts the railways; the 1910 French and 1911 British railway strikes; 1914 requisition cuts civilian services and restricts the Channel. Stochastic events draw from seeded distributions tuned by region and year. Buffer time is the player's insurance; information reduces the buffer needed.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure and travel primary. Economy: information has a price, and disruption moves commodity prices, enabling trade. Politics/warfare: the macro-clock is the main disruption source. Deception/conversation: insiders and clerks leak forecasts. Items: newspapers and cables as dated information objects. Hunt: disruptions also strand hunters and create crowds to hide in.
- **Historical grounding:** As above; also wartime permit zones and epidemic port quarantines.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Planning under uncertainty with purchasable information engages anticipation and the pleasure of a correct forecast; variable-ratio disruption keeps routine travel from going flat; emergent stories ("the strike that stranded me in the wrong jurisdiction").
- **Engine implications:** Disruption layer as scheduled plus seeded-random edge modifiers; forecast objects are events with lead time and accuracy; one shared layer affects all agents equally.
- **Risks / open questions:** Must not feel arbitrary; always offer a forecast channel.

## P04.09 — Cover Is a Route: Itinerary Plausibility
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every cover identity implies a pattern of movement, and every journey is a statement about who you are. Hunters and officials compare your observed itinerary against the pattern the papers claim; implausibility, not bad papers, is what gets you detained.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each identity carries an itinerary template: a commercial traveller for a textile house visits manufacturing towns on weekdays in second class; a tourist follows guidebook circuits in season and sleeps in known hotels; an engineer for a concession company travels to railheads and ports. Actual legs are scored for plausibility against the active identity (season, class, frontier frequency, direction). Low plausibility raises examination probabilities at frontiers and hotels and accelerates matching in the trace ledger. The player repairs plausibility by inserting business-like legs (costly in time, but they can be made genuinely profitable), by switching identity at a node where no record links the two, or by carrying supporting artifacts (samples, order books, letters of introduction).
- **Directive coverage:** Deception and travel primary. Economy: the cover's business should actually trade, funding travel. Items: the supporting artifacts. Politics/warfare: war redefines plausible movement (a tourist near a front is a spy). Time pressure: repairs cost days. Hunt: plausibility is a modifier on every trace record.
- **Historical grounding:** Commercial travellers as a ubiquitous, unremarked class of passenger; letters of introduction and visiting cards; hotel registration; wartime suspicion of foreigners; the real tradecraft of legends matched to movement.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Role-consistency as a puzzle; self-expression through chosen covers; the satisfaction of a journey that is simultaneously profitable, plausible and operational.
- **Engine implications:** Plausibility scoring as pattern matching over the itinerary log; identity templates as data; the score feeds the examination and matching rules.
- **Risks / open questions:** Scoring must be explainable, or detentions feel unjust.

## P04.10 — The Long Leg: Compartment Time as a Bounded Session
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Long legs (Atlantic crossing five to six days, London–Bombay via Suez two to three weeks, Trans-Siberian ten to fourteen days) are sealed sessions: no exit, a fixed company, limited information in, and a world that keeps moving outside until arrival dumps the accumulated news.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Boarding commits the player. Inside, time is spent on the vessel's own action economy: working ciphers on intercepted cables, forging papers with the tools packed (P04.03), conversation with the passenger body (abstracted as a social graph of institutions and types), trading at ports of call, writing letters to post at the next port, resting to recover health. News arrives only via port stops or shipboard wireless bulletins once they exist, so decisions are made on stale information. Hunters cannot board mid-sea but can wire ahead, so the arrival port is a known choke point to prepare for.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and time pressure primary. Coding/deception: the leg is the natural workshop for ciphers and legends. Items: tools and documents carried. Economy: port trade and shipboard gambling. Politics/warfare: wars can begin during a crossing, changing the regime at the destination port. Hunt: the wire-ahead problem.
- **Historical grounding:** Liner schedules; shipboard wireless news bulletins from the mid-1900s; Wagons-Lits and Trans-Siberian de luxe trains; port quarantine; August 1914 stranding passengers mid-voyage.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Commitment devices heighten stakes; bounded sessions create natural stopping points and anticipation of arrival; the information blackout produces dramatic reveals generated by the simulation, not authored.
- **Engine implications:** Leg as a container state with its own action budget; world simulation continues; news delivery gated by node (port) or technology (wireless by date); hunter messages to the destination are scheduled events.
- **Risks / open questions:** Long legs must not become dead time; the action budget needs real content.

## P04.11 — The Dispatcher: Running Couriers, Cables and Cargo on the Same Timetable
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The spy is a network operator. The timetable graph that moves the player also moves couriers, documents, contraband, money and agents the player dispatches, each with its own interception risk per node and dependence on connections. The career is about building and defending routes, not only riding them.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Take commissions (deliver plans from port to capital; move arms samples to a buyer; get an agent out of a hostile city). Plan itineraries for things and people other than yourself: by registered luggage, by ambulant post, by cable in commercial code, by courier in third class. Each edge has an interception probability shaped by regime, censorship and the hunters' current attention. Build assets: cover addresses, poste restante drops, friendly left-luggage clerks, a shipping agency as legitimate business. Revenue comes from logistics clients (arms firms, concession hunters, newspapers); the hunt comes from services tracing the network rather than the person; when a node is compromised, dependencies cascade and everything in flight must be rerouted.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel, economy and hunt primary. Coding: cable routes and code books. Deception: the legitimate business as cover. Items: consignments as tracked entities. Politics/warfare: censorship, blockade and fronts reshape corridors; wartime smuggling margins explode. Time pressure: consignments move while you sleep.
- **Historical grounding:** Commercial code books; TPO/ambulant post; telegraph copy retention and wartime censorship offices; arms commission agents; contraband control and neutral-country routes 1914–18.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Optimisation pleasure and system-building; ownership of a growing machine; cascading failures create emergent crises that are entirely the player's own doing.
- **Engine implications:** Any entity can hold an itinerary on the graph; interception as a rule table; a dependency graph between consignments, nodes and commissions so one compromise propagates; entities are cheap, so it scales.
- **Risks / open questions:** Can become a spreadsheet; keep the player's own body central with legs that must be ridden in person.

## P04.12 — Alibi Arithmetic: Several Lives Held Apart by the Timetable (risky)
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player maintains several identities with households in different cities. Each identity has a presence record (where it was seen, when). The hunters test whether two records could belong to one body using the real timetable: if the fastest connection between two sightings exceeds the gap, they are different people; if not, suspicion links them. The game is scheduling lives so they stay timetable-incompatible.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each household costs rent, servants and visible presence; each identity runs its own business, debts and obligations that demand appearances on dates. The player plans circuits so that identity A's dinner in one city and identity B's bank visit in another are provably impossible for one person, or exploits a connection the hunters do not know (P04.01) to make the impossible possible. Night trains, through sleepers and the new air services shrink the world and threaten the alibis; wars that cut lines strengthen them.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and deception primary. Economy: multiple households and businesses with separate ledgers. Items: document sets per identity. Politics/warfare: an identity can be a national of a belligerent, which closes or opens edges. Time pressure: obligations fall due on dates. Hunt: the correlation engine is the hunter.
- **Historical grounding:** The multi-identity, multi-city household pattern of freelance agents of the era; hotel and residence registration; passport regimes binding name to face after 1914; shrinking journey times as tunnels, through trains and air services open.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Plate-spinning cognitive load with clear rules; dread of correlation; the deep satisfaction of an alibi that holds because you knew a night service existed.
- **Engine implications:** Presence records as events; hunter correlation via fastest-path queries on the time-dependent graph (bounded and cacheable); identity as a first-class entity with its own ledger.
- **Risks / open questions:** Complexity may overwhelm; needs a very clear ledger of identities and slow onboarding (one life first).
