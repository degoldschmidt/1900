# Round 1A Proposals — Deception, Conversation & Cryptography Designer (Panelist P05)

## Panelist statement
My lens is information as the game's true currency: every fact has a cost to obtain, a cost to verify, a shelf-life and a market, and every lie is a debt the world may call in. Deception must never be dialogue flavour; it must be a system of typed claims, belief models, documents and messages that the hunter plays by the same rules as the player. The game must get right the asymmetry of knowledge — what each actor believes, how fast a belief can be checked through period infrastructure, and what it costs to keep a false picture standing.

## P05.01 — The Legend Ledger: Cover Identities as Falsifiable Fact-Graphs
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** A legend is a graph of typed claims (birthplace, employer, regiment, referees, bank), each with a *verification cost* — the real days and money a hunter needs to check it through period infrastructure — and a *corroboration level* raised by planting evidence.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player assembles legends in a ledger, favouring claims cheap to support and slow to check (an overseas registry answers in weeks; a Berlin employer in a day). Every assertion in conversation or on a hotel police form joins the legend's record. When a hunter checks, per-claim clocks run; a forewarned player shores it up (bribe, forged reference, back-dated register) or burns the legend. Concurrent legends in different cities must never be placeable together: the timetable is an alibi constraint.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception primary. Travel: verification latency is routing; alibis are timetable facts. Items: references, cards, registers. Economy: a merchant legend needs a real account. Politics: which registries each service reaches. Hunt: verification clocks; burned legends.
- **Historical grounding:** Passport-free Western Europe before 1914 alongside compulsory hotel registration (fiche d'hôtel, Meldezettel); letters of introduction; registries queried by post; consular enquiries by cable; wartime passport photographs and exit permits.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Spinning-plates cognitive load with legible failure; anticipatory dread from visible clocks; authorship pride in a legend surviving inspection; loss aversion when a long-tended identity burns.
- **Engine implications:** Claim registry of canonical propositions; verification as scheduled events with latency computed from the communications and travel graphs; per-faction registry access; lazy evaluation (clocks spawn only when someone checks).
- **Risks / open questions:** Players must remember their legends — needs an excellent ledger interface; latency tuning so checks bite, not annoy.

## P05.02 — Every Lie Is a Debt: The Claims Ledger with Maturity Dates
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every falsehood the player tells becomes a *liability* with a principal (distance from truth), a holder (who believes it) and a *maturity date* — when the world's clock will naturally test it ("my partner arrives on Thursday's Nord-Express").
- **How it plays (core loop):** A lie maturing untested hardens into accepted fact. A lie maturing tested defaults: suspicion, a demanded explanation, a burned relationship. Before maturity the player can *service* the debt (make it true — put a partner on that train), *roll it over* (a supporting lie, compounding principal and widening who must be deceived) or *buy it back* (partial confession, paid in rapport). Each outstanding lie raises its holder's verification appetite. Sessions acquire a rhythm of due dates.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception primary; time pressure intrinsic (maturities are world-clock events). Travel: lies about movement are serviced by travelling. Economy: lies about money, with money. Politics: lies to factions are debts to factions. Hunt: defaulted lies are leads.
- **Historical grounding:** Period society ran on checkable promises — bills of exchange with due dates, letters of credit, published steamer arrivals, society columns — so stated intentions were verifiable within days.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Converts deception into the gripping psychology of debt: sunk cost, compounding, relief at a quiet maturity, dread at a loud one; emergent stories when lies collide.
- **Engine implications:** Each lie is a proposition with holder set, truth-distance and a test event bound to a world-clock trigger; servicing updates truth value; propagation when holders talk. Scales as a priority queue of maturities.
- **Risks / open questions:** Free-text speech cannot be classified — claims come from typed menus; the ledger must read as tension, not bookkeeping.

## P05.03 — The Exchange: Conversation as a Game of Claims, Calls and Tells
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Conversation is a turn-based information game with poker's structure: parties make *claims*, back them with *evidence* (items, witnesses, hardened facts), *bluff* when they lack it, and either side may *call*. No dialogue trees — the state is a belief model, a want-list and resources.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each party has Composure (depletes under pressure), Rapport (per pair), Leverage (secrets, documents) and an Agenda (what to learn, obtain, conceal). Moves: Assert, Probe, Offer, Press, Concede, Deflect, Call. A Call forces evidence; a matching document wins the point; a bluff resolves against the opponent's suspicion and the player's composure. Every Assert commits a proposition to the opponent's belief model (feeding P05.02). NPCs have *tell profiles* — observable deltas when they bluff — learnable over repeated exchanges.
- **Directive coverage:** Conversation/deception primary. Items are evidence. Economy: Offers trade money, introductions, commissions. Politics: faction agendas drive NPC want-lists. Travel: timetable facts are admissible evidence ("you could not have been in Trieste"). Hunt: interrogation is this game played short of composure.
- **Historical grounding:** A society of calling cards, letters of introduction and club references where standing was continuously asserted and tested; Bertillonage and fingerprint evidence entering police interviews.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Bluff/call yields variable-ratio reinforcement and genuine tension; tell-reading gives a mastery curve; evidence-backed wins reward preparation done elsewhere in the game.
- **Engine implications:** Per-NPC bounded belief sets with confidence and provenance; agenda-driven NPC move selection (utility over information gain and loss); evidence matching as proposition unification. NPCs outside the hot set keep summary beliefs only.
- **Risks / open questions:** Can abstract moves feel like talking? Needs feedback strong enough that players learn opponents, not menus.

## P05.04 — Wires and Post: Every Message Is an Object on the Network
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Telegrams and letters are physical entities on the player's own scheduled network: cables pass through offices that keep copies readable by whoever controls them; letters ride mail trains through jurisdictions that may open, copy and reseal them. Messages are routed, intercepted, delayed, forged — or outrun.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Composing a cable is a word-cost puzzle (commercial codes compress; private ciphers conceal but attract censors); its route chooses its readers. Letters go by cover address, poste restante or courier. A posted denunciation starts a race: beat it to the recipient, intercept it at a sorting office, or overtake it by cable. Hunters use the same wires and post; bribed clerks show them to the player, who can poison them.
- **Directive coverage:** Coding/deception primary; travel co-primary (mail moves at timetable speed; a cable beats any train, so the hunt's reach is the wire's). Items: letters, seals, code books. Economy: per-word costs; bills of exchange are mail. Politics: censorship and cable control shift with war. Hunt: cable-ahead arrests versus unpredictability.
- **Historical grounding:** Retained telegraph copies; the All Red Line and 1914 cable-cutting; travelling post offices; pneumatic post; Okhrana black cabinets; wartime censorship; wireless letting a wanted notice overtake a liner.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The controlled leak as dramatic irony; Zeigarnik pull of a message in transit; legible races on a visible network; beating the post as "one more leg".
- **Engine implications:** Messages as scheduled entities on a comms graph beside the travel graph; office/jurisdiction ownership as faction attributes; tamper state with detection; one scheduler for passengers and mail.
- **Risks / open questions:** Postal speeds near express speeds narrow races — design around margins; templates against composition tedium.

## P05.05 — The Black Chamber: Hands-on Cryptanalysis, an Abstraction Ladder and Burning the Source
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Intercepts are *real ciphertexts* (Vigenère, Playfair, columnar transposition, codebook, later ADFGVX) the player may crack by hand with in-game tools, delegate to a hired cryptanalyst (time, cost, leak risk) or abstract to a skill roll — three rungs of one ladder yielding the same intelligence object.
- **How it plays (core loop):** A frequency tally, period test and crib tool make hand-solving feasible. Captured code books are items that make a traffic class readable until its owner *rotates* the book — triggered by a hidden compromise-suspicion value that rises when the player acts too visibly on intercepts. Every use of a decrypt is a wager: exploit now and risk the source, or hold.
- **Directive coverage:** Coding primary. Items: code books, cipher slides, developer chemicals. Economy: cryptanalysts cost; code books sell for fortunes. Politics: compromise events alter faction behaviour. Travel: intercepts reveal shipments and movements worth catching a train for. Hunt: reading the hunters' traffic is the ultimate counter-surveillance — and the most dangerous to act on.
- **Historical grounding:** Playfair in the Boer War and WWI; Vigenère and transposition in commercial and consular traffic; naval code books captured in 1914; Room 40 (1914); the 1918 German field cipher; one-time pads from 1917.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Puzzle "aha" and competence; the ladder lets novices progress while experts earn faster, cheaper intelligence; the source-burning dilemma is strategic tension with delayed, legible consequence.
- **Engine implications:** String-level encryption of generated messages; generators producing plausible, solvable traffic; compromise-suspicion as per-faction hidden state driven by the observability of player actions; the abstract rung returns identical outputs.
- **Risks / open questions:** Difficulty calibration; players who skip puzzles must never be strictly worse off.

## P05.06 — The Forgery Bench: Documents as Composed Items with Inspection Depth
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every document is a field-structured item (stock, typeface, seal, signature, serial, stamps, dates) in which each field has a *quality*, and every inspector has an *inspection depth*: a gendarme glances at the seal; a consulate wires the issuing register.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Forgery is crafting from components — blank stock (stolen, bought from printers), seals (carved, cast, stolen), signatures practised from specimens, typewriters with identifiable wear, inks. The player predicts who will inspect and how deeply, spending only the quality needed. Genuine documents can instead be *altered* (photograph substitution, date erasure) with different failure modes. Documents age, collect border stamps and build a history that must match the legend carrying them.
- **Directive coverage:** Items and deception primary. Travel: visas, exit permits and passes gate the network, intensifying after 1914. Economy: letters of credit and bills of exchange are forgeable money. Politics: which documents exist depends on regime and year. Hunt: a failed document is a hard fix on name and position.
- **Historical grounding:** Pre-war passport-free Europe against Russian and Ottoman passport regimes; 1915 photographic passports; 1920 standardised booklets; typewriter typeface identification in forensic practice; the stamp-and-seal culture of continental bureaucracy.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Crafting satisfaction plus calibrated risk ("good enough for a gendarme, not a consul"); the border check as a knowable, dice-free test rewards preparation; attachment to a well-worn passport.
- **Engine implications:** Documents as typed field vectors; inspectors as depth profiles per institution and year; inspection resolves field by field deterministically, with partial feedback to the player; histories as appended stamps.
- **Risks / open questions:** Component sourcing must not become a fetch quest; the bench needs its own tempo.

## P05.07 — Whisper Networks: Rumour and Reputation Propagation on the Social Graph
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Rumours are mutable propositions travelling a social graph with per-edge delays, mutation chances and credibility weights; a rumour corroborated by two independent paths hardens into "known". Player and hunters both inject and both listen.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player seeds rumours at high-reach nodes — cafés, clubs, bourses, salons, barracks, newspaper offices (a paid journalist), personal columns. Each identity carries a *reputation vector* per faction and city, moved by hardened rumours. The player reads the network through hired listeners (porters, concierges, telegraph boys) and by scanning each day's editions. Counter-rumours, denials and plausible alternative explanations are moves. The hunters' network leaks too: a forming cordon is itself a rumour.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception/conversation primary. Politics: faction credibility weights; rumour panics move crises. Economy: reputation gates credit and commissions; planted rumours move prices. Travel: rumours cross cities at post and telegraph speed — slower than an express the player is on. Hunt: a false sighting is a seeded rumour.
- **Historical grounding:** Multi-edition daily press and news agencies; agony columns as semi-public channels; café and club society; concierge and hotel-staff informant culture; bourse panics driven by rumour.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Agency at a distance with delayed payoff — planting a rumour and watching it arrive is a Zeigarnik open loop; attribution pleasure ("I started that"); legible social cause and effect.
- **Engine implications:** Propagation at city/faction aggregate with per-NPC resolution only for hot nodes; rumours as objects with provenance chains; hardening as a two-path corroboration test; triggers on reputation thresholds.
- **Risks / open questions:** Opacity — players must see enough network to feel causal; rumour-spam exploits need damping.

## P05.08 — Spot, Assess, Develop, Recruit — and the Chair Turned Round
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Recruiting an asset and being interrogated are the *same* information game from opposite seats: one side holds a dossier of vulnerabilities and known facts; the other must infer what it contains.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Potential assets have hidden motivation profiles in period terms — debt, grievance, conviction, vanity, fear — and discoverable vulnerabilities (gambling, affairs, compromising letters). The player investigates (observation, bribed servants, intercepted mail) to fill a dossier, then pitches at a size matched to the profile; a mismatched pitch alerts the target. Recruited assets run through cover addresses and dead drops; reliability decays without attention and payment. When detained, the player faces an interrogator whose dossier is built from the hunters' evidence; question patterns hint at its contents, and the player chooses what to admit, deny or trade.
- **Directive coverage:** Conversation/deception primary. Items: compromising letters, photographs. Economy: assets cost retainers; recruited clerks yield commercial intelligence. Politics: assets inside ministries and arsenals are the main lever. Travel: handling assets across cities sets the itinerary. Hunt: interrogation is the climax, resolved by information rather than dice.
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana and Special Branch informant networks; blackmail of indebted officers as a documented recruitment method; dead drops and cover addresses; police detention before recorded statements.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Curiosity gap ("what do they know?"); mastery of reading people as profiles; dread of an asset's decay; the symmetry teaches players to feel their own exposure.
- **Engine implications:** Motivation profiles as vectors; dossiers as belief sets; interrogation questions generated from the hunters' evidence graph; asset reliability as decaying state with scheduled check-ins.
- **Risks / open questions:** Interrogation must be survivable and interesting, not a fail state.

## P05.09 — The Double Ledger
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player works simultaneously for several intelligence services and commercial principals, each holding its own *model of the player*: what it believes the player knows, has done, delivered and owes. The core resource is the *discrepancy* between those models and the truth.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each principal issues tasks, pays and periodically *reconciles*, cross-checking product against other sources. The player delivers real intelligence, chicken-feed or fabrication, trading money and standing against future reconciliation risk. Travel between principals' capitals sets the rhythm; ciphered correspondence sustains distance. Over years, services compare notes and the accumulated discrepancy becomes the hunt: whichever service finds the gap becomes a pursuer holding everything the player ever told it as a dossier. Victory is a retirement with the ledgers never reconciled.
- **Directive coverage:** All systems primary by construction — travel (principals in different capitals), deception/coding (fabricated product; ciphered reports), items (stolen plans as product), economy (commissions, double pay, debts), politics/warfare (demand follows the world clock: war makes naval plans priceless), time pressure/hunt (reconciliation events; defection into pursuit).
- **Historical grounding:** The freelance agent-cum-commission-broker career; competing services before 1914 (Secret Service Bureau, Deuxième Bureau, Evidenzbureau, Abteilung IIIb, Okhrana); wartime hunger for naval and industrial plans.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Sustained dramatic irony — knowing more than every faction; juggling tension with relief at each passed reconciliation; long-horizon consequence: early choices matter decades later.
- **Engine implications:** Per-faction belief models of the player as first-class objects; reconciliation as scheduled events comparing deliveries; product as proposition sets with truth distance; defection converting a model into a hunter dossier.
- **Risks / open questions:** Complexity ceiling beyond three principals; legibility of what each service believes.

## P05.10 — The Bourse of Secrets
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player's business is information arbitrage. Every fact has a market price set by *freshness*, *exclusivity*, *verifiability* and *buyer need*; services, newspapers, bourses, arms firms and embassies are rival buyer classes. Lies are inventory too — and every sold item carries provenance traceable to the seller.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Acquire information (eavesdropping, intercepts, recruited clerks), *verify* it (time and cables raise its price), *package* it (document, tip, rumour) and sell to the best buyer — or hold for a better moment on the world clock: a mobilisation crisis multiplies a railway timetable's value. Selling twice breaks exclusivity; fabrications pay until a buyer verifies. Information decays: plans sold after the launch are worthless. The hunt emerges from provenance — every item leaks who could have known.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy and deception/coding co-primary. Travel: price gaps between cities; what cannot be cabled must be carried. Items: documents as provenanced inventory. Politics/warfare: demand follows crises; selling can move them. Time pressure: decay and event-driven spikes. Hunt: provenance tracing; buyers turning hunters.
- **Historical grounding:** Commission agents and concession brokers trading insider knowledge; news agencies paying for scoops; rumour-driven bourses; services buying naval and industrial plans; pre-1914 competing services.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Market play with information as the good — loss aversion over decaying inventory, the thrill of a held secret spiking, dramatic irony in selling one truth to rivals; emergent stories as provenance closes in.
- **Engine implications:** Information items as propositions with timestamps, exclusivity sets, provenance chains and per-buyer utility functions; prices from world-state demand; verification as scheduled queries; hunter-side provenance tracing.
- **Risks / open questions:** Pricing legibility; preventing fabrication farming — verification must bite often enough.

## P05.11 — Borrowed Lives
- **Type:** whole-game concept (risky)
- **Central mechanical hook:** Instead of inventing legends, the player takes over the identity of a *procedurally generated real person* — recently dead, emigrated or vanished — inheriting their social graph: creditors, employers, acquaintances, relatives, and every NPC's beliefs about them.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The world generates life histories for its population. The player scouts "donors" via death notices, passenger lists and hospital records, obtains their papers, and *performs* a life never lived. Acquaintances hold memories the player must infer from letters, effects and conversation; a Call can come from anyone who knew the donor. The player harvests the donor's assets, position and access for espionage and business, choosing how long to stay: longer tenure earns deeper access while risk compounds as distant contacts arrive.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception primary. Travel: donors are found far from where they are known; acquaintances travel too. Items: the donor's papers. Economy: inherited debts and businesses. Politics: a donor with faction ties grants access. Time pressure/hunt: returning relatives; hunters seeking someone who should be dead.
- **Historical grounding:** Weak identity infrastructure before photographic passports; emigration and wartime casualty churn; death notices and passenger lists in the press; identity established by acquaintance and paper.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Role-inhabitation through inference; constant "do they know?" tension; procedural intimacy — each borrowed life is an emergent mystery the player must solve to survive.
- **Engine implications:** Generated biographies whose facts are distributed consistently across NPC belief models; relationship graphs with expectation events; discovery as inference. Heavy but reusable: the same generator powers every NPC.
- **Risks / open questions:** Believable life histories at scale; sufficient clues; keeping generated histories mechanical (facts and relations), never authored narrative.

## P05.12 — The Notebook Is the Only Record
- **Type:** game-defining system (risky)
- **Central mechanical hook:** The engine records every claim the player has made but *never shows the player that record*. The only memory aid is a notebook the player writes — a physical item that can be lost, searched, stolen, read or used as evidence.
- **How it plays (core loop):** After each exchange the player may jot entries (typed shorthand plus free notes). Carrying the notebook risks catastrophic exposure at a search; leaving it in a safe or dead drop risks forgetting; ciphering it with a real player-chosen cipher costs time to consult; burning pages trades memory for safety. Hunters who seize it gain its contents as hard evidence; a planted *false* notebook becomes a weapon. Memory lapses surface as Calls the player cannot answer.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception/coding primary. Items: the notebook and its key. Travel: luggage searches at frontiers make carrying it a routing decision. Economy: the notebook of debts and commissions is also the business record. Politics: a seized notebook exposes networks and triggers faction consequences. Hunt: the most valuable trace object in the game.
- **Historical grounding:** Seized notebooks and address books as decisive evidence in period espionage prosecutions; luggage searches at Russian and wartime frontiers; book ciphers for personal records.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Memory becomes a genuine resource; the endowment effect binds players to the object; "do I take it across?" dilemmas; player-authored artifacts gain emergent meaning.
- **Engine implications:** Nothing new for the belief layer; the notebook as a free-text item with search and seizure events and an optional real cipher transform; notes must be searchable.
- **Risks / open questions:** Punishes forgetful players — needs mitigation (P05.01's ledger as a safer, weaker alternative); accessibility.
