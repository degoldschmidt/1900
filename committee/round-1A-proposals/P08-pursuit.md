# Round 1A Proposals — Pursuit & Hidden-Information Designer (Panelist P08)

## Panelist statement
I design the hunt. The game's promise is that for forty hours the player is somebody's quarry, and that this never degenerates into either an invisible random death or a nagging meter. That requires three things: hunters who reason only from evidence the player actually left (so every capture is auditable and fair), period institutions that act as forced reveals (registers, frontiers, banks, cables) so exposure is a *choice* the player trades for comfort and speed, and a reveal cadence legible enough that the player can feel the net tighten and slip it. Everything below is built so the hunt and the life sim are one system: the register you sign is the bed you sleep in.

## P08.01 — The Ledger of Traces
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player is never observed directly. Every act writes a record into an institutional ledger (hotel police register, booking clerk's memory, telegraph copy, bank counterfoil, passport stamp, poste-restante signature), and hunters can only ever read ledgers.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each record carries place, time, identity used, fidelity (a signed Meldezettel versus a porter's memory of a face), persistence (a Prussian hotel sheet collected nightly versus an inn book nobody reads) and access (which services may query it, at what delay and cost). The player sees their own trail as a map and may spend time, money and risk cleaning it — bribe a clerk, steal a register page, burn a counterfoil — knowing cleaning writes a record too. Minute-to-minute: the convenient act that writes a high-fidelity record, or the awkward one that writes none. Session-to-session: watch the trail age out.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel (tickets, class, stamps) and economy (drafts, letters of credit) are the chief record-writers; deception chooses which identity signs; items (a labelled trunk) are records on legs; politics sets who may read which ledger; time pressure comes from records propagating on institutional clocks. Primary: hunt, travel, economy.
- **Historical grounding:** Compulsory hotel registration in Germany, Austria, France and Russia against none in Britain until the 1914 Aliens Restriction Act; telegraph companies retaining copies; passport-free Western Europe before 1914; drafts and circular notes as named instruments.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Mundane verbs become tense decisions (loss aversion over a clean trail); a legible map of one's own exposure sustains fear without helplessness; "it was the laundry mark" stories emerge from systems alone.
- **Engine implications:** Append-only event log indexed by place, identity and time; cost-bounded hunter queries; lazy materialisation — only ledgers actually queried expand into detail, the rest remain counts. Continent-scale because the world simulates books, not clerks.
- **Risks / open questions:** Showing a trail without a spreadsheet; tuning persistence so hunts neither evaporate nor become inescapable.

## P08.02 — The Quiet Route: Forced Reveals at Register, Frontier, Bank and Cable
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** Scotland Yard's periodic reveal becomes the period's four compulsory disclosure points. Sleeping in a registered hotel, crossing a passport frontier, encashing a named instrument and sending a cable each reveal identity-plus-location to whoever reads that institution's records, on its clock. Routes are planned on two axes: fastest and quietest.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each day the player buys a comfort/exposure bundle. Wagons-Lits and a grand hotel reveal nightly; a third-class waiting room, gold coin and a letter by post reveal nothing but cost sleep, health and time. An exposure forecast for any planned route (how many forced reveals, to which services, with what delay) sits beside the timetable. Hardship accumulates as life-sim debt — illness, missed appointments, a shabby appearance that itself draws police attention — so silence is never free.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and hunt primary; economy (cash versus instruments); deception (which identity reveals); politics (border regimes flip in 1914, exit permits, 1920 passport standardisation); items (luggage class as signal); time (reveals propagate on institutional schedules).
- **Historical grounding:** Meldepflicht in German-speaking lands; French hotel registers; Russian internal passports and hotel reporting; British absence of controls until 1914; universal passport and visa controls 1914–1920; drafts payable to a named person; telegraph forms retained.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** A clean trade-off loop — pay in suffering for silence — felt every night; variable-ratio tension because a reveal only matters if a hunter is reading that ledger; mastery as the player learns each country's disclosure texture; 80 Days' "one more leg" compulsion with a second axis.
- **Engine implications:** Each institution is a reveal channel with latency and a reader list; the route planner queries channels for an exposure estimate; the world clock mutates channel parameters (1914, 1917, 1920).
- **Risks / open questions:** A degenerate "always sleep rough" strategy; hardship must bite.

## P08.03 — Heat Belongs to the Legend, Not the Player
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Hunters hold a dossier per cover identity, not per player. The hunt advances through *linkage events* — moments when evidence ties two legends together — and the player's craft is keeping legends unlinked.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each legend has its own papers, bank, luggage, tailor, cable address, handwriting and habitual cities. Links form when a witness sees both faces, two registers show one hand, a draft is endorsed from one name to another, the same trunk arrives under two names, or a photograph is matched across files. The player sees legends as separate heat columns and a linkage-risk indicator for any planned action ("arriving as A on the train B's ticket was bought for"). Burning a legend discards its heat but also its credit, contracts, friendships and households. Destroying the specific linking record can sever a link.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception primary; economy (each legend's finances separately funded); travel (never change identity mid-journey without a gap); items (handwriting, typewriter, luggage as linking artifacts); politics (a legend's nationality decides which frontiers open); time (links age; witnesses forget).
- **Historical grounding:** Multiple-passport careers among freelance agents; portrait-parlé description cards that made face-matching a filing operation; handwriting and typewriter typeface comparison; endorsement chains on bills and drafts.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Compartmentalisation is a satisfying puzzle; the endowment effect on a carefully built legend makes linkage genuinely dreadful; failure is granular — lose a name, not the game — so the player keeps playing after disasters.
- **Engine implications:** Evidence records carry identity tags; hunter inference includes a link-detection pass over co-occurring tags; legends are first-class entities owning assets and relationships.
- **Risks / open questions:** Bookkeeping burden; how many legends a player can truly manage.

## P08.04 — The Patchwork Dragnet: Jurisdictions and Liaison
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** There is no single hunter. Each service and private agency pursues with its own belief, reads only its own jurisdiction's ledgers freely, and learns from others through liaison channels whose existence, delay and honesty are set by the political world clock.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player routes through the *seams*: a Sûreté lead dies at the Belgian frontier unless Paris cables Brussels and Brussels agrees; the Okhrana's Paris bureau can shadow in France but must ask Special Branch for London. Channels open and close historically — anti-anarchist police cooperation after 1898 and 1904, Entente sharing from 1914, the Okhrana's extinction in 1917, the international police commission of 1923. The player can poison a channel (feed one service a claim about another), buy a liaison clerk, or pick a destination where the dominant hunter has no friends. Private hunters — creditors' inquiry agents, Pinkertons on contract — ignore borders but have small budgets.
- **Directive coverage:** Politics/warfare primary (alliances literally are the hunt map); travel (the seam is a frontier); economy (private hunters are hired by debts and lawsuits you created); deception (forged liaison traffic); time (channels mutate on dates).
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana foreign Agentura in Paris; Special Branch–Okhrana contacts over anarchists; Rome 1898 and St Petersburg 1904 anti-anarchist protocols; wartime allied bureaux; Vienna 1923 commission.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** A readable geography of safety that shifts with history, producing fresh strategic puzzles every few game-years; theory-of-mind pleasure from setting hunters against each other.
- **Engine implications:** N independent belief states; a liaison graph with latency, fidelity and political gating; inter-hunter messages as scheduled events the player can intercept. Dormant hunters hold only a shelved dossier, so cost scales with active hunts.
- **Risks / open questions:** Legibility of liaison state; avoiding a single safe-country exploit.

## P08.05 — The Honest Hunter: Hypothesis-Set Belief Engine (risky)
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Hunters never read the player's true position. Each maintains a bounded set of weighted hypotheses (identity, place, time, mode, habit) propagated along the real timetable graph and reweighted only by evidence records. Whatever the hunter "knows" is derivable, and the player can learn to shape it.
- **How it plays (core loop):** A quarry recorded in Vienna at 20:00 cannot be in Paris before the earliest arrival, so hypotheses advance along Bradshaw's; a river steamer outside the hunter's profile leaves the player outside the set until a record reveals the habit. Hunters build a profile (class, hotels, banks, cable addresses), query ledgers where weight concentrates, or sit on fixed points — households, offices, banks — that hypotheses must eventually revisit, all within a budget. The player, modelling the model, exploits gaps: travel out of profile, cross modes, hold still where hypotheses are thin. When weight collapses onto one city, a cordon (P08.07) fires.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary; deception (profile-breaking is a disguise of behaviour); economy (hunters spend on cables and watchers); politics (jurisdiction gates which ledgers a hunter may query); time (propagation in schedule time).
- **Historical grounding:** Period detection really was ledger-and-timetable work — police circulars, Bradshaw's consulted to test alibis, description cards, telegraphed requests to stations ahead.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The hunter feels like a mind rather than a meter; outwitting a legible reasoner produces genuine competence satisfaction; every capture is explicable, which lets forty hours of being hunted feel fair rather than cruel.
- **Engine implications:** Particle-filter-like propagation on a hierarchical graph (city → region → country), expanding resolution only where weight concentrates; deterministic seeds for replay and the autopsy (P08.09); hypotheses × hunters × targets must fit a frame budget.
- **Risks / open questions:** Tuning; hunters too dumb or too lucky; conveying the model without exposing it.

## P08.06 — Shadows and Dry-Cleaning
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Physical surveillance is never announced. When a hunter assigns watchers, the world emits ambient anomalies (a repeated face, a cab that waits, a porter with questions), and the player decides whether to spend time on counter-surveillance routes that either expose or lose the shadow.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Anomalies are generated with a false-positive rate that falls with experience and rises with fatigue, drink and unfamiliar cities. Dry-cleaning uses real urban texture: department stores with several exits, a Métro or U-Bahn interchange, boarding a train and alighting before departure. Each costs time against appointments and the timetable. A lost shadow hands the hunter a last-seen fix and widens their hypotheses; an undetected shadow follows you to a meeting or a household, converting a location into a linkage (P08.03).
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt primary; travel (urban transit as counter-surveillance terrain); deception (behaving like your legend while checking your tail); items (a change of hat and coat in a trunk at left-luggage); economy (fares, lost business hours); politics (shadowing intensity varies by regime — Okhrana Russia against pre-war London).
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana watcher teams and Special Branch shadowing; motor taxis from c. 1905–10; metropolitan railways in London, Paris, Berlin, Budapest and Vienna.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Calibrated paranoia — pattern detection with real costs for both false alarms and complacency — produces the "I knew it" peak; the skill is learnable, so veterans feel growth; it makes the hunt visible without a meter.
- **Engine implications:** Watchers as cheap local processes attached to a hunter's lead; anomaly emission as a signal-plus-noise channel; city templates listing dry-cleaning affordances.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players distrusting all signals; ground truth must be recoverable afterwards (P08.09).

## P08.07 — The Cordon Clock
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** When a hunter's belief concentrates on a city, a cordon forms in real time — cables to outlying stations, men to piers and frontier posts — with a formation curve, gaps and a decay. Escape is a race against a fuse the player can partially read.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Formation takes hours to days depending on the service's reach and the city's size; coverage climbs unevenly — main termini first, river piers and branch halts last; night shifts thinner; third-class exits watched less than first. The player reads warning signs (a plainclothesman on the concourse, papers demanded at the hotel, a paragraph in the evening edition) and consults Bradshaw's for the pre-dawn departure from the unwatched suburban station. Or hide inside the ring: cordons cost the hunter money and men and collapse after days without fresh evidence, but hiding needs a safe address and total silence — no cables, no bank.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and hunt primary; economy (a budget contest; hunter funds are finite); deception (passing a checkpoint on a cold legend); politics (wartime cordons are denser — requisitioned stations, military police); items (what fits in one bag when the trunk is abandoned); time (the fuse).
- **Historical grounding:** Telegraphed descriptions to stations along a line, frontier posts and ports; wartime exit controls and port checks; the practical porosity of pre-1914 continental borders.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The burning fuse is the purest tension/relief cycle in games; slipping the net is a near-miss event the brain rewards disproportionately; going to ground is a slow-burn alternative with its own payoff.
- **Engine implications:** Cordon as a scheduled process over a city's exit set with per-exit coverage curves; joined with the timetable to compute real escape windows; decay tied to hunter budget.
- **Risks / open questions:** Over-frequent cordons numb; they must be rare, earned and memorable.

## P08.08 — The Newspaper Mirror
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Wanted notices, reward adverts and "information wanted" columns make every NPC within a paper's circulation a potential witness — and because the player reads the same papers, the press becomes the one window into what hunters believe.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Multiple daily editions deliver the hunt's public state: a portrait-parlé description, a name, increasingly after the early 1900s a half-tone photograph. Publication raises witness recall and spontaneous denunciation inside the circulation area and propagates via agencies to other countries with delay. The player answers through the same medium: agony-column coded messages to accomplices, a planted sighting via a stringer, a paid paragraph announcing a departure that never happens. A photograph in print is catastrophic for a legend and forces a change of appearance that alters the player's social reach (a shaven agent cannot resume the bearded legend's club).
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and deception primary; economy (buying paragraphs; a share in a paper as long-term asset); politics (wartime censorship suppresses or amplifies notices); travel (circulation areas as risk maps); coding (agony-column ciphers); time (editions as a daily reveal rhythm).
- **Historical grounding:** Police Gazette and continental Steckbrief traditions; reward advertising; the spread of half-tone photography in dailies; Reuters, Havas and Wolff syndication; wartime press censorship; the agony column as a real covert channel.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The player voluntarily seeks dread (information-gap curiosity) on a daily cadence; relief when the edition is silent; the inversion of turning the hunter's megaphone against them.
- **Engine implications:** Publications as broadcast events with circulation geometry and recall multipliers; agency propagation as delayed re-broadcast; the witness model subscribes to broadcasts.
- **Risks / open questions:** Notice text must be procedural, never authored; the photo-era transition needs tuning.

## P08.09 — The Trail Autopsy
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Capture is never game over and never a mystery. Every arrest, near-miss or exposed legend produces a replayable reconstruction of the exact evidence chain and inference the hunter used, with the branch points where the player could have broken it.
- **How it plays (core loop):** After an arrest the player sees the hunter's dossier as it was built: the register in one city, the cable copy in another, the teller who remembered a face, the timetable deduction that excluded other routes. Then the life sim continues — interrogation (the deception system), remand, trial, expulsion, exchange, escape, or a sentence that ages the character and advances the world clock. A redacted autopsy is also offered for close calls the player survived, so learning is not gated behind failure.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt primary; deception (interrogation as the next act); economy (bail, bribes, counsel, lost contracts); politics (who you are exchanged to depends on current alignments; a wartime arrest is a different fate from a peacetime one); travel (expulsion routes); time (sentences consume years).
- **Historical grounding:** Expulsion or short sentences as the common fate of foreign agents before 1914; wartime courts-martial; prisoner exchanges; archives that enabled later re-arrest.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Fairness attribution is the strongest predictor of persistence after loss; the autopsy converts loss into mastery ("never endorse a draft across legends again"); the chain reads as a story the player authored by accident; it makes the hunter's intelligence credible.
- **Engine implications:** Deterministic, seed-replayable simulation with provenance on every inference (record → reweight → action); this doubles as the balancing and debugging tool for the whole engine.
- **Risks / open questions:** Reconstructions must read as narrative, not log dumps; capture consequences need variety across a career.

## P08.10 — Feeding the Hunter: The False-Evidence Economy (risky)
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Because hunters reason only from records, the player can manufacture records. Deception and the hunt become one system: buy a through-ticket you never use, have an accomplice sign your name into a register two countries away, post letters with distant postmarks, send a cable from a station you leave by cab.
- **How it plays (core loop):** False evidence must fit the timetable and the hunter's profile of you, because the hunter checks Bradshaw's too; an impossible journey is discarded and raises the hunter's scepticism against that evidence class. Fabrication costs money, accomplices (each a liability with their own trail) and time. The payoff is steering hypotheses into a cordon on the wrong city while you do business in the right one. Hunters that detect a fabrication learn, and a service burned twice begins to read inversions.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception primary; travel (fabrications are routes); economy (the price of a decoy itinerary; accomplices on retainer); items (a monogrammed trunk sent ahead as bait); politics (planting evidence that implicates a rival service); time (decoys sequenced against real movements).
- **Historical grounding:** Documented agent use of decoy bookings, mail drops and cover addresses; telegraph office provenance on every cable; the anonymity of continental third-class travel.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Deceiving a visible reasoner is the richest form of agency; a cordon forming on the decoy is a theory-of-mind payoff; the learning hunter sustains the arms race across a career rather than one exploit; decoys that can backfire deliver gambling-grade anticipation.
- **Engine implications:** Records need an authorship field hidden from hunters but used by the scepticism learner; hunter plausibility checks against the timetable graph; accomplices as agents with their own traces.
- **Risks / open questions:** Cheap decoys would trivialise the hunt; the scepticism learner must punish overuse without punishing cleverness.

## P08.11 — Poacher and Gamekeeper: The Hunter's Board
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** One career contains both roles. Between fugitive stretches the player takes counter-espionage or inquiry commissions and hunts an AI quarry across the same ledgers, timetables and liaison channels used against them — learning the hunter's mind by being it.
- **How it plays (core loop):** As hunter the player receives delayed ledger extracts, allocates a budget of watchers to termini, piers and frontier posts, files cable requests to foreign police that politics may refuse, prunes impossible hypotheses with Bradshaw's, and chooses when to spring a cordon — early and leaky, or late and expensive. The quarry runs on the inverted belief engine (P08.05): it leaves traces, buys decoys, dry-cleans. Commissions pay and build standing with a service — standing that later becomes the liaison channel you most fear as quarry. Hunting writes your own records too: expense claims and cables show where you were.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt primary in both directions; politics (your employer decides which ledgers you may read); economy (commission, expense budget, reward money); travel (the hunter must physically move to question a clerk); deception (quarries and witnesses lie; a double agent may hunt their own network); time (the quarry moves whether or not you act).
- **Historical grounding:** Freelance agents genuinely served several services in sequence; private inquiry agencies took pursuit contracts; police circulars and telegraphed descriptions were the working tools.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Deduction pleasure (hypothesis elimination) alternates with evasion pleasure across sessions; skill transfer is real — a good hunter becomes a far better fugitive, felt as mastery; symmetry makes the AI's later pursuit credible and legible.
- **Engine implications:** The belief engine must be human-usable: hypotheses exposed as a weighted board; the quarry agent reuses the player-facing trace systems. Negligible extra content cost since both roles share one simulation.
- **Risks / open questions:** Hunter sessions must stay embodied (travel, interrogation), not become a spreadsheet.

## P08.12 — The Long Hunt: Dossiers That Never Close (risky)
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** A 1895–1925 career in which no evidence is ever deleted. Fingerprint cards, photographs and cable copies persist in archives, are shelved when a hunt stalls, and reopen when new evidence, new decryption or a new political priority arrives. The endgame is the convergence of shelved dossiers.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Early exposures feel cheap. But a 1902 arrest leaves prints at the Fingerprint Bureau; a 1916 cable copy becomes readable in 1919 when its code breaks; a service you worked for in 1912 hunts you in 1918 after a revolution inherits its files. Dormant dossiers cost nothing and wake when a linkage (P08.03) touches them or the world clock changes who wants you. The player can attack archives — fire, theft, bribery, lawsuit — each a high-stakes operation. Ageing matters: the face of 1905 diverges from the man of 1920, but portrait-parlé fields that never change (height, ear form, scars) stay dangerous.
- **Directive coverage:** All six: travel (where archives are), deception (identity drift over decades), items (the single incriminating artifact), economy (old debts and partners as reopeners), politics/warfare (regime change as the principal reopener), time (decades), the hunt as the career's shape.
- **Historical grounding:** Fingerprinting at Scotland Yard from 1901; Pinkerton's photographic rogues' gallery; revolutionary successors inheriting portions of old police files in 1917; Room 40 and post-war decryption; the 1923 international police commission pooling records.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Long-horizon consequence is the rarest, most prized feeling in games; dread of the archive gives every early success a sweet-and-sour flavour; the ending is written by systems, not an author.
- **Engine implications:** Dossiers as cold storage with wake triggers; the world clock as a dossier reopener; cheap at scale because sleeping dossiers consume no cycles; needs stable record identity across decades of saves.
- **Risks / open questions:** Inevitability could feel oppressive — there must be real paths to retire clean.
