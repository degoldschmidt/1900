# Round 1A Proposals — Life-Sim & Character Progression Designer (Panelist P10)

## Panelist statement
My lens is the spy as a whole life lived across up to thirty years: a body that ages and falls ill, skills that grow and rust, names that must be fed and can die, households and dependants that eat money and leak information, debts that keep their own calendar, and an ending that the life itself earns. The game must get one thing right: everything mundane (rent, a cough, a birthday) must run on the same resource system as everything operational (a stolen blueprint, a frontier, a tail), so that the private life is never a side-menu but the very record the hunters eventually read. I propose states, resources and rules only; whatever people and plots appear are what the systems generate.

## P10.01 — Thirty Winters: The Whole-Life Arc as the Game
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** One life, 1895–1925, the agent ageing from early twenties to early fifties while three unsynchronised clocks run: the world clock (macro-events), the life clock (ageing, health, skill rust) and the hunt clock (accumulated exposure). The game is designed to end; the player's craft is choosing how.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Minutes: operations, conversations, travel legs, trades. Days: routing, correspondence, feeding names and households. Yearly: an audit (P10.11) applies age curves, compounds debts, decays unmaintained standings and raises the hunt's baseline from every exposure the life has generated. Early years are cheap and forgiving (passport-free Europe, gold-standard money, a body that heals); middle years build an empire of households, concessions and retainers; late years are the hunt, when wartime border regimes, photographic passports and thickening files make every past choice load-bearing.
- **Directive coverage:** Life-sim primary; travel, deception, items, economy and politics are the verbs by which the life accumulates; time pressure comes from three unaligned clocks; the hunt is the life's integral.
- **Historical grounding:** The window spans the shift from a passport-free, gold-standard Europe before systematic fingerprinting (Scotland Yard, 1901) to one of visas, exit permits, counter-espionage bureaux and currency collapse.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Mortality salience plus the endowment effect: the player has built things that can be lost, and the drift from freedom to vulnerability generates unscripted dread. Every mundane decision has a thirty-year tail: the "one more year" pull of grand-strategy campaigns.
- **Engine implications:** Append-only, event-sourced life record; decay functions evaluated lazily on access, not per tick; year-granular compaction of stale state; the hunter AI mines the same record.
- **Risks / open questions:** Pacing thirty years into sessions; many players will never finish a life, so fast-forward and audit cadence must carry it.

## P10.02 — Legends as Maintained Assets
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** A cover biography is an owned asset: a fact-graph (name, nationality, profession, residence, bank, references, papers) whose facts carry evidentiary weights that decay unless fed and can be contradicted by the player's other legends.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player builds a legend fact by fact: a hotel-registration trail, a bank account, an accommodation address receiving real letters, a club membership, a visible means of support. Each fact costs money and time to maintain, because the name must appear somewhere periodically. The engine records where each legend was witnessed (city, circle, date); legends witnessed in overlapping circles accumulate collision risk, and a collision becomes an inconsistency record hunters can find. Legends may be rested (cheap, decays), retired cleanly (slow, expensive) or burnt (instant; every fact becomes hunter evidence).
- **Directive coverage:** Deception primary. Travel: frontier regimes test papers; passport-free pre-1914 Europe demands social proof instead. Items: cards, letters and papers with forgery grades. Economy: legends cost money and their cover occupation must produce real transactions. Politics: nationality becomes enemy-alien status in 1914. Hunt: legends are the deduction target.
- **Historical grounding:** Letters of introduction, visiting cards, hotel police registration, residence permits, accommodation addresses, enemy-alien registration from 1914, photographic passports from 1914–15, the 1920 League standardisation.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Plate-spinning cognition: several fragile constructs each demanding attention is a reliable flow generator. The endowment effect makes a well-fed legend feel like property; dread of collision supplies tension with no authored content.
- **Engine implications:** Per-legend weighted, timestamped fact graphs; witness records as a sparse legend × circle × city matrix; collision detection by graph intersection on access.
- **Risks / open questions:** Upkeep must not become chores; needs a legible legend-health view and delegable routines that leak.

## P10.03 — Households as Resource Engines
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** A household (flat, house, long-let hotel suite, room above a shop) is a persistent state machine anchored to one city and one legend: it produces plausibility, storage, an address and local standing; consumes rent, wages and attention; and leaks information through everyone who serves it.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player leases under a name, staffs abstract roles (housekeeper, valet, cook, porter) carrying wage, discretion and loyalty values, stores items and papers at graded concealment levels, and anchors the legend to the address. Each household has a quarter-day rent calendar, a condition stock, a talk rate (staff and neighbours emit witness records) and standing in local circles. Neglect cascades: unpaid rent, distraint on goods, an auction notice, a court record, documents in hunters' hands. Several households in several cities multiply plausibility and refuges but divide money and attention. External regimes can delete a household: wartime requisition, enemy-property custodians from 1914, expropriation from 1917.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy and deception primary. Travel: a household is where luggage, caches and post wait between legs. Items: graded storage. Politics: requisition and seizure regimes. Hunt: every household is a hiding place and a known address at once.
- **Historical grounding:** Quarterly rent terms, servant-keeping as ordinary middle-class practice, concierges and Russian yard-keepers reporting to police, distraint for rent, trading-with-the-enemy custodianship, Soviet expropriation.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Nest-building compulsion; the endowment effect turns every household into a hostage to fortune; "spread or consolidate" is a renewable strategic dilemma.
- **Engine implications:** Household entities depending on city rent calendars, legal regimes and the local circle graph; cascade rules fired on missed payments; lazy evaluation while the player is absent.
- **Risks / open questions:** Micro-management load; delegated management must exist and must leak.

## P10.04 — Circles of Repute: Reputation Attached to Names, Not to the Player
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Reputation is not one bar but a set of circles (a diplomatic set, an officers' mess, a bourse, a dockside underworld, an émigré colony, a service's desk), each with memory, gossip rate and bridges to other circles, each remembering a name rather than the player.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Actions under a legend write to that legend's standing in the circle that witnessed them: trusted, dangerous, rich, ridiculous, foreign, wanted. Standing decays toward neutral with time and distance. Circles connect through bridging institutions (a bank, a club, a consulate); gossip crosses bridges at a rate, so a scandal surfaces elsewhere weeks later, possibly linking two names if someone sits in both circles. High standing opens gates (introductions, credit, invitations); low standing in the right circle is an asset. Hunters read circles too: notoriety raises ambient awareness.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception and conversation primary (conversation moves are reputation moves). Economy: credit is reputation. Politics: circles align with factions and flip in 1914 and 1917. Travel: circles are place-bound; arriving with no standing differs from returning with too much. Hunt: notoriety propagation.
- **Historical grounding:** Club and mess culture, blackballing, letters of introduction, bourse rumour, consular networks, émigré colonies watched by the Okhrana's Paris bureau, society columns.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The double-life fantasy made mechanical: being a different person in each circle is legible juggling, and gossip propagation yields delayed, deducible consequences: the dread of "when will that catch up with me".
- **Engine implications:** Circle graph per city; sparse name × circle standing vectors; propagation along bridges with rate and decay, capped breadth per tick.
- **Risks / open questions:** Too many circles become noise; keep five to eight active per city with a legible map.

## P10.05 — The Rust Model: Skills Grow by Use and Decay by Disuse
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every skill (languages by social register, cipher work, forgery, marksmanship, riding, motoring, wireless operation, bookkeeping, dancing, lock work) is a learning curve with use-based growth, time-based forgetting and age-modified rates. Rusty skills return cheaply but produce tells meanwhile.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Skills are exercised by actions, never bought. Growth is fast at low levels and slows toward mastery; disuse decays effective level toward a retained floor, so mastery returns at a discount but costs time at the wrong moment. Age bends the curves: physical skills decline early, cognitive late, social grow longest. Period technology moves the skill map: motoring is rare in 1900 and expected of a class by 1920; wireless operation is a trade born inside the game's span; typewriting becomes ordinary clerical work, and typefaces identify machines.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception (a rusted accent register is a tell) and coding (cipher speed and error rate) primary. Travel: riding, motoring, reading a foreign timetable. Economy: bookkeeping and languages gate business. Politics: military skills matter 1914–22. Hunt: a tell is a witness record.
- **Historical grounding:** Motor licensing (France from 1893, Britain 1903, Germany nationally 1909), Marconi operators, typewriter typeface identification, French as the diplomatic language, class-coded accomplishments (riding, dancing, cards).
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Mastery and the pain of letting go: the player curates a portfolio and chooses which selves to keep sharp. "Use it or lose it" is a renewable prompt to act, and rust-induced near-misses create memorable moments.
- **Engine implications:** Skill stored as (level, last-used, floor) evaluated lazily; age modifiers as functions of birth year; tells emitted as witness records.
- **Risks / open questions:** Decay can feel punitive; tune half-lives generously and surface rust before it bites.

## P10.06 — Vices as Compound Interest (risky)
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Gambling, drink, opiates and cocaine, spending and liaisons are habit systems with craving, tolerance and withdrawal. Each gives real short-term benefits (access, nerve, liquidity, information) and compounding long-term costs (health, debt, blackmail, predictability), so the player manages the agent's addiction while the game works on their own.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each vice has a craving stock rising with stress and time, a tolerance level raising the dose needed, and a withdrawal penalty on skills if unmet. Gambling is a true variable-ratio system (tables, racecourses, private clubs) that also opens society and underworld circles and liquidates assets. Drink lubricates conversation and dulls fear at the cost of tells. Opiates manage wound pain (P10.07) and become a dependency whose supply tightens with the law. Liaisons are abstract entanglement entries (P10.08) granting access and generating letters, i.e. physical evidence. Habitual consumption makes the agent predictable (P10.10).
- **Directive coverage:** Economy (debt, liquidity) and deception (tells, blackmail) primary. Items: supply and letters. Politics: legal regimes shift (Hague Opium Convention 1912, Harrison Act 1914, Dangerous Drugs Act 1920; Russian prohibition 1914, US Prohibition 1920, absinthe bans). Travel: where supply is legal. Hunt: predictability.
- **Historical grounding:** Over-the-counter morphine and cocaine before regulation, Monte Carlo and private clubs, wartime prohibitions.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The meta-mirror: the player feels variable-ratio reinforcement at the table while watching its cost on the ledger, producing self-aware, remembered play. Vices are also the game's pressure valves: the tension/relief cycle is built in.
- **Engine implications:** Per-vice state (craving, tolerance, supply); law-regime tables by jurisdiction and date; coupling hooks into health, debt and hunt.
- **Risks / open questions:** Taste and ratings; must stay mechanical, never glamorise; players may optimise vices away, so benefits must genuinely tempt.

## P10.07 — The Body Keeps the Books: Health, Injury and Period Medicine
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Health is several stocks (constitution, acute injuries, chronic conditions, infection exposures) with recovery measured in weeks during which neither the world nor the hunt pauses, and period medicine that is slow, expensive and leaves records.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Wounds heal on timers modified by age, care and rest; infection is a probabilistic complication without antibiotics. Chronic conditions (tuberculosis, syphilis, gas-damaged lungs from 1915, an old fracture) are permanent modifiers with flare rules. Cities carry seasonal disease exposure (typhoid; cholera in Russia 1908–10; influenza 1918–20), so routing is also health routing. Treatment is logistics: a sanatorium season buys recovery and concealment for months, money and a registration trail; Salvarsan from 1910 changes the syphilis calculus; X-rays from 1895 reveal concealed injuries; morphine manages pain and starts a habit (P10.06). Prescriptions, bills and case notes are stealable items. Ageing lowers ceilings and lengthens every timer.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary: convalescence is a forced wait against two other clocks. Travel: health gates class and speed; a sick agent cannot manage third class or a Siberian winter. Economy: medicine costs money. Deception: a limp or scar is an identifier. Politics: war makes wounds likely. Hunt: medical registration.
- **Historical grounding:** No antibiotics until the mid-1930s, Salvarsan 1910, X-rays 1895, sanatorium culture, typhoid inoculation from 1896, the influenza pandemic.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Vulnerability raises stakes; convalescence creates planning tension because the world moves while you lie still; permanent scars give the life a texture the player authored.
- **Engine implications:** Health stocks with timers; per-city seasonal exposure tables; treatment as scheduled multi-week actions coupled to the hunt and travel graphs.
- **Risks / open questions:** Long waits frustrate; design convalescence as a planning phase (correspondence, cipher work, delegated business).

## P10.08 — The Obligations Ledger: Dependants, Pensioners and Creditors as Leverage Lines
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Everyone bound to the player is an abstract obligation entry (demand frequency, cost, loyalty, leverage, failure consequence), and every obligation is at once an asset (access, cover, labour) and a lever the hunters can pull.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Obligations accrue from play: household staff (P10.03), a burnt sub-agent's dependants, a patron expecting favours, a liaison (P10.06), an informant on retainer. Each has a calendar (monthly remittances, school fees, quarterly visits) and a loyalty state that decays when demands are missed. Missed obligations convert into leverage: letters, denunciations, saleable debts of honour. Hunters target the web: pressure on a dependant produces an approach event the player may not detect. The player can discharge an obligation (expensive, final), hide it, or cut it (loyalty turns hostile and leverage activates). No entry is a character: each is a tuple given institutional context.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception (every obligation knows one of your names) and hunt (the web is the hunters' map) primary. Economy: remittances are a fixed cost base. Politics: obligations in enemy territory after 1914 become unreachable or treasonous. Travel: visiting placed obligations is routing. Items: letters as evidence.
- **Historical grounding:** Remittances by postal order and bank draft, boarding schools, secret-service funds, third-party cover addresses, the Okhrana's and Cheka's practice of pressuring relatives.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Sunk cost and guilt expressed as resources; moral weight emerges from the ledger rather than a script; fear of an approach makes the player protective of abstractions: attachment.
- **Engine implications:** Obligation entities with calendars, loyalty decay and leverage payloads referencing legends; hunter target selection over the obligation graph.
- **Risks / open questions:** Must stay humane in presentation without becoming narrative; needs a clear ledger view.

## P10.09 — Debt as the Second Clock
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Debt is a time system, not a negative balance: every borrowing has an instrument (bill of exchange, overdraft, moneylender's note, debt of honour, tradesman's account, a service's advance), a maturity, an interest rule and a jurisdictional enforcement regime, filling the calendar with due-dates independent of the world clock.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player raises money against standing (P10.04) and legends (P10.02). Each instrument has a rollover at rising cost and a default consequence: a protested bill becomes a public record; a gazetted bankruptcy burns the name it was incurred under, expelling it from clubs and credit; an unpaid debt of honour destroys standing in the witnessing circle; an unredeemed service advance converts into coercive tasking. Debts are tradable paper, buyable as leverage by or against the player. Currency regimes are debt events: gold parities make multi-currency debt safe until 1914; exchange controls, rouble collapse and mark hyperinflation then wipe or multiply debts depending on which side the player stands.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy and time pressure primary. Deception: debt under which name. Politics: currency regimes and moratoria. Travel: enforcement is jurisdictional, so fleeing a debt is routing. Hunt: bankruptcy gazettes are public documents.
- **Historical grounding:** Three-month usance bills, quarter-day rents, bankruptcy gazettes, the abolition of imprisonment for debt across Western Europe while bankruptcy remained social ruin, the 1914 moratoria, the 1923 hyperinflation.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The quarter-day as a dread appointment; leverage as thrill; "making the payment" releases tension on a reliable cadence and teaches deliberate risk.
- **Engine implications:** Debt instruments as scheduled events with rollover branches; jurisdiction tables; a currency index by date.
- **Risks / open questions:** Debt spirals ending runs early; give early warning and make bankruptcy playable, not terminal.

## P10.10 — Routine Is a Trail: The Personal Calendar the Hunters Read (risky)
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** The mundane calendar (rent days, club dinners, doctors' appointments, school terms, letters to a cover address) shares one scheduler with operations and travel, and the hunter AI deduces from habits: regularity makes a legend plausible and the agent catchable; irregularity preserves freedom and thins the legend.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player keeps appointments to feed legends (P10.02), circles (P10.04) and obligations (P10.08); every kept appointment writes a timestamped witness record. Hunters see, Scotland Yard–style, transport types and periodic reveals, but also mine the pattern: a name dining at the same club every Thursday is an ambush point; a remittance posted on the first of each month is a trace. The player can break pattern (costly to plausibility), delegate (leaky) or lay false routine (expensive, but a trap). Pursuer inference power varies by service and date.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and time pressure primary. Deception: plausibility versus predictability is the core dial. Travel: a routine is a route. Economy: standing orders and remittances. Politics: which service hunts, with what powers. Items: letters and tickets as pattern evidence.
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana external-surveillance logs, hotel registration cadence, postal perlustration, the regularity of bourgeois life (quarter-days, club nights, spa seasons).
- **Why captivating & addictive:** A dilemma that never resolves: what makes you safe socially makes you vulnerable physically. It converts life-sim upkeep into stealth decisions, so nothing is ever "just a chore", and successful misdirection yields unauthored triumphs.
- **Engine implications:** Unified scheduler for personal and operational events; pattern mining over the witness log (periodicity detection, place clustering); pursuer models with varying inference power.
- **Risks / open questions:** Opacity: the player must see their own pattern roughly as hunters might, through an instrument that does not trivialise it.

## P10.11 — The Annual Audit: Life at Three Time Scales
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Time runs at three granularities, minutes (conversation, pursuit), days (legs, letters, business) and years, and the year boundary is a ritual audit that batch-applies ageing, rust, compounding, standing decay, legend review, obligation demands and the hunt's baseline rise, and stamps the coming year's known macro-events onto the calendar.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Play proceeds in days and minutes. At each year-end the game pauses into an audit: a ledger showing age and health changes, what rusted, what matured, which names are thin, which circles have forgotten you, what the hunters are believed to hold, what fell due. Here the player decides where to winter, which household to close, which legend to retire, which skill to retrain, which currency to hold, whether to take a sanatorium season. The audit also presents the coming year's known schedule (seasonal closures, conferences, treaty dates, mobilisation risk); unknown events still arrive mid-year.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Travel: seasonal planning around ice closures. Economy: compounding and currency choices. Deception: legend review. Politics: the macro-event calendar. Items: depreciation. Hunt: baseline exposure.
- **Historical grounding:** The social year (spa seasons, parliamentary sessions, trade fairs), annual accounts and quarter-days, the fixed dates of the period's world clock.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Ritual cadence creates natural session boundaries and the strongest "one more year" pull in strategy design; the audit converts diffuse decay into a legible report the player owns; it is also the natural save point.
- **Engine implications:** Batched lazy evaluation at the year boundary; event compaction; deterministic replay from audit snapshots; sparse day-level simulation between audits.
- **Risks / open questions:** The audit must not feel like a tax return; limit it to a few consequential decisions with sharp feedback.

## P10.12 — Exits: Retirement, Ruin, Exile, Capture and Succession (risky)
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** Endings are systems of visible preconditions the player steers toward: retirement (unencumbered wealth in a stable currency, a clean name, a non-extraditing jurisdiction), exile, ruin, capture, death, turning (joining a service as its hunter), plus succession: play continues as a protégé inheriting households, debts, standings and the hunters' file.
- **How it plays (core loop):** An exit dashboard shows how near each ending is: wealth by currency and jurisdiction, clean names, the hunters' file size. Retirement requires converting the life over game-years while the hunt continues: selling concessions, closing households, discharging obligations, buying an annuity. Ruin and capture are survivable system states. Succession, chosen or forced by death, resets body and skills but not the world: a system-generated successor inherits a configurable share of assets, every debt and household, standings in circles that knew the predecessor's organisation, and a two-subject hunters' file.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy (liquidating a life) and time pressure (exit windows open and close with the world clock; 1914 and 1917 slam doors) primary. Politics: extradition regimes. Travel: reaching the refuge. Deception: clean names. Items: what persists. Hunt: the inherited file.
- **Historical grounding:** Annuities as retirement instruments, the extradition-treaty patchwork with political-offence exceptions, émigré destinations, the 1917 expropriations and 1923 hyperinflation that destroyed retirements in the wrong currency.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Goal-gradient on a life scale; legacy and generativity make death a continuation, not a loss, and support roguelike replay; steering toward a chosen exit gives the long game a destination without a story.
- **Engine implications:** Exit conditions as declarative queries over the life record; succession as state transfer under inheritance rules; multi-subject hunter files.
- **Risks / open questions:** Succession must stay abstract; players may game visible thresholds, so some should remain hidden.
