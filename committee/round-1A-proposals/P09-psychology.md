# Round 1A Proposals — Player Psychology & Retention Designer (Panelist P09)

## Panelist statement
I judge every mechanic by two questions: what pulls a player in within the first minute, and what makes them refuse the natural stopping point an hour later. The compulsion must come from honest sources — tension/relief cycles anchored to the period's real clocks, loss aversion over things the player actually built, mastery that lives in the player's head, and choices that visibly close doors. No variable-ratio reward schedules, no appointment nagging, no manufactured scarcity: players should keep playing because the world moves without them and because what they hold is worth protecting.

---

## P09.01 — The Next Departure
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Session structure is dictated by the timetable, not by menus. Every pause the game offers (hotel bed, sleeper berth, safe house) is placed by the system a few in-game hours before a departure the player can already see, so the natural stopping point always sits in the shadow of a visible connection.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player plans a leg from a Bradshaw-style timetable: departures, durations, fares, classes, connections. Arrival opens a city window — the hours until the next useful departure — spent on errands: bank, poste restante, contact, market. The game offers rest-and-save at the hotel register, but the board shows the 23:40 through-train that would reach Berlin before the bourse opens. Night trains convert "I'll stop here" into "I'll sleep on the train": the leg resolves on next load with the world and the hunt advanced.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and time pressure primary (the city window is a countdown). Deception, economy and politics fill the window. The hunt: a hotel stop means police registration, a trace, so the save point costs cover; the sleeper leaves a ticket-class trace instead.
- **Historical grounding:** Bradshaw's Continental Guide; through-trains (Orient Express, Nord-Express); Wagons-Lits sleeping cars; compulsory hotel registration in Germany, Austria, France and Russia; bourse hours; several postal deliveries a day.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** This harnesses the Zeigarnik effect (unfinished plans stay active in mind) and the goal-gradient effect (effort rises as a visible goal nears). The player always pauses within sight of a connection and so seldom pauses. Sessions end aboard a train, converting stopping into anticipation — a cliffhanger generated by the schedule, never authored. Because the hotel pause carries an honest cover cost, continuing is a real choice, not a nag.
- **Engine implications:** Timetable as data rows; the city window as a scheduled-event queue; deferred, deterministic resolution of legs (world and hunter advance, then resolve on load); saves keyed to a world timestamp. Batch resolution scales linearly with legs.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players who truly want to stop must not be punished hard; the register cost must stay modest. Off-screen resolution must never kill the player without warning signs visible before boarding.

## P09.02 — The Reveal Calendar
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The hunt runs on a known cadence. At scheduled intervals the pursuing services consolidate the traces the player left into a "fix" on their whereabouts. The player knows exactly when the fix happens but not what it will show, because its quality depends entirely on their own prior behaviour.
- **How it plays (core loop):** A calendar strip shows the next consolidation date. Between fixes the player trades, travels and meets, each act leaving a typed trace (ticket class, hotel register, telegram copy, bank draft). On fix day the player receives an abstract readout: which services narrowed to which region and which trace type gave them most. A short window follows to move, swap legend or go quiet before a cordon forms. Cadence tightens as the world changes: weekly notices before 1914, near-daily under wartime censorship and universal passport control.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and time pressure primary. Travel is the escape between fixes; deception (legend swap) breaks a fix; items (forged papers) enable it; economy (going quiet forfeits income); politics decides which services hunt and whether they share.
- **Historical grounding:** Scotland Yard reveal cadence mapped onto real practice: wanted notices in the press, police gazettes, telegraphed circulars to frontier posts, the Okhrana's Paris bureau, 1914 passport controls, wartime censorship offices.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Scheduled uncertainty — a fixed interval with uncertain content — is the strongest honest anticipation structure: tension climbs predictably toward fix day, relief or alarm follows, then reset. Unlike random reveals it is fully causal, so outcomes carry an internal locus of control and feel earned. Learning to read trace types is a mastery curve. The "quiet week before the fix" creates a tempo of boldness and retreat: the tension/relief cycle that sustains long-term play.
- **Engine implications:** Trace log (type, timestamp, location, legend); per-service inference executed at scheduled ticks; cordon generation as an event; cadence as a world-state parameter altered by macro-events (1914, 1917). Batch inference at ticks is cheap and deterministic.
- **Risks / open questions:** Readout abstraction: too legible is exploitable, too opaque feels unfair. Cadence tuning per era.

## P09.03 — Legends You Can Burn
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Cover identities are long-lived assets with their own ledgers — bank account, address, letters of introduction, club standing, registration history. They appreciate with use and can be deliberately burned to collapse a hunter's fix. The game makes the player love them first.
- **How it plays (core loop):** A legend is built over months: hotel registrations under it, an account opened, references acquired, trades conducted. Each use adds credibility (doors open faster) and exposure (trace density). When the hunt closes, the player chooses which legend to burn: its account, household and contacts become inaccessible, and the fix collapses into pursuit of a ghost. Legends can be partially rehabilitated at cost, or sold — a clean legend is an item with provenance.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception and hunt primary. Economy: accounts and letters of credit are name-bound, so wealth is split across legends. Travel: after 1914 passports are per-legend. Items: forged papers, visiting cards. Politics: a legend's nationality decides which frontiers open in wartime.
- **Historical grounding:** Letters of introduction and visiting cards as credentials; name-bound letters of credit and circular notes; hotel police registration; the 1914 passport regime and its 1920 standardisation; forged papers; the period agent's practice of parallel households in several cities.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The endowment effect: people value what they have built far above its market worth, so burning a legend nurtured for twenty hours is a genuine loss — the hunt bites without killing. Loss aversion makes the player ration exposure, turning every registration into a meaningful choice. Choosing which legend dies is rich in anticipated regret, and the fresh start of a new legend supplies the clean-slate appeal that makes roguelikes compulsive, inside one continuous career.
- **Engine implications:** Identity as an entity with its own trace log, accounts and relationship edges; a dependency graph so burning cascades correctly (contacts bound to the name, not the agent); provenance records for sold legends. Scales as records.
- **Risks / open questions:** Interface load; cascade correctness; legend hoarding, countered by upkeep costs.

## P09.04 — The Second Agent
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** One persistent world runs 1895–1925 on its own clock. The player's agent can be caught, killed or ruined; when that happens the world does not reset. The player takes up a new agent at the current date with nothing but what the player knows. The world remembers the predecessor: dossier, burned legends, debts, artifacts in evidence rooms.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Live a career; fail; begin another with the clock still running — 1912 now, Balkan war looming, the predecessor's codebook in a Vienna evidence vault, a Paris account frozen. Routes, timetables, codes, which markets move in which crises: this knowledge is the only progression and it lives in the player's head. Recovering the predecessor's assets is an optional objective with its own trace cost.
- **Directive coverage:** All five. Politics/warfare drive the clock; the hunt persists as institutional memory; economy as frozen or recoverable assets; items as persistent artifacts with provenance; travel knowledge as the mastery axis; deception restarts from zero legends.
- **Historical grounding:** The thirty-year macro-event calendar; institutional continuity of services and archives (Okhrana files inherited by successors; Special Branch records); frozen accounts and expropriation after 1917; the genuine expertise of Bradshaw-literate travellers.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** This dissolves the sunk-cost versus fresh-start conflict that kills retention in permadeath games: failure costs the agent, not the world or the player's competence. Mastery is honest and visible — a veteran plans a Constantinople escape in seconds — which is competence in the self-determination sense, the most durable motivator known. The predecessor's trail opens a curiosity gap. The hard 1925 horizon makes the campaign finite, so every agent's life is a chapter the player writes by playing.
- **Engine implications:** Single persistent world state with multiple agent lifespans; entity memory across agents; fully data-driven content (no scripted sequences to break); deterministic macro-event schedule with perturbation flags. The world is one simulation; agents are entities.
- **Risks / open questions:** Late-start agents miss the pre-war era; wartime is harder, which may be a feature. Needs a world-state difficulty curve rather than a player-level one.

## P09.05 — Stale News
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player never sees the world directly, only through period channels with realistic lag: newspapers (multiple editions, location-dependent), telegrams (fast, costly, interceptable), letters (slow, private), rumour (free, unreliable). Everything acted upon is hours to weeks old.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each morning in a city the player chooses which editions and wires to buy. Headlines reveal that something happened — a mobilisation, a bourse panic, an arrest — but details wait for the next edition or for arrival. Acting on stale information is the core risk: a concession buyable three days ago in Baku is gone; a frontier that was open is closed. Cables cost money and leave a copy at the telegraph office; a commercial code shortens and obscures. The player may also plant news through an agency stringer.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary — the lag is the pressure. Coding (cable codes); economy (information arbitrage); politics (macro-events arrive late); travel (lag depends on where you stand); hunt (telegram copies are traces; wanted notices are themselves news).
- **Historical grounding:** Multi-edition dailies; Reuters, Havas, Wolff; telegraph companies retaining copies; commercial codebooks; the All Red Line (1902); wireless; poste restante; censorship from 1914.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Loewenstein's information-gap theory of curiosity: a headline without detail is a gap the mind must close, so "buy the evening edition" is irresistible. Need for closure pulls the player physically toward events. Counterfactual feedback arrives honestly through the world's own channels — the paper reports what happened where you were not — producing regret and anticipation without any authored voice. Experts learn which city hears which news first: a mastery curve of its own.
- **Engine implications:** An event-visibility layer: each event has a propagation profile per channel and location; player knowledge is stored separately from world truth and diffed lazily; interception hooks on cables. Propagation is a per-event function, cheap at scale.
- **Risks / open questions:** Lag must be legible (edition dates visible) or it reads as unfair; planted news must not trivialise markets.

## P09.06 — The Obligation Calendar
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The spy's life is a calendar of dated promises — deliveries, meetings, bill maturities, household upkeep, visa expiries, reports due. The calendar is the main planning surface, and the hunter reads it too, because obligations predict location.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Every accepted commission, loan, lease or rendezvous places a dated entry. The player routes travel to satisfy them; conflicts are visible and resolved by renegotiation (trust loss), proxies (cut-outs, cover addresses) or default (debt and reputation consequences). The hunter's inference uses observable obligations — a bill of exchange maturing in Vienna, a registered lease — to predict presence, so the player's reliability is their vulnerability. Breaking pattern costs money and trust.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Economy (bills, debts, rents); hunt (predictability as evidence); travel (the calendar is a routing problem); deception (proxies; false appointments as lures); politics (faction retainers demand reports on dates).
- **Historical grounding:** Bills of exchange with fixed maturities; letters of credit; multiple households in different cities; visa validity periods after 1914; club dues; telegraphed appointments; poste restante holding limits.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Commitment devices: people honour self-set deadlines far more than imposed ones, so every promise becomes a deadline the player owns. The planning fallacy becomes a learnable skill — veterans leave slack. The reliability-versus-predictability dilemma is a recurring meaningful choice, not a one-off. Dated obligations also create natural sessions: the player knows the 14th is the Vienna maturity and plays toward it, which is the goal-gradient effect at the scale of a week.
- **Engine implications:** Dated obligation entities with dependencies; conflict detection; the hunter granted access only to observable obligations; default cascades through debt and reputation. Scales as a priority queue.
- **Risks / open questions:** The calendar can become a spreadsheet; obligation types must be few and legible.

## P09.07 — The Commission Board
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Opportunities — commissions from services, firms and factions; trades; meetings — arrive as time-windowed offers tied to the world clock and made mutually exclusive by geography: accepting one in Baku forecloses one in Tangier purely by travel time. The player picks and watches the rest lapse.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The board fills from world state: an arms commission after a crisis, a concession when a railway reaches a province, a document job when a fleet sails. Each offer carries a window, a location, a payoff and a trace cost. The player evaluates by timetable (can I even get there?), by hunt state (does it cross a cordon?) and by politics (who is angered). Lapsed offers are reported with their outcomes: taken by a rival, or evaporated as the crisis cooled.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Travel (feasibility is a timetable query); economy (payoffs, trades); politics (offers generated by macro-events); deception (the jobs themselves); hunt (trace cost); items (deliverables with provenance).
- **Historical grounding:** Commission agents for Vickers, Krupp, Schneider and Škoda; concession brokering in Manchuria, Persia and Ottoman lands; Baku oil; naval plans as prize documents; the crisis calendar (Morocco 1905 and 1911, Bosnia 1908, the Balkan Wars).
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Meaningful choice requires mutual exclusivity; here every acceptance is also a visible refusal. Scarcity is honest, arising from geometry and the world clock, not from timers. Anticipated regret sharpens each decision, and reported outcomes of lapsed offers deliver counterfactual feedback that fuels "next time". Because offers follow foreseeable macro-events, the board's refresh produces scheduled anticipation: Morocco is heating up, so offers will come.
- **Engine implications:** Offer generator reading world state; expiry queue; feasibility checks against timetable and cordons; outcome resolution for unaccepted offers. Offers are lightweight and can be generated lazily per region.
- **Risks / open questions:** Must not degrade into a quest log — keep offers few and consequential.

## P09.08 — Cipher Hands
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Encoding and decoding are performed by the player with period methods — commercial codebooks, Vigenère, Playfair, columnar transposition, book cipher — taught incrementally. Skill lives in the player's head; in-world aids trade effort for cost and trace.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Messages arrive as ciphertext. The player chooses: decode by hand (free, private, slow in real time), use a codebook item (fast, but the book is an incriminating artifact that can be found or stolen), or pay a commercial decoding service (fast, costs money, leaves a copy). Sending mirrors this. Mastery compounds: a player who knows Playfair reads a captured dispatch in minutes while the hunt advances by only minutes.
- **Directive coverage:** Coding primary. Items (codebooks with provenance); economy (clerk fees; commercial codes cut telegram costs); hunt (plaintext in luggage is evidence; cable copies are traces); politics (systems change with the war: ADFGVX in 1918, one-time pads 1917–19); time (decoding consumes in-game hours).
- **Historical grounding:** Playfair in British field use; Vigenère and transposition; book ciphers; ABC and Bentley's commercial codes; Room 40; the Zimmermann telegram as the archetypal intercept; ADFGVX; the one-time pad.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Competence, in self-determination theory, is the most durable motivator; a skill genuinely acquired, not a stat raised, produces flow and pride. The staircase from commercial code to Playfair to transposition is a mastery curve with visible payoff. The three-way choice keeps novice and expert both engaged while making expertise strictly better for concealment. Decoding a captured message is a curiosity gap closed by effort, the most satisfying closure there is.
- **Engine implications:** Cipher routines as pure functions; message entities carrying system and key; key distribution as world events; a difficulty ramp and an optional assist layer. Trivially scalable.
- **Risks / open questions:** Friction for some players; core progress must never be gated on hand-decoding — the paid route must always exist.

## P09.09 — Comfort or Cover
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every convenience of the age — first class, a Wagons-Lits berth, a grand hotel, a motor taxi, a telegram, a letter of credit — is faster, healthier and better for standing, and more registered. Every anonymous alternative — third class, boarding house, gold coin, walking, the post — is slower, harder on health and nearly traceless. The player tunes one continuous dial every day.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each purchase presents the dial implicitly: class, lodging, payment instrument, channel. Health is a resource worn down by cheap choices (fatigue, illness, the 1918 pandemic); standing — doors opened by apparent means — is worn down too. Trace density rises with expensive choices and is harvested at frontier posts and hotel registers. The dial interacts with legends: a wealthy legend travelling third class is itself an anomaly.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary. Economy (the price of anonymity); deception (consistency of legend with mode); items (coin versus instruments); politics (wartime requisition removes first class; passport checks shift the dial).
- **Historical grounding:** Three classes of carriage; Wagons-Lits; hotel police registration; telegraph copies; name-bound credit instruments against gold coin; motor taxis c. 1905–10; the influenza pandemic; wartime requisition of rolling stock.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Approach-avoidance conflict is the engine of tension: the player wants the sleeper and fears the register. Because the dial is continuous and daily it yields micro-dilemmas at a rhythm that sustains attention without fatigue — not every choice is high-stakes, but all are consequential. Loss aversion operates on two fronts (health, cover), so no dominant strategy exists; the player's style emerges and becomes identity, which is exactly what a modelling hunter exploits (P09.10).
- **Engine implications:** Every transaction carries a cost vector (money, time, health, standing, trace type and density); frontier and register events harvest traces; a legend-consistency check. Vectors on data rows — cheap to scale.
- **Risks / open questions:** The dial must be visible without becoming a spreadsheet; health death spirals need a floor.

## P09.10 — The Dossier Mirror
- **Type:** game-defining system (risky)
- **Central mechanical hook:** The hunter maintains a dossier on the player built only from traces: habits (always first class, the same cable address, bank visits on Thursdays), known legends, associated institutions. The player can steal, buy or glimpse their own dossier, see themselves as the hunter sees them, and must decide which habits to break at efficiency cost.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Pursuit prediction is pattern-based, not omniscient: the hunter forecasts where a creature of habit will be. Periodically the player can read the dossier — intercept a circular, bribe a registry, burgle a bureau — as an abstract, honest list of inferred regularities with confidence levels. The player sees precisely which conveniences betray them. Breaking a pattern costs time, money or health (P09.09), and a sudden break is itself a detectable anomaly.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and deception primary — counter-surveillance as self-knowledge. Travel (routes as habits); economy (pattern-breaking costs); items (the dossier is a stealable artifact); politics (whether services pool dossiers depends on alliances: the 1907 Convention, Entente sharing in wartime).
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana Paris bureau files; Special Branch; Bertillonage and fingerprinting (1901); mail perlustration; informant networks; inter-allied intelligence sharing; wanted notices.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Being modelled is uncanny and compelling: the game reflects the player's own behaviour back as evidence, producing self-recognition and genuine learning. It converts the hunt from "avoid the red dot" into "know thyself"; pattern-breaking is agency at its purest. The dossier also externalises the mastery curve — a veteran's dossier is thin. Risky because the inference must be legible and never feel like cheating; potentially brilliant because the hunter's intelligence becomes a mirror rather than a monster.
- **Engine implications:** Per-service inference over the trace log (frequency, recency, co-occurrence); confidence scoring; dossier serialisation as a readable artifact; an inter-service sharing graph. Inference at ticks, summaries cached.
- **Risks / open questions:** Inference quality; players seeding deliberate noise (acceptable if costly); readability of probabilistic output.

## P09.11 — Three Masters
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player may hold retainers from several services and firms at once. Each maintains a trust/suspicion state; each transaction of information or goods with one is a liability with the others. The game is keeping several plausible accounts of yourself alive simultaneously.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Accept retainers from, say, two services and a trading house. Each demands exclusivity, periodic reports and occasional proofs of loyalty. Information sold to one can leak through the very channels the player exploits — telegraph copies, perlustration, informants — raising suspicion elsewhere. The player balances by feeding each patron something true, something stale (P09.05) and something fabricated; patrons verify with lag. A crossed threshold turns a patron into a pursuer, feeding P09.02.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception and politics primary; alliance shifts (1907 Convention, 1914 alignments) make some combinations impossible overnight. Economy (stacked retainers); hunt (patrons become hunters); coding (reports in cipher); travel (patrons sit in different capitals); time (reports due on dates).
- **Historical grounding:** Freelance and semi-official agents serving several services; the Secret Service Bureau (1909), Deuxième Bureau, Evidenzbureau, Abteilung IIIb, Okhrana; arms-commission double-dealing; wartime censorship increasing leak likelihood.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Plate-spinning produces sustained cognitive engagement — the pleasurable load of keeping accounts consistent — punctuated by near-misses (a leak that almost crossed a threshold). Near-misses motivate strongly when the outcome is under the player's control, which is what separates this from gambling. Each added master raises income and tension together: a self-set difficulty slider. Betrayal becomes an emergent story generated by systems, and the moment a patron turns hunter is a reversal the player authored.
- **Engine implications:** Per-faction belief state; information objects with provenance and per-channel leak probability; threshold events; an alliance graph with scheduled changes. Factions are few; information objects many but lightweight.
- **Risks / open questions:** Legibility of suspicion; preventing "lie to everyone" — fabricated reports must be verifiable with delay.

## P09.12 — The Retirement Clause
- **Type:** whole-game concept (risky)
- **Central mechanical hook:** On any day the player may walk away: liquidate, choose a legend, pick a city, retire. The game ends and scores the life kept — wealth at that day's exchange, the cleanliness of the chosen legend, outstanding debts, which services still hunt. The commission board always has one more job and the hunt always tightens. The whole game's question is when to stop.
- **How it plays (core loop):** All other systems run. The retirement option is permanently visible with a live estimate of what would be kept: wealth in gold-standard terms (after 1914 exposed to exchange controls and collapse — retire on roubles in 1916 and watch it vanish), legend safety, debts, enemies. The player plays on for more and risks all. Death or capture scores far below any retirement. Multiple retirements across careers (P09.04) build a record.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy primary (what you keep). Politics (where retirement is safe changes: not Russia after 1917); hunt (retirement holds only if the chosen legend is unknown); travel (reaching the retirement city); time (the 1925 horizon; currency events); deception (the clean legend).
- **Historical grounding:** Gold-standard parities until 1914; wartime exchange controls; rouble collapse and expropriation from 1917; mark hyperinflation 1923; neutral havens; the model career of the period agent who never retired.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** This is the honest heart of "one more job": sunk cost and loss aversion pull the player to protect accumulated wealth while the escalating board pulls them to risk it. Making quitting-while-ahead a real, scored choice gives the game a genuinely meaningful endgame decision instead of a final confrontation. The live estimate turns every risky act into a visible bet on something the player owns — the endowment effect made explicit. Risky because players may retire too early for a satisfying arc; mitigated by scoring that rewards legend quality and by the persistent world inviting a new agent.
- **Engine implications:** A continuous valuation function over assets, legends, debts and pursuit; a currency-event schedule; end-state generation. Cheap.
- **Risks / open questions:** Early retirement; scoring that accidentally rewards greed; the board must escalate honestly, not artificially.
