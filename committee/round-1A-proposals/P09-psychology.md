# Round 1A Proposals — Player Psychology & Retention Designer (Panelist P09)

## Panelist statement
I judge every mechanic by two questions: what pulls a player in within the first minute, and what makes them refuse the natural stopping point an hour later. The compulsion must come from honest sources — tension/relief cycles anchored to the period's real clocks, loss aversion over things the player actually built, mastery that lives in the player's head, and choices that visibly close doors. No variable-ratio reward schedules, no appointment nagging, no manufactured scarcity: players should keep playing because the world moves without them and because what they hold is worth protecting.

---

## P09.01 — The Next Departure
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The timetable, not a menu, structures the session: every pause the game offers (hotel, sleeper berth, safe house) sits a few in-game hours before a departure the player can already see.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The player plans a leg from a Bradshaw-style timetable: departures, durations, fares, classes, connections. Arrival opens a city window — the hours until the next useful departure — for errands: bank, poste restante, contact, market. Rest-and-save is offered at the hotel register, but the board shows a 23:40 through-train reaching Berlin before the bourse opens. Night trains turn "I'll stop here" into "I'll sleep on the train": the leg resolves on next load with world and hunt advanced.
- **Directive coverage:** Travel and time pressure primary (the window is a countdown). Deception, economy and politics fill the window. Hunt: a hotel stop means police registration, so saving costs cover; a sleeper leaves a ticket-class trace instead.
- **Historical grounding:** Bradshaw's Continental Guide; through-trains (Orient Express, Nord-Express); Wagons-Lits sleepers; compulsory hotel registration in Germany, Austria, France and Russia.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Zeigarnik effect (unfinished plans stay mentally active) plus goal-gradient effect (effort rises as a visible goal nears): the player always pauses within sight of a connection and so seldom pauses. Sessions end aboard a train, converting stopping into anticipation — a cliffhanger generated by the schedule, never authored. The hotel pause carries an honest cover cost, so continuing is a real choice, not a nag.
- **Engine implications:** Timetable as data rows; the city window as a scheduled-event queue; deferred, deterministic leg resolution on load; saves keyed to world time. Scales linearly.
- **Risks / open questions:** Players who truly want to stop must not be punished hard; off-screen resolution must never kill without warning signs visible before boarding.

## P09.02 — The Reveal Calendar
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The hunt runs on a known cadence: at scheduled intervals the pursuing services consolidate the player's traces into a "fix". The player knows exactly when, never what — because the fix's quality depends entirely on their own prior behaviour.
- **How it plays (core loop):** A calendar strip shows the next consolidation date. Between fixes the player trades, travels and meets, each act leaving a typed trace (ticket class, hotel register, telegram copy, bank draft). On fix day an abstract readout arrives: which services narrowed to which region and which trace type gave them most. A short window follows to move, swap legend or go quiet before a cordon forms. Cadence tightens with the world: weekly notices before 1914, near-daily under wartime censorship and passport control.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and time pressure primary. Travel is the escape between fixes; deception (legend swap) breaks a fix; items (forged papers) enable it; economy (going quiet forfeits income); politics decides which services hunt and whether they share.
- **Historical grounding:** Scotland Yard's reveal cadence mapped onto wanted notices, police gazettes, telegraphed circulars to frontier posts, the Okhrana's Paris bureau, 1914 passport controls, wartime censorship.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Scheduled uncertainty — a fixed interval with uncertain content — is the strongest honest anticipation structure: tension climbs toward fix day, relief or alarm follows, then reset. Unlike random reveals it is fully causal, so outcomes carry an internal locus of control and feel earned. Reading trace types is a mastery curve; the quiet week before a fix creates a tempo of boldness and retreat — the tension/relief cycle that sustains long play.
- **Engine implications:** Trace log (type, time, place, legend); per-service inference at scheduled ticks; cordon generation as an event; cadence as a world-state parameter altered by macro-events. Batch inference is cheap and deterministic.
- **Risks / open questions:** Readout abstraction: too legible is exploitable, too opaque feels unfair. Cadence tuning per era.

## P09.03 — Legends You Can Burn
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Cover identities are long-lived assets with their own ledgers — account, address, letters of introduction, registration history. They appreciate with use and can be deliberately burned to collapse a hunter's fix. The game makes the player love them first.
- **How it plays (core loop):** A legend is built over months: hotel registrations, an account, references, trades under its name. Each use adds credibility (doors open faster) and exposure (trace density). When the hunt closes, the player chooses which legend to burn: its account, household and contacts become inaccessible and the fix collapses into pursuit of a ghost. Legends can be partially rehabilitated at cost, or sold — a clean legend is an item with provenance.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception and hunt primary. Economy: letters of credit are name-bound, so wealth is split across legends. Travel: after 1914 passports are per-legend. Items: forged papers, visiting cards. Politics: a legend's nationality decides which wartime frontiers open.
- **Historical grounding:** Letters of introduction and visiting cards as credentials; name-bound letters of credit and circular notes; hotel police registration; the 1914 passport regime, standardised 1920; the period agent's parallel households.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Endowment effect: people value what they built far above market worth, so burning a legend nurtured for twenty hours is a genuine loss — the hunt bites without killing. Loss aversion makes the player ration exposure, turning every registration into a meaningful choice. Choosing which legend dies is rich in anticipated regret, and a new legend offers the clean-slate appeal that makes roguelikes compulsive, inside one continuous career.
- **Engine implications:** Identity as an entity with its own trace log, accounts and relationship edges; a dependency graph so burning cascades correctly (contacts bound to the name, not the agent); provenance for sold legends.
- **Risks / open questions:** Interface load; cascade correctness; legend hoarding, countered by upkeep costs.

## P09.04 — The Second Agent
- **Type:** whole-game concept
- **Central mechanical hook:** One persistent world runs 1895–1925 on its own clock. When the player's agent is caught, killed or ruined, the world does not reset: a new agent begins at the current date with nothing but what the player knows, in a world that remembers the predecessor.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Live a career; fail; begin another with the clock still running — 1912 now, Balkan war looming, the predecessor's codebook in a Vienna evidence vault, a Paris account frozen. Routes, timetables, codes, which markets move in which crises: this knowledge is the only progression and it lives in the player's head. Recovering the predecessor's assets is an optional objective with its own trace cost.
- **Directive coverage:** All five. Politics/warfare drive the clock; the hunt persists as institutional memory; economy as frozen or recoverable assets; items as persistent artifacts with provenance; travel knowledge as the mastery axis; deception restarts from zero legends.
- **Historical grounding:** The thirty-year macro-event calendar; institutional continuity of services and archives; frozen accounts and expropriation after 1917; the real expertise of Bradshaw-literate travellers.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Dissolves the sunk-cost versus fresh-start conflict that kills retention in permadeath games: failure costs the agent, not the world or the player's competence. Mastery is honest and visible — a veteran plans a Constantinople escape in seconds — competence in the self-determination sense, the most durable motivator known. The predecessor's trail opens a curiosity gap. The hard 1925 horizon keeps the campaign finite, so each agent's life is a chapter written by play.
- **Engine implications:** Single persistent world with multiple agent lifespans; entity memory across agents; fully data-driven content; deterministic macro-event schedule with perturbation flags. One simulation; agents are entities.
- **Risks / open questions:** Late-start agents miss the pre-war era; wartime is harder, perhaps a feature. Needs a world-state difficulty curve.

## P09.05 — Stale News
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player never sees the world directly, only through period channels with realistic lag: newspapers (multiple editions, location-dependent), telegrams (fast, costly, interceptable), letters (slow, private), rumour (free, unreliable). Everything acted upon is hours to weeks old.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each morning the player chooses which editions and wires to buy. Headlines reveal that something happened — a mobilisation, a bourse panic, an arrest — but details wait for the next edition or for arrival. Acting on stale information is the core risk: a concession buyable three days ago in Baku is gone; an open frontier has closed. Cables cost money and leave a copy at the telegraph office; a commercial code shortens and obscures. News can also be planted through an agency stringer.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary — the lag is the pressure. Coding (cable codes); economy (information arbitrage); politics (macro-events arrive late); travel (lag depends on where you stand); hunt (telegram copies are traces; wanted notices are news).
- **Historical grounding:** Multi-edition dailies; Reuters, Havas, Wolff; telegraph companies retaining copies; commercial codebooks; the All Red Line (1902); poste restante; censorship from 1914.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Loewenstein's information-gap theory of curiosity: a headline without detail is a gap the mind must close, so "buy the evening edition" is irresistible. Need for closure pulls the player physically toward events. Counterfactual feedback arrives honestly through the world's own channels — the paper reports what happened where you were not — producing regret and anticipation without an authored voice. Learning which city hears what first is its own mastery curve.
- **Engine implications:** An event-visibility layer: each event carries a propagation profile per channel and location; player knowledge is stored apart from world truth and diffed lazily; interception hooks on cables. Cheap at scale.
- **Risks / open questions:** Lag must be legible (edition dates visible) or it reads as unfair; planted news must not trivialise markets.

## P09.06 — The Obligation Calendar
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The spy's life is a calendar of dated promises — deliveries, meetings, bill maturities, household upkeep, visa expiries, reports due. The calendar is the main planning surface, and the hunter reads it too, because obligations predict location.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Every accepted commission, loan, lease or rendezvous places a dated entry. The player routes travel to satisfy them; conflicts are visible and resolved by renegotiation (trust loss), proxies (cut-outs, cover addresses) or default (debt and reputation consequences). The hunter's inference uses observable obligations — a bill maturing in Vienna, a registered lease — to predict presence, so reliability is the player's vulnerability. Breaking pattern costs money and trust.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Economy (bills, debts, rents); hunt (predictability as evidence); travel (the calendar is a routing problem); deception (proxies; false appointments as lures); politics (faction retainers demand dated reports).
- **Historical grounding:** Bills of exchange with fixed maturities; letters of credit; households in several cities; visa validity after 1914; club dues; telegraphed appointments; poste restante holding limits.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Commitment devices: people honour self-set deadlines far more than imposed ones, so every promise becomes a deadline the player owns. The planning fallacy becomes a learnable skill — veterans leave slack. Reliability versus predictability is a recurring meaningful choice, not a one-off. Dated obligations also create natural sessions: the player knows the 14th is the Vienna maturity and plays toward it — the goal-gradient effect at the scale of a week.
- **Engine implications:** Dated obligation entities with dependencies; conflict detection; the hunter sees only observable obligations; default cascades through debt and reputation. A priority queue.
- **Risks / open questions:** The calendar can become a spreadsheet; obligation types must be few and legible.

## P09.07 — The Commission Board
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Opportunities — commissions from services, firms and factions; trades; meetings — arrive as time-windowed offers tied to the world clock and made mutually exclusive by geography: accepting one in Baku forecloses one in Tangier purely by travel time.
- **How it plays (core loop):** The board fills from world state: an arms commission after a crisis, a concession when a railway reaches a province, a document job when a fleet sails. Each offer carries a window, location, payoff and trace cost. The player evaluates by timetable (can I get there?), hunt state (does it cross a cordon?) and politics (who is angered). Lapsed offers are reported with outcomes: taken by a rival, or evaporated as the crisis cooled.
- **Directive coverage:** Time pressure primary. Travel (feasibility is a timetable query); economy (payoffs, trades); politics (offers generated by macro-events); deception (the jobs); hunt (trace cost); items (deliverables with provenance).
- **Historical grounding:** Commission agents for Vickers, Krupp, Schneider and Škoda; concession brokering in Manchuria, Persia and Ottoman lands; Baku oil; naval plans as prize documents; the crisis calendar (Morocco 1905/1911, Bosnia 1908, Balkan Wars).
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Meaningful choice requires mutual exclusivity; here every acceptance is a visible refusal. Scarcity is honest, arising from geometry and the world clock, not timers. Anticipated regret sharpens each decision, and reported outcomes of lapsed offers deliver counterfactual feedback that fuels "next time". Because offers follow foreseeable macro-events, the board produces scheduled anticipation: Morocco is heating up, so offers will come.
- **Engine implications:** Offer generator reading world state; expiry queue; feasibility checks against timetable and cordons; outcome resolution for unaccepted offers. Lightweight, generated lazily per region.
- **Risks / open questions:** Must not degrade into a quest log — keep offers few and consequential.

## P09.08 — Cipher Hands
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Encoding and decoding are performed by the player with period methods — commercial codebooks, Vigenère, Playfair, columnar transposition, book cipher — taught incrementally. Skill lives in the player's head; in-world aids trade effort for cost and trace.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Messages arrive as ciphertext. The player chooses: decode by hand (free, private, slow in real time), use a codebook item (fast, but an incriminating artifact that can be found or stolen), or pay a commercial decoding service (fast, costs money, leaves a copy). Sending mirrors this. Mastery compounds: a player who knows Playfair reads a captured dispatch in minutes while the hunt advances by only minutes.
- **Directive coverage:** Coding primary. Items (codebooks with provenance); economy (clerk fees; commercial codes cut telegram costs); hunt (plaintext in luggage is evidence; cable copies are traces); politics (systems change with the war: ADFGVX 1918, one-time pads 1917–19); time (decoding consumes in-game hours).
- **Historical grounding:** Playfair in British field use; Vigenère and transposition; book ciphers; ABC and Bentley's commercial codes; Room 40; the Zimmermann telegram; ADFGVX; the one-time pad.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Competence, in self-determination theory, is the most durable motivator; a skill genuinely acquired rather than a stat raised produces flow and pride. The staircase from commercial code to Playfair to transposition is a mastery curve with visible payoff. The three-way choice keeps novice and expert engaged while making expertise strictly better for concealment. Decoding a captured message is a curiosity gap closed by effort — the most satisfying closure there is.
- **Engine implications:** Cipher routines as pure functions; message entities carrying system and key; key distribution as world events; a difficulty ramp and optional assist layer. Trivially scalable.
- **Risks / open questions:** Friction for some players; core progress must never be gated on hand-decoding — the paid route must always exist.

## P09.09 — Comfort or Cover
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** Every convenience of the age — first class, a Wagons-Lits berth, a grand hotel, a telegram, a letter of credit — is faster, healthier, better for standing, and more registered. Every anonymous alternative — third class, boarding house, gold coin, the post — is slower, harder on health and nearly traceless. One continuous dial, tuned daily.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Each purchase presents the dial implicitly: class, lodging, payment instrument, channel. Health is a resource worn down by cheap choices (fatigue, illness, the 1918 pandemic); standing — doors opened by apparent means — is worn down too. Trace density rises with expensive choices and is harvested at frontier posts and hotel registers. A wealthy legend travelling third class is itself an anomaly.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and travel primary. Economy (the price of anonymity); deception (legend consistent with mode); items (coin versus instruments); politics (wartime requisition removes first class; passport checks shift the dial).
- **Historical grounding:** Three classes of carriage; Wagons-Lits; hotel police registration; telegraph copies; name-bound credit instruments against gold coin; motor taxis c. 1905–10; the influenza pandemic; wartime requisition of rolling stock.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Approach-avoidance conflict is the engine of tension: the player wants the sleeper and fears the register. Because the dial is continuous and daily it yields micro-dilemmas at a rhythm that sustains attention without fatigue — not every choice is high-stakes, but all are consequential. Loss aversion operates on two fronts (health, cover), so no dominant strategy exists; the player's style emerges and becomes identity, which is exactly what a modelling hunter exploits (P09.10).
- **Engine implications:** Every transaction carries a cost vector (money, time, health, standing, trace type and density); frontier and register events harvest traces; a legend-consistency check. Cheap to scale.
- **Risks / open questions:** The dial must be visible without becoming a spreadsheet; health death spirals need a floor.

## P09.10 — The Dossier Mirror
- **Type:** game-defining system (risky)
- **Central mechanical hook:** The hunter keeps a dossier on the player built only from traces: habits (always first class, the same cable address, Thursday bank visits), known legends, associated institutions. The player can steal, buy or glimpse their own dossier and must decide which habits to break at efficiency cost.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Pursuit prediction is pattern-based, not omniscient: the hunter forecasts where a creature of habit will be. Periodically the player can read the dossier — intercept a circular, bribe a registry, burgle a bureau — as an abstract, honest list of inferred regularities with confidence levels. The player sees precisely which conveniences betray them. Breaking a pattern costs time, money or health (P09.09), and a sudden break is itself a detectable anomaly.
- **Directive coverage:** Hunt and deception primary — counter-surveillance as self-knowledge. Travel (routes as habits); economy (pattern-breaking costs); items (the dossier is a stealable artifact); politics (dossier-pooling depends on alliances: the 1907 Convention, Entente sharing in wartime).
- **Historical grounding:** Okhrana Paris bureau files; Special Branch; Bertillonage and fingerprinting (1901); mail perlustration; informant networks; inter-allied intelligence sharing; wanted notices.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Being modelled is uncanny and compelling: the game reflects the player's own behaviour back as evidence, producing self-recognition and genuine learning. It converts the hunt from "avoid the red dot" into "know thyself"; pattern-breaking is agency at its purest. The dossier also externalises the mastery curve — a veteran's dossier is thin. Risky because inference must be legible and never feel like cheating; brilliant if the hunter becomes a mirror rather than a monster.
- **Engine implications:** Per-service inference over the trace log (frequency, recency, co-occurrence); confidence scoring; dossier serialised as a readable artifact; an inter-service sharing graph. Inference at ticks, summaries cached.
- **Risks / open questions:** Inference quality; players seeding deliberate noise (acceptable if costly); readability of probabilistic output.

## P09.11 — Three Masters
- **Type:** game-defining system
- **Central mechanical hook:** The player may hold retainers from several services and firms at once. Each maintains a trust/suspicion state; each transaction with one is a liability with the others. The game is keeping several plausible accounts of yourself alive simultaneously.
- **How it plays (core loop):** Accept retainers from, say, two services and a trading house. Each demands exclusivity, periodic reports and occasional proofs of loyalty. Information sold to one can leak through the very channels the player exploits — telegraph copies, perlustration, informants — raising suspicion elsewhere. The player feeds each patron something true, something stale (P09.05) and something fabricated; patrons verify with lag. A crossed threshold turns a patron into a pursuer, feeding P09.02.
- **Directive coverage:** Deception and politics primary; alliance shifts (1907 Convention, 1914 alignments) make some combinations impossible overnight. Economy (stacked retainers); hunt (patrons become hunters); coding (reports in cipher); travel (patrons in different capitals); time (reports due on dates).
- **Historical grounding:** Freelance and semi-official agents serving several services; the Secret Service Bureau (1909), Deuxième Bureau, Evidenzbureau, Abteilung IIIb, Okhrana; arms-commission double-dealing; wartime censorship raising leak likelihood.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** Plate-spinning produces sustained cognitive engagement — the pleasurable load of keeping accounts consistent — punctuated by near-misses (a leak that almost crossed a threshold). Near-misses motivate strongly when the outcome is under the player's control, which separates this from gambling. Each added master raises income and tension together: a self-set difficulty slider. Betrayal becomes an emergent story generated by systems, and a patron turning hunter is a reversal the player authored.
- **Engine implications:** Per-faction belief state; information objects with provenance and per-channel leak probability; threshold events; an alliance graph with scheduled changes. Factions few; information objects lightweight.
- **Risks / open questions:** Legibility of suspicion; preventing "lie to everyone" — fabricated reports must be verifiable with delay.

## P09.12 — The Retirement Clause
- **Type:** whole-game concept (risky)
- **Central mechanical hook:** On any day the player may walk away: liquidate, choose a legend, pick a city, retire. The game ends and scores the life kept — wealth at that day's exchange, the chosen legend's cleanliness, outstanding debts, which services still hunt. The board always has one more job; the question is when to stop.
- **How it plays (core loop):** All other systems run. The retirement option is permanently visible with a live estimate of what would be kept: wealth in gold-standard terms (after 1914 exposed to exchange controls and collapse — retire on roubles in 1916 and watch it vanish), legend safety, debts, enemies. The player plays on for more and risks all. Death or capture scores far below any retirement. Retirements across careers (P09.04) build a record.
- **Directive coverage:** Economy primary (what you keep). Politics (safe havens change: not Russia after 1917); hunt (retirement holds only if the chosen legend is unknown); travel (reaching the city); time (the 1925 horizon; currency events); deception (the clean legend).
- **Historical grounding:** Gold-standard parities until 1914; wartime exchange controls; rouble collapse and expropriation from 1917; mark hyperinflation 1923; neutral havens; the model career of the period agent who never retired.
- **Why captivating & addictive:** The honest heart of "one more job": sunk cost and loss aversion pull the player to protect accumulated wealth while the escalating board pulls them to risk it. Making quitting-while-ahead a real, scored choice gives the game a genuinely meaningful endgame decision instead of a final confrontation. The live estimate turns every risky act into a visible bet on something the player owns — the endowment effect made explicit. Risky because players may retire early; mitigated by scoring legend quality and by the persistent world inviting a new agent.
- **Engine implications:** A continuous valuation function over assets, legends, debts and pursuit; a currency-event schedule; end-state generation. Cheap.
- **Risks / open questions:** Early retirement; scoring that rewards greed; the board must escalate honestly, not artificially.
