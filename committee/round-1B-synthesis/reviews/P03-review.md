# Round 1B-1 Review — Period Historian (1895–1925) (Panelist P03)

## Overall reading of the pool
The pool converges, independently and from every lens, on one architecture: the player is never observed, only recorded, and hunters reason over institutional ledgers that arrive with lag (P01.04, P02.05, P03.01, P04.05, P08.01, P08.02, P09.02, P10.10); a second convergence makes information travel at wire speed while bodies travel at timetable speed (P01.06, P02.02, P03.08, P04.06, P09.05); a third treats legends as maintained record-sets with upkeep and burn (P02.04, P03.10, P05.01, P06.07, P08.03, P09.03, P10.02). Those three clusters are the game, and they are historically true: this is the only era in which telegraph, register and passport regimes produced exactly this asymmetry. The strongest single hooks are the fairness architecture (dossier as filtered log, autopsy on capture: P02.05, P08.09), the three-tier bounded history that reconciles accuracy with agency (P07.02), and the multi-master belief models that are the touchstone career itself (P07.01, P05.09, P02.10, P09.11). What is thin: conversation as a system (only P05.03 and P05.08 go beyond ledgers), the period's calendars and clocks as a cross-cutting rule (P03.07 alone), and war as something observable rather than a backdrop (P03.09, P07.03, P07.04). Accuracy across the pool is high; I found precision issues, not howlers, listed below.

## Merge proposals (up to 5)
### M-P03-1 — The Ledger State: Records, Lags and the Readable Hunter
- **Members:** P08.01, P03.01, P04.05, P02.05
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** One append-only record store. Every act under a legend emits a typed record (register slip, berth sheet, passport stamp, bank counterfoil, cable copy, porter's memory) carrying jurisdiction × date parameters — coverage (Meldewesen in Germany, Austria, Russia and France; nothing in Britain before 1914–16), forwarding lag, fidelity, persistence and reader list — together with the travel designer's mode-specific marks. Each hunting service's dossier is simply a filtered view of that store (only records that reached it), so its deductions are derivable and auditable; the player sees an estimate of their own trail and may thin it, knowing that cleaning writes records too.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Four panelists described the same spine from four lenses; merged, it has a historical parameter table, an engine representation, travel-mode trace semantics and a fair hunter view in one specification.

### M-P03-2 — Faster Than the Wire: The Information Front
- **Members:** P02.02, P01.06, P03.08, P04.06
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** A communications graph (cable offices with hours and retained copies, Reuters/Havas/Wolff territories, edition times, liaison gates that need a treaty and a willing office) carries every payload — wanted descriptions, warrants, prices, headlines — at measured latency, while the player's body moves on the timetable graph. The player sees the estimated front of their own description, outruns it along low-latency corridors, slows it (bribed clerk, cut cable, closed office, missing liaison) or changes the thing described; bourses reprice when news arrives, so the latency that saves a life also pays for it.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** One propagation layer serves the hunt, the economy and the press, and it is the era's unique physics (no telephony across frontiers or oceans, agency cartels, the 1914 cable cuts).

### M-P03-3 — Papers: Regimes, Forgery and Inspection Depth
- **Members:** P05.06, P03.05, P04.04, P02.07
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** Every border edge carries a dated regime record (none / passport / consular visa / exit permit / photograph / currency control / gauge break); every document is a field-structured item (stock, seal, signature, serial, stamps, dates) with a quality per field and a provenance chain whose safety is live (a forger's arrest burns every paper from his hand once the news reaches a post); every inspector has a depth profile by institution and year. A crossing resolves field by field against regime and depth, with partial feedback, and appends stamps that must match the legend's itinerary.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Requirement, production and inspection were split across three panels; together they make a single crisp, dice-free test at every frontier that flips historically in 1914, 1915 and 1920.

### M-P03-4 — Jurisdiction as Terrain: Law, Liaison and Inherited Files
- **Members:** P08.04, P03.06, P07.06
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** Several independent hunters, each bounded by legal reach (extradition treaties, the political-offence exception, capitulations and consular courts until October 1914) and by liaison channels whose existence, delay and honesty follow the diplomatic calendar (Rome 1898, St Petersburg 1904, Entente 1904 and 1907, wartime Allied bureaux, the 1923 Vienna commission). Dossiers are copied along alliance edges with delay, inherited incompletely by successor regimes (1917) and lost in collapses; the passport the player presents decides which court tries them, so legend nationality is a legal shield and sanctuary is a dated, eroding resource. Private inquiry agencies ignore borders but run on small budgets.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** It adds the "which law applies" layer to the "who talks to whom" layer; both are historically dated and together give the hunt a readable, shifting geography.

### M-P03-5 — The Timetable of War: Crisis Ladders, Mobilisation and War Regimes
- **Members:** P07.03, P07.04, P03.09
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** Crises run as state machines with literal deadlines (the 48-hour and 12-hour ultimatums of July 1914), each rung tightening borders, panicking bourses and raising hunter powers, some leaving permanent ratchets (Official Secrets Act 1911, Aliens Restriction Act 1914). At general mobilisation the military schedule overwrites the civilian graph edge by edge; war then runs as a regime overlay with internal phases (1914 mobility, 1915–16 stasis, 1917 Russian collapse, 1918 fluidity) over travel, money, identity, censorship and hunting, with neutral hubs as bazaars. The player never fights; they read the war by sampling flows (train-composition counts sent through censored channels) and sell the readings on value curves.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** It makes "warfare" a process the player observes and trades through the same graph they travel on, rather than a separate wargame or a backdrop.

## Role-lens notes
Accuracy audit. The pool respects the prohibition (no named persons; firms and institutions only) and is unusually accurate; what follows are precision corrections. P04.01/P04.08: the Circum-Baikal line carried temporary traffic from autumn 1904 and regular service from 1905; the ice-rail crossing of February–March 1904 is right. P02.04/P07.04: British internment of German men of military age began selectively in August 1914 and became general after May 1915 — stage it. P10.02: British photographic passports date from February 1915, not 1914. P02.11/P08.12: wartime cable censors read current traffic; retrospective reading of retained copies should be a rare review event, not routine. P06.11: gold hoarding was criminal in Soviet Russia (1918 decrees) and restricted in Germany (1923), not in Britain or France — table it by jurisdiction. P07.06 and P09.02: see flags. Confirmations worth recording so nobody "corrects" them: the Official Secrets Act 1911 passed its Commons stages in under an hour on 18 August 1911 (P07.03); five German cables were cut on 5 August 1914 (P07.04, P03.02); the 1906 Serbian artillery order did help trigger the Austro-Serbian customs war (P07.02); Cook's circular notes date from 1874 (P06.02). One cross-cutting omission: only P03.07 handles calendars and clocks, yet every record in the ledger architecture is date-stamped — Russian registers are Julian until 31 January 1918, Russian railways run on Petersburg time, France keeps Paris time to 1911 — so the hunter's alibi arithmetic (P04.12, P08.05) must convert, and a forger's wrong calendar is a tell. I ask the Rapporteur to attach that rule to whichever ledger candidate survives.
