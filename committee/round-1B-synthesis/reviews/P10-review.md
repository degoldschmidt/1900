# Round 1B-1 Review — Life-Sim & Character Progression Designer (Panelist P10)

## Overall reading of the pool
Read whole, the pool has a centre of gravity: nine panelists independently made the cover identity the unit of account, so that heat, credit, debts, registrations, households and files attach to a name rather than to the player (P01.03, P02.04, P03.10, P04.12, P05.01, P06.07, P08.03, P09.03, P10.02), and this is the correct chassis for a life sim because it makes failure granular. Around it sit four further convergences: hunters who read only institutional ledgers at institutional latency (P02.05, P03.01, P04.05, P08.01, P08.02, P09.09); information at cable speed against bodies at train speed (P01.06, P02.02, P03.08, P04.06, P09.05); several paymasters with belief models plus commerce as the engine of espionage (P01.07, P02.10, P05.09, P07.01, P09.11; P03.12, P06.01, P06.05, P06.06, P07.10); and a discrete-event world beating at several cadences, departures, reveals, maturities and the macro-calendar (P01.01, P01.09, P02.01, P04.02, P09.01, P09.02, P10.11). The strongest hooks are honest ones: endowment over legends the player built, forced reveals traded for comfort, the past arriving by archive mechanism (P02.11, P08.12), and retirement or succession as a scored choice (P09.04, P09.12, P10.12). What is thin relative to directive 2 is the person inside the portfolio: ageing, health, injury, skills and vices appear only in P10 and as asides in P08.02, P08.12 and P09.09; dependants appear mostly as cost lines rather than as leak sources; and the pool carries an unresolved tension between mastery in the player's head (P09.04, P09.08) and progression in the character. Housekeeping for synthesis: two pairs share titles (P05.09 and P06.01 "The Double Ledger"; P06.06 and P07.10 "The Concession Hunter") and should be renamed or merged.

## Merge proposals (up to 5)
### M-P10-1 — Several Lives: The Legend Portfolio
- **Members:** P02.04, P10.02, P08.03, P06.07
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** The unit of account is the legend, not the player. Each legend is an entity owning a fact-graph with evidentiary weights that decay unless fed (P10.02), a residence, bank accounts, registrations, mail, debts and obligations in a city that keep running whether or not the player wears it (P02.04), a social class with a monthly burn and live or dormant states (P06.07), and its own heat column in each hunting service's files (P08.03). Unattended legends go stale: rent unpaid, mail unanswered, registration lapsed. The player plans circuits by timetable to feed them, delegates upkeep at leak risk, and watches a linkage-risk indicator for any action that could tie two names together (a trunk under two names, one hand in two registers, an endorsed draft). Burning a legend discards its heat and also its credit, contracts, households and friendships; retiring one cleanly is slow and expensive. The cluster also absorbs P03.10 (institutional record-sets; Britain invents, Germany audits), P05.01 (verification clocks), P09.03 (burn to collapse a fix; sell a clean legend), P04.12 (timetable alibis), P01.03 (credit per institution) and P10.03 (households as the legend's physical anchor and leak source).
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** The four parts are one entity seen from the life, the deception, the economy and the hunt; merged, every other system (debts, traces, circles, routine) has a single object to attach to, and failure becomes granular: lose a name, not the game.

### M-P10-2 — The Body Keeps the Books: Comfort, Cover and the Cost of Both
- **Members:** P10.07, P09.09, P08.02, P10.06
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** One dial runs from comfort to cover (P09.09): first class, a berth, a grand hotel and a letter of credit are fast, healthy, good for standing and registered; third class, a boarding house and gold coin are slow, traceless and corrosive. The period's forced reveals (register, frontier, bank, cable; P08.02) harvest the visible end of the dial; the body pays for the quiet end. Health is several stocks with timers (P10.07): fatigue, a city's seasonal disease exposure, wounds that heal in weeks without antibiotics, chronic conditions, and age lowering every ceiling. Hardship accumulates as life-sim debt (illness, missed appointments, a shabby appearance that itself draws police attention); medicine is slow, expensive and leaves records; a sanatorium season hides and registers you at once. Vices (P10.06) are the pressure valves: drink, cards and morphine deliver real short-term benefits and compounding costs in health, debt, blackmail and predictability.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** "Pay in suffering for silence" needs a body that can suffer, and a health model needs a reason to be touched daily; together they give the thirty years a physical arc (the agent of 1900 can sleep rough, the agent of 1920 cannot) instead of a change of rules only.

### M-P10-3 — Routine Is Evidence: The Calendar the Hunter Reads
- **Members:** P09.10, P10.10, P09.06, P04.09
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** Every dated promise (bill maturities, rent, reports due, club nights, school terms, visa expiries, a weekly letter to a cover address; P09.06) sits in one scheduler with operations and travel (P10.10), and each kept appointment writes a witness record. Hunters infer habits from those records (always first class, the same cable address, Thursday bank visits; P09.10) and score itineraries against the movement pattern a cover implies (P04.09): a commercial traveller who never visits manufacturers is implausible, and a name that dines at the same club every Thursday is catchable. The player can read the hunter's inferred regularities by stealing or buying the dossier, then chooses which habits to break at a cost in plausibility, standing or money; a sudden break is itself an anomaly, and false routine can be laid as a trap.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Four lenses describe one dilemma, reliability versus predictability; merged, life-sim upkeep becomes a stealth decision and the hunter acquires a mind that reads the life rather than a map.

### M-P10-4 — Everyone You Owe Is a Hunter
- **Members:** P06.08, P10.09, P01.05, P10.08
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** Idleness costs money (P01.05): legends, households, retainers and sub-agents burn daily while income is lumpy, so the player borrows. Every borrowing is a dated instrument with a jurisdictional enforcement regime (P10.09): bills protest publicly, a gazetted bankruptcy prints a legend's name, a service's advance converts into coercive tasking, and currency regimes rewrite debts in 1914, 1917 and 1923. Creditors pursue in parallel with counter-intelligence (P06.08): patience timers, information shared by letter or cable, bailiffs and inquiry agents; fleeing a jurisdiction buys time, not freedom, and debts die with a burned identity, tempting fraud. Dependants, pensioners and patrons (P10.08) are obligation lines with their own calendars whose missed demands convert into leverage (letters, denunciations) that hunters can pull. Insolvency is a playable reset, never game over.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Four panelists found the same second clock; merged, money becomes a pursuer with a legible countdown, and the moral weight of a life (who depends on you, whom you abandon) emerges from the ledger without a script.

### M-P10-5 — Thirty Winters: One World, Successive Agents, Chosen Exits
- **Members:** P10.01, P09.04, P10.12, P09.12
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** One persistent world runs 1895–1925 whether or not the current agent survives (P09.04). The agent ages across three unaligned clocks, world, life and hunt (P10.01); early years are forgiving, late years are the hunt. On any day the player may retire: liquidate, choose a clean legend and a non-extraditing haven, and score the life kept at that day's exchange rate (P09.12); an exit dashboard shows how near each ending is: retirement, exile, ruin, capture, death, turning (P10.12). Capture and ruin are survivable states (interrogation, sentence, bankruptcy) that age the agent and advance the clock; death or a chosen succession hands play to a system-generated successor who inherits a configurable share of assets, every debt and household, and a hunters' file that now has two subjects, while routes, codes and crisis knowledge persist only in the player's head. The trail autopsy (P08.09) and the inquest against the default timeline (P02.03) supply systems-generated closure.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** It resolves the permadeath versus sunk-cost conflict and the "when to stop" problem in one structure, gives the thirty years a destination without a story, and lets the hunt bite hard because losing an agent is not losing the campaign.

## Role-lens notes
Audit of THE LIFE (directive 2). Whole-person systems exist in the pool, but they model the life as a portfolio of names and ledgers: P02.04, P06.07, P08.03, P09.03 and P03.10 (legends that run, cost and burn); P01.05, P06.08 and P09.06 (burn rate, creditors, dated promises); P09.04, P09.12, P08.09 and P08.12 (succession, retirement, sentences that age the character, archives that outlive him); P07.04 and P06.11 (the macro-world seizing households and fortunes). These are mutually compatible because they reduce to one primitive, identity-scoped, dated, jurisdiction-tagged records, and M-P10-1, 3, 4 and 5 say how they interlock. What the pool lacks is the body and the competences: outside P10.05, P10.06 and P10.07, health is a resource worn down by cheap travel (P08.02, P09.09) with no recovery, medicine or ageing model; nobody's skills change in thirty years; and the only vices belong to recruitable NPCs (P05.08). Without a body and rusting skills, 1895 and 1925 differ only in world rules, and the touchstone's arc from young adventurer to hunted veteran has no physical substrate, so the committee should carry at least one body-and-ageing candidate (M-P10-2). The mission-loop risk comes from the job generators, P01.02, P02.12, P09.07 and P02.06: excellent content engines that, if they become the frame, turn the life into a quest log; they should sit beneath the portfolio so that jobs are taken to pay rent, not rent paid to take jobs. P03.11 (a four-year scenario) and P08.11 (alternating roles) are strong slices, not lives. The mastery tension, player's head (P09.04, P09.08) versus character skill (P10.05), is resolvable with P05.05's abstraction ladder: hand-solving is the player's skill, while the delegated and abstract rungs run on the character's rusting, ageing skill.
