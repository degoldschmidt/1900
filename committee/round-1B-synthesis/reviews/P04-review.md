# Round 1B-1 Review — Travel & Logistics Designer (Panelist P04)

## Overall reading of the pool
The pool converges hard on five hooks: bodies move at train speed while facts move at wire speed (P01.06, P02.02, P03.08, P04.06, P05.04, P09.05); hunters read only institutional ledgers (P01.04, P02.05, P03.01, P04.05, P08.01, P08.02, P09.09); hunters and cargo ride the same timetable (P02.08, P04.06, P06.12, P08.05, P08.07); the timetable is the turn or session structure (P01.01, P02.01, P04.02, P09.01); and cover identities are maintained assets held apart by the timetable (P02.04, P04.12, P05.01, P08.03, P09.03, P10.02). That convergence is the good news: the engine is one time-expanded transport graph plus one communications graph, which P02 has already specified. The strongest whole-game frames are P01.01, P02.02, P08.02 and the concession/commission family (P03.12, P06.05, P06.06, P07.10); the strongest risky ideas are P03.09, P08.10 and P07.08. What is thin relative to Directive 3 is the physical texture of travel itself (modes other than rail, luggage, delay, season, long legs) and conversation, where only P05.03 proposes an actual conversation game. Nothing in the pool violates the prohibition outright; the only Directive 1 tension is P02.03's movable macro-dates.

## Merge proposals (up to 5)
### M-P04-1 — The Departure Is the Turn
- **Members:** P01.01, P09.01, P04.02, P02.01
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** The world is a discrete-event simulation whose visible face is the timetable (P02.01). The player's decision points are departures: every action (meet, cable, bank, forge, trade) is placed in the slack between timetabled trains, steamers and mail collections (P01.01). The atomic tension unit is the connection, where dwell time at a junction is simultaneously money spent, a hotel register signed and a hunter's telegram catching up (P04.02). Session structure follows: every pause the game offers sits a few hours before a departure the player can already see, so sessions end aboard trains and the leg resolves deterministically on next load (P09.01). Slack management, buffer planning and the choice of night trains are the mastery curve; the same priority queue schedules the hunters, the markets and the macro-events.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Four panelists independently made the timetable the clock; merged, the engine primitive (event queue), the core loop (departures as turns), the tension unit (the connection) and the retention device (the next departure) become one legible frame rather than four overlapping rules.

### M-P04-2 — Hunters on the Same Bradshaw
- **Members:** P02.08, P08.05, P08.07, P08.01
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** The player is never observed directly; every act writes a record into an institutional ledger with place, time, identity, fidelity, persistence and access (P08.01; P04.05 and P03.01 supply the mode-specific trace table: named berth, third-class ticket, hotel form, passenger list, cable copy). Each hunting service maintains a bounded set of weighted hypotheses about identity, place and mode, propagated along the real time-expanded graph and reweighted only by records that have reached it (P08.05). Hunter agents are physically on the same timetable: they cannot teleport, they wire ahead and board the next departure, and they can only post watchers at chokepoints they can reach in time (P02.08). When weight collapses onto a city, a cordon forms over its real exit set with per-exit coverage curves, main termini first, river piers and suburban halts last, and the escape window is computed against the timetable (P08.07). The player exploits night trains that outrun the morning dispatch, mode changes that split the hypothesis set, and chokepoints the hunters cannot reach.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** One graph serves player routing, hunter routing, reachability and cordon planning, so fairness, legibility and cheap computation come together; separately, P08.01 is a data model without a mind, P08.05 a mind without a body, and P08.07 a climax without a trail.

### M-P04-3 — The Known World: Guides, Clocks and Frontier Regimes
- **Members:** P04.01, P03.07, P03.05, P04.04
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** The network is a date-versioned master graph (lines opening, services suspended, fronts cutting edges), but the player sees only the subgraph they know, learned from owned, dated guides (Bradshaw's, Cook's, the Reichskursbuch, the Russian indicator), station posters and conversation, each edge stamped with its source and edition (P04.01, P03.07). Stale knowledge fails at run time as ghost connections. Displayed times pass through local clocks and calendars (Petersburg railway time, the Julian calendar to 1918, Ottoman sunset reckoning), so a forgery with the wrong calendar is a tell (P03.07). Every border-crossing edge carries a dated regime record (none, passport, visa, exit permit, photograph, customs intensity, currency control, gauge break, quarantine) that flips with the world clock, with August 1914 as the great flip and 1920 as standardisation (P03.05, P04.04). The frontier station, where gauge breaks force a change of train into a hall of passport desks, customs, money changers and a telegraph office, is the set-piece node where knowledge, papers and the hunt collide.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Known-route overlays and regime layers are the same data structure, edge attributes with validity windows, so one authoring model yields both the discovery game and the border game; the Historian's precision supplies the tables, the travel design supplies the play.

### M-P04-4 — Things Travel Too
- **Members:** P06.12, P04.03, P04.11, P06.10
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** Everything that moves is an entity with its own itinerary on the same graph: hand luggage with the player, registered luggage in the van or sent ahead by slow freight, crates under bills of lading on grande or petite vitesse schedules, consignments and couriers dispatched by the player, and letters riding the ambulant post (P04.03, P06.12, P04.11). Each frontier and port applies an examination probability keyed on edge, date, class, papers, manifest plausibility and bribes; left-luggage offices are caches and dead drops with receipts as tradable keys; labels accumulate on trunks as readable history. The 80 Days trade loop runs on top: city price vectors shaped by tariff walls, season and crisis, bounded by weight and customs risk, with news arriving at cable speed so an intercepted telegram is a trading edge (P06.10). The hunter can pursue the crate as a second fugitive and follow it to the consignee, who may be one of the player's legends.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** It satisfies the items and economy directives with the travel engine already built, doubles the hidden-movement puzzle without new AI, and lets the player choose between carrying, consigning and dispatching, which is a richer decision than any one of the parts offers alone.

### M-P04-5 — Your Itinerary Is Your Identity
- **Members:** P04.09, P10.10, P09.10, P04.12
- **Merged concept (one paragraph):** The hunter mines the player's movement log three ways, and the player manages all three with the same timetable. Plausibility: each cover identity implies an itinerary template (a commercial traveller's weekday towns in second class, a tourist's seasonal circuit, an engineer's railheads), and legs out of pattern raise examination odds and matching speed (P04.09). Routine: kept appointments, remittance days and club nights are periodic witness records that make a legend credible and an agent ambushable (P10.10), and the hunter's dossier of habits can be stolen or glimpsed so the player sees which conveniences betray them (P09.10). Alibi: when two legends' presence records are compared, the fastest timetable connection between sightings decides whether they can be one body (P04.12). Repairs cost time and money: business-like legs that can be made genuinely profitable, deliberate pattern breaks, or a connection the hunters do not know exists.
- **Why the merge is stronger than the parts:** Separately these are three meters; together they form one dilemma, that what makes a legend plausible (regular, in-pattern, reachable) is exactly what makes the body catchable, and that dilemma is renewed by every leg the player books.

## Role-lens notes
Travel-depth audit. Travel is treated as a real schedule, network and knowledge problem that spends time, money and traces in P01.01, P01.04, P02.01, P02.02, P02.06, P02.08, P03.05, P03.07, P03.09, P03.11, P05.04, P06.10, P06.12, P08.02, P08.05, P08.07, P09.01, P09.09 and P10.10, and throughout P04. These agree on the data model: a time-expanded graph with dated edge attributes, routed by the player, the hunters and the engine's own planner (P02.06 validates operations against it). Travel is reduced to a precondition ("being in the right capital", "which edition you can buy") in most of P05, in P06.03 to P06.09, P07.05 to P07.12, P09.05, P09.08 and P10.04 to P10.07; acceptable for sub-systems, but every final candidate should state which departure its verbs cost. Gaps nobody but P04 fills: non-rail modes with their own physics (steamers and ice seasons, river boats, motor, Zeppelin, 1919 air), luggage weight and customs as inventory, delays and strikes as a forecastable layer (P04.08, which the scheduled war disruptions of P03.09, P07.03 and P07.04 should absorb), and long legs as bounded sessions (P04.10). Two cautions. First, several proposals assume a night train outruns the wire; that is only true because telegraph offices close, queue and need a human to act, so the communications graph must carry office hours and liaison delays or the race is unwinnable. Second, hunters must use noisy rather than exact reachability (P02.08 says so), or Bradshaw's becomes a cage instead of a board. Errata compliance for my own file: P04.01 and P04.08 should read Circum-Baikal 1904; my hotel-registration lists already exclude Britain; P04.04 already describes the gauge break as a change of train.
