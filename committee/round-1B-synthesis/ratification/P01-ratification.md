## Amendments (≤3; only if AMEND)
### A-P01-1 — correct — C03
- **Change:** In C03's Historical grounding, replace "Extradition Act 1870, no Anglo-Russian treaty" with "Extradition Act 1870 (political-offence exception, s.3) and the Anglo-Russian Treaty for the Mutual Surrender of Fugitive Criminals, signed in London 24 November 1886". In How it plays, replace "London is sanctuary until wartime alien registration" with "London is sanctuary for offences that can be characterised as political, until wartime alien registration; ordinary crimes are extraditable under the 1886 treaty, so how a service frames your offence decides its reach". Note the same correction against P03.06's source line.
- **Reason:** Factual error. Britain and Russia did have an extradition treaty (signed London, 24 November 1886; listed among pre-1917 British treaties in the Commons debate of 25 February 1924). The pre-war sanctuary rested on the 1870 Act's political-offence exception, not on the absence of a treaty. The correction keeps the mechanic and sharpens C03's hook — jurisdiction as terrain turns on the characterisation of the offence, a lever for the hunter (frame a theft as ordinary crime) and for the player (be a political offender) — and it corrects a fact, so it should carry on its own.

### A-P01-2 — clarify — C16
- **Change:** In C16's One-line pitch and Central hook, state that the four windows are the only readings of hunter *inference* — what the services have concluded. The player's estimate of their own *trail* (C17's exposure map, C02's exposure forecast beside the fare, C04's linkage-risk indicator) is computed from the player's own records, is always available, and says nothing about which records have been read or what was inferred. Add one engine line to C17: "the player's trail view is a filter over their own records; dossiers (what reached whom) and inference (what was concluded) are shown only through C16's windows."
- **Reason:** As drafted, C16 says "you never see the hunters' belief directly, only through four channels" while C17, C02 and C04 give the player exposure maps, forecasts and indicators — the twenty read as contradicting one another, and C16's hook blurs into C17's. Separating "what I left" (own records, visible) from "what they inferred" (four windows) makes the two candidates distinct by hook, honours P08.05's own requirement (convey the model without exposing it) and P01.09's rule (the reveal clock is inferred, never displayed), and is what keeps C02's thirty-second decision legible without a minimap.

### A-P01-3 — clarify — C04
- **Change:** Add to C04's Central hook the timetable-incompatibility test as the canonical linkage rule: "two names whose presence records (register, bank drawing, witness) could have been produced by one body on the real timetable are a link candidate; records that no connection could reconcile keep the names apart, so the player plans circuits that make lives provably incompatible." List P04.12 under C04's "draws on". P04.12 stays a member of C14 for habit mining; no provenance moves and no tally changes.
- **Reason:** C04's hook is "the hunt advances only by linking two names", yet its listed link types (witness sees both faces, same hand in two registers, endorsed draft, same trunk, photograph match) omit the one link the timetable makes decidable — the mechanic stated by P04.12 and by P05.01 ("the timetable is an alibi constraint", a member text of M-P05-1 that the merge lost). Homed only in C14 it reads as a routine mechanic, where it is mis-placed. Without it C04 loses its travel coupling (directive 3, travel ↔ deception) and C04 and C14 blur; with it they are distinct: C04 is the linkage of names, C14 the mining of habits.
