## Amendments (≤3; only if AMEND)
### A-P03-1 — correct — C06
- **Change:** In C06's Historical grounding replace "British photographic passports compulsory November 1915" with "British passports carry photographs from February 1915 and are compulsory for everyone entering or leaving the United Kingdom from November 1915; Germany imposed passports by Bundesrat decree of 31 July 1914, France in the first days of August 1914". Keep the "1914, 1915, 1920 flips" note in Directive coverage.
- **Reason:** Factual error. The draft conflates two distinct British measures: the photograph on the passport (1 February 1915, under the British Nationality and Status of Aliens Act 1914) and compulsory passports for travel (Defence of the Realm order, November 1915). C04 already carries February 1915; C06's edge-requirement table is the one that will be built from these dates, so it must carry both flips correctly.

### A-P03-2 — clarify — C10
- **Change:** Add P07.07 (Doomed Projects) to C10 as a member mechanic, also drawn on by C09: "Political ventures — coups, counter-revolutions, separatist risings, concession wars — are Tier-1 project objects with stages, resource gates (funds, arms, officers' loyalty, foreign backing, timing), hidden feasibility read only through intelligence quality, and a collapse trigger that spawns a pursuit state under the new regime; ventures history forbids are Tier-0 doomed and are scored on extraction (money, people and reputation got out before the collapse), never on victory." Record P07.07 in C10's provenance.
- **Reason:** A nominated proposal wrongly dropped (the Rapporteur names it a natural amendment target). The Charter's Ace of Spies touchstone lists "politics (coups, counter-revolution)" as a pillar of the career, and P07.07 is the only proposal that makes it a system; C10's tier model already supplies the immovable rail and the saturation that makes extraction scoring honest (P09's condition). Grounding is sound: Allied-mission conspiracies of 1918, émigré counter-revolution to 1925, the 1912 United States arms embargo on Mexico.

### A-P03-3 — clarify — C05
- **Change:** Add P08.11 (Poacher and Gamekeeper) to C05 as a mode: "Between fugitive stretches the player may take counter-espionage or inquiry commissions and run the hunter's board — delayed ledger extracts, watchers allocated to termini, piers and frontier posts, cables to foreign police who may refuse, hypotheses pruned with Bradshaw's, the cordon sprung early and leaky or late and expensive — against an AI quarry running the same belief engine; hunting writes the player's own records (expense claims, cables)." Record P08.11 in C05's provenance.
- **Reason:** A nominated proposal wrongly dropped. By P08.11's own engine note both roles share one simulation, so the cost is small, and playing the hunter teaches the hunter's mind, which is the panel's stated condition for forty hours of pursuit feeling fair and legible. Bounded as a mode rather than a standalone life, it answers P01's and P10's objection. Grounding is sound: freelance agents served services in sequence; private inquiry agencies took pursuit contracts; police circulars and telegraphed descriptions were the working tools.
