## Amendments (≤3; only if AMEND)
### A-P07-1 — merge — C10
- **Change:** Add P07.07 (Doomed Projects) to C10's members (provenance "P07.02 (lead), P02.03, P01.10, P07.08, P07.07") and give C10 the project verb it lacks. In "How it plays" add: "Rarely, at the largest scale: join or sponsor a political venture — a coup, a counter-revolution, a rising, a concession war — modelled as a staged project with resource gates and a hidden feasibility read only through intelligence. Ventures whose success history forbids are Tier-0 doomed; they are still playable because the game scores extraction explicitly — money, people and reputation got out before the collapse — and the collapse itself spawns the pursuit state (C05) under the successor regime's service." In "Engine implications" add: "project templates as stage graphs; feasibility hidden behind intelligence quality; collapse triggers spawning pursuit states and city regime changes; the inquest reports each venture's Tier 1–2 footprint." In "Why captivating" add: "commitment escalation redeemed by mastery of exit timing; the touchstone's late arc generated by systems, not scripts."
- **Reason:** A nominated proposal wrongly dropped: the Rapporteur's own "nominated but not carried" note names P07.07 as fitting beneath C10/C09 as a Tier-1 project type and as a natural amendment target. C10 is the one candidate whose player verbs are thin ("read the papers; operate where gradients pay; rarely influence"); Doomed Projects is the player-facing proof of its central promise — you can join the counter-revolution and cannot make it win — and it supplies the Reilly-touchstone arc (directive 2) as a system. P09's condition (extraction scoring must be explicit) is written into the change.

### A-P07-2 — clarify — C09
- **Change:** In C09's "Central hook" or "Engine implications", state the tier rule explicitly: "Crisis rung dates and rung outcomes (who backs down, whether a ladder ends in war), mobilisation dates, belligerents' entries and armistices are Tier 0 with zero elasticity, as in C10. 'Tier 1 jitter' applies only to local parameters — the hour a given frontier post closes, requisition fractions by line, the size of a service's emergency fund, which stations are watched, how long a strike holds a junction. The lesser wars (Boer, Russo-Japanese, Italo-Turkish, Balkan) run on the same fixed calendar."
- **Reason:** Directive 1 protection. Of the seven candidates where the player touches politics (C01, C03, C09, C10, C13, C15, C18), C09 is the only one whose text names "Tier 1 jitter" on the ladders without saying which parts of a crisis are Tier 0; read alone, an implementer could jitter the ultimatum of 23 July 1914 by a week. C15 and C18 carry the explicit convergence/exogeneity sentence; C09 should too, so no candidate can move a Tier-0 date.

### A-P07-3 — clarify — C01
- **Change:** Restore the budget loop from P07.01 and M-P07-2. In "How it plays" (campaign) add: "Each service's annual secret-service vote rises with the successes credited to it and funds both the prices it pays for requirements and its counter-intelligence capacity, so the player's deliveries pay for the hunters who will one day read him." In "Engine implications" change "budget" to "a yearly budget that is a Tier 1 output of credited Tier 2 outcomes, scaling task value and hunter capacity (C05 watcher and cable budgets)."
- **Reason:** A mechanic the merge watered down: the C01 text keeps "budget" as a static faction attribute, but the lead proposal's loop (success → budget → counter-intelligence capacity → pursuit of the player) is the clearest directive-5 dependency chain in the pool — one agent's actions propagating into an institution's capacity and back into his own life — and it is the honest, self-balancing form of P09.11's "each added master is a self-set difficulty slider", which C01 cites in its retention argument without its mechanism.
