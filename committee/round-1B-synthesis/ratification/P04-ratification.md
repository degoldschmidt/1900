### A-P04-1 — merge — C07
- **Change:** Move P04.01 and P03.07 from C07's "draws on, by breadth adjustment" into its member provenance (support unchanged at 4; P04 is already counted) and write the mechanic into the candidate. Central hook, append: "The board you plan on is the subgraph you know: each edge is learned from an owned, dated guide, a station poster or a porter, clerk or fellow passenger, carries source, edition date and confidence, and goes stale as the real network changes." How it plays, append: "Campaign: the master graph is date-versioned and multi-modal: tunnels and lines open (Simplon 1906, Lötschberg 1913, Amur 1916), services are suspended or rerouted (Orient Express 1914, Balkanzug 1916–18, Simplon-Orient 1919), motor cabs (c. 1905–10), DELAG airships (1910–14) and scheduled airlines (1919 London–Paris) appear, so knowledge must be re-bought and corridor knowledge is saleable; hunters hold their own known graph, so a connection they do not know is your escape; long legs (a liner, the Trans-Siberian) are sealed sessions in which the world moves and news arrives only at ports or by wireless (P04.10)." - **Reason:** Directive 3 names Bradshaw's and 80 Days, whose depth is route discovery on a dense real network, and the Rapporteur's own breadth note records that no candidate carried routes-as-knowledge. The attachment kept one clause and lost four mechanics the sources had: social acquisition of routes (which binds conversation to travel), the date-versioned multi-modal network as thirty-year progression, the hunter-side knowledge asymmetry that feeds C05, and sealed long legs. Membership rather than cross-reference guarantees the mechanic reaches the final pitch; no ranking changes.

### A-P04-2 — clarify — C09
- **Change:** Add P04.08 to C09's members (support unchanged; P04 is already counted) and write the disruption layer and the war graph into the candidate. Central hook, append: "War is the extreme case of a disruption layer that runs every year: deterministic seasonal closures (Baltic ice shutting the Petersburg approach while Libau stays open; Lake Baikal crossed by icebreaker ferry, by rails laid on the ice in winter 1904, then by the Circum-Baikal line from 1904–05; Alpine passes), strikes on the world clock (Russia October 1905, France 1910, Britain 1911) and seeded accidents, all forecastable at a price through editions, agents' cables and insiders, so buffer time is insurance and information reduces the buffer." How it plays, append: "Inside the war the network is different, not merely closed: North Sea crossings under mine and submarine risk; the single Haparanda–Torneå land route to Russia (change of train and gauge, passport check, sledge or carriage between the towns until the 1919 bridge); the electrified Belgian–Dutch frontier from 1915; blockade zones, navicerts and neutral transshipment. Disruptions strand hunters too and make crowds to hide in." Engine implications, append: "the disruption layer is scheduled plus seeded-random edge modifiers shared by all agents; forecasts are events with lead time and accuracy."
- **Reason:** The Charter's grounding kit lists seasonal ice closures, strikes and wartime requisition as travel infrastructure; P04.08 (drawn on) and P03.11 (member, carried as the "vertical slice") held that depth, and the merged text keeps only "the timetable collapses". Without a forecastable layer the connection tension in C07 is bare randomness, which P04.02, P02.01 and P09's audit all warn against, and Directive 4's independently moving world loses its most frequent, legible expression.
