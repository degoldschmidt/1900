# Historian's audit — C01 Several Masters, One Truth

## Verdict
Accurate; one dating imprecision in the worked example and three precision corrections.

## Corrections
1. **A worked example of play** — "Spring 1913, between the two Balkan Wars" → the First Balkan War ended with the Treaty of London on 30 May 1913 and the Second began on 29 June; spring 1913 is the First War's last phase → write "June 1913, between the wars"; the deployment tables' value curve depends on which war is running.
2. **A worked example of play** — "a hunter with Aliens Restriction Act powers takes the file" → the Aliens Restriction Act (5 August 1914) reached aliens only; against a British-papered legend the new powers are the Defence of the Realm Act (8 August 1914) and, from November 1915, compulsory passports → which Act bites depends on the legend's nationality, which the pitch elsewhere makes decisive.
3. **A worked example of play** — "the Belgian firm's books are sequestrated in occupied Brussels" (dated August 1914) → Brussels was occupied on 20 August 1914; German supervision and sequestration of enemy-owned firms followed under occupation ordinances over the autumn → "from the autumn of 1914".
4. **Core loop** — "1923 networks Europe's police" → the International Criminal Police Commission (Vienna, September 1923) exchanged circulars between member forces; it was a correspondence network, not a shared file → model it as liaison edges with delay, not a merged store.

## Notes
The institutional calendar is right: Admiralty and War Office buyers before October 1909, then the Secret Service Bureau's Home and Foreign sections; the Entente Cordiale of 8 April 1904 and the Convention of 31 August 1907; the Okhrana's March 1917 abolition with files partly burned; the unnamed May 1913 supersession. One affordance for the budget loop: the real Secret Service Vote was a fixed parliamentary sum (about £50,000 a year before the war; the Bureau began on roughly £7,000), freelancers were paid by results against audited vouchers, Abteilung IIIb paid through Dutch intermediaries and postal orders, and the Okhrana in cash from Paris — payment channels are records with readers.
