# Historian's audit — C04 The Legend Portfolio: Several Lives at Once

## Verdict
Accurate; four precision corrections, all on German dates and terms.

## Corrections
1. **Historical grounding** — "German and French passports in the first days of August 1914" → Germany's Bundesrat decree reintroducing passports is dated 31 July 1914; France followed in the first days of August → the German edges flip a few days before the French ones.
2. **A worked example of play** — "a Meldezettel at the Berlin Polizeipräsidium" → the Meldezettel is Vienna's form; in Berlin a resident filed a polizeiliche Anmeldung at the precinct, indexed at the Einwohnermeldeamt, and an Abmeldung on leaving → the Prussian term matters because deregistration is itself a record a cleanly retired legend must write.
3. **A worked example of play** — "From August, B's nationality makes its flat and account enemy property under a custodian and its wearer liable to internment" → German payment bans and compulsory administration of enemy property came by ordinances from the autumn of 1914, and British civilian men were interned at Ruhleben from November 1914 → "from the autumn of 1914", as the pitch's own grounding paragraph states.
4. **Historical grounding** — "1917 expropriates every Russian household and account" → bank nationalisation and safe-deposit seizures came in December 1917 and January 1918, housing requisitions through 1918 → "1917–18".

## Notes
The invention asymmetry (Britain until 1915, a foreign name only until August 1914; Germany where inventions die), the Hook of Holland day, letters of indication, the lax pre-1915 British passport and staged internment are all exact. Affordances not yet used: the Abmeldung, so that a legend that merely vanishes from a German city leaves a police query while one that deregisters leaves a forwarding address; Berlin's quarterly moving days (1 April and 1 October) as the rent and removal calendar; and club candidate books with proposers and seconders, a backstop record a hunter's inquiry agent could read.
