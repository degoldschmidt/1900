# Historian's audit — C13 Faster Than the Wire: The Information Front

## Verdict
Accurate; one substantive clarification (private telegrams crossed frontiers without liaison) and two precision corrections.

## Corrections
1. **Systems: Travel** — "a wire needs open offices at both ends and, across a frontier, a liaison agreement" → only police and official wires needed liaison; private telegrams crossed every frontier under the International Telegraph Convention (code words under the 1903 London regulations, censorship from 1914) → the player's own cables are gated by offices, tariffs and censors, never by C03's matrix, which gates the hunters' wires.
2. **Historical grounding** — "in 1910 a wanted notice overtook a transatlantic liner for the first time" → the celebrated 1910 case ran the other way: the ship's master recognised passengers from press descriptions and wirelessed ashore, and the pursuer crossed on a faster liner to board at Quebec → model it as a reader at sea querying the shore plus a body racing by timetable.
3. **A worked example of play** — "an Ottoman circular reaches the Bulgarian frontier post by wire within two hours" → specify the Ottoman post on the Bulgarian frontier (Mustafa Pasha, beyond Adrianople); Bulgaria, independent since 5 October 1908, would be sent nothing, as the example says; and state that Constantinople's clocks ran an hour ahead of Vienna's → the 11:00 Vienna arrival is local time.

## Notes
The latency table is right where it matters: All Red Line 1902, a shilling a word, Bentley's 1906, the Reuters–Havas–Wolff ring, the November 1905 postal-telegraph strike, the 5 August 1914 cable cuts, bourse closures (London 31 July 1914 to 4 January 1915), Julian editions to 1918, the January 1917 cable published on 1 March. Affordances not yet used: the deferred half-rate cable (from 1912) and the week-end cable letter gave a third and fourth speed between wire and post; and the time-zone patchwork (Paris time to 1911, Petersburg time, Ottoman alla turca) is a latency confusion the forecast could price.
