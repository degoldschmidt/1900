# Historian's audit — C07 The Departure Is the Turn

## Verdict
Accurate; two precision corrections and one dating caution in the worked example.

## Corrections
1. **Historical grounding** — "whose editions change at seasonal changeovers … and were suspended in wartime" → only Bradshaw's Continental Guide (a British publication) ceased for the war; the Reichskursbuch and the Chaix continued, printing reduced Kriegsfahrplan and horaire de guerre services → the wartime edge data come from the national guides; the network thins, it does not go unpublished.
2. **A worked example of play** — "Berlin, late October 1905 … then the scheduled Tier-0 event fires: the October general strike" → the railway strike spread across Russia on 20–26 October (N.S.; 7–13 October O.S.) and the October Manifesto followed on 30 October; date the departure about 18–19 October N.S. so the rumours precede the closure → Berlin editions report Russian events in Gregorian dates while Petersburg papers and registers use Julian; the thirteen-day gap is the calendar tell the pitch itself cites.
3. **A worked example of play** — "the evening Nord-Express … or second class to Eydtkuhnen" → the Nord-Express was a train de luxe, first class only, running on fixed days (twice weekly) rather than nightly; the daily second-class alternative was the ordinary D-Zug to Eydtkuhnen → running days are a Bradshaw constraint as real as departure times.

## Notes
The pitch gets the hard things right: a change of train at the gauge break, the British registration asymmetry (nothing before August 1914; aliens 1914, subjects 1915, hotels 1920), Libau ice-free against the Petersburg approach, Paris time to 1911 and Julian registers to 31 January 1918. Two affordances it could still use: Russian exit formalities (the passport exchanged for a residence permit on entry, an exit permit needed to leave) make the Petersburg leg a gate at both ends; and the Trans-Siberian ran on Petersburg time across seven zones, so a published arrival hour differed from the station clock by up to seven hours, a planning trap no other era offers.
