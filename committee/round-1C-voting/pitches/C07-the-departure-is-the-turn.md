# Finalist Pitch — C07 The Departure Is the Turn

*Rank 1 of 20 · Borda 188/200 · mean score 8.60 · Lead author P01, audited by P03 (history), P02 (engine) and P09 (hook honesty)*

## One-line pitch
A spy's thirty-year life played as an itinerary: the world is a discrete-event simulation faced by the timetable and the diary, every decision point is a departure, and every verb — meet, cable, forge, trade, bribe, register — takes hours that must fit into the slack before a train that leaves without you.

## The hook — what is new
There is no abstract turn and no free time. The world advances through one queue of timestamped events and choices are made in the gaps between scheduled departures, so the connection — arriving to leaving, when you are stationary, visible and spending — is the atomic unit of tension. You plan on the subgraph you know from owned, dated guides and porters, and it goes stale as the network changes. Because the hunters ride and read the same timetable, time pressure and the hunt are the same object as travel.

## Core loop
- **Every 30 seconds:** Decide what fits before the departure you have provisionally booked — a bank drawing in business hours, the poste restante, a meeting, a cable, or waiting — and choose class, lodging and instrument, each priced in money, hours, health and trace.
- **Every 10 minutes:** Assemble a day of time-stamped slots: mail deliveries, bank hours, a 14:05 express, the hotel register that reaches the police tomorrow. A tight connection risks a seasonal delay; a loose one buys dead hours of exposure and expense. Interrupts — a telegram, a late train, a strike rumour — reopen the plan.
- **Every session (1–2 hours):** One commission carried across two or three cities on a chain of connections — gauge break, frontier hall, night boat. Every pause sits within sight of a departure, so sessions end aboard a night train with the world and the hunt advancing until next load.
- **Over a campaign (years of game time):** The network thickens and accelerates — tunnels, through services, motor cabs, airships, airlines — while borders harden (1914), currencies break (1917, 1923) and your files thicken. Routes are learned, re-bought, sold and forgotten; the same verbs grow faster and riskier; the diary becomes a life.

## The systems and how they interlock
- **Travel** (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): A date-versioned, multi-modal master graph of services built from monthly guides. The player routes on a known-edge overlay — learned date, source, confidence — while the simulator executes the truth and emits ghost-connection events when an edition has gone stale. A named berth writes a high-fidelity, persistent record; a third-class ticket reveals only mode and direction. Travel necessarily spends time, money and evidence, so every other system hangs from it.
- **Deception / conversation / coding** (C04, C12, C20): Two legends stay apart only while no connection could have carried one body between their recorded appearances — the alibi test is a fastest-path query. Conversations run on a fine-grained clock nested inside a slot; a cipher takes hours by hand or minutes by bureau; a cable leaves an office copy. Every assertion is a record, with an authorship field, in the store the hunters read.
- **Items / artifacts** (C06, C19): Papers, codebooks, luggage and crates hold itineraries of their own on the same queue — registered luggage follows the ticket, a crate moves at goods speed. Documents are tested in the frontier hall, a scheduled dwell of fifteen to ninety minutes at a gauge break, and their stamps are dated against the legend's itinerary.
- **Economy / business** (C08, C18, C15): Money is instruments with hours and places — a letter of credit draws only at listed correspondents in banking hours; gold is anonymous and seizable. Rent days and maturities are queue events; burn is continuous and income lumpy, so the ledger forces you onto the network. Commissions are located, time-windowed offers made mutually exclusive by travel time: accepting Baku forecloses Tangier.
- **Politics / warfare** (C10, C09, C03): The world calendar writes rows into one dated parameter layer the timetable reads: passport requirements per edge, registration coverage, police liaison, requisition and suspension of services. Crises climb ladders with literal deadlines; at general mobilisation military flows claim the civilian graph. Politics is felt as which trains run, which frontiers ask for papers and who may read the register you signed.
- **Time pressure & the world clock**: Native. Departures leave without you; windows close; the Meldezettel reaches the police within about a day; a frontier post receives its circular only in office hours and where liaison exists. Three cadences beat against each other — the day of slots, the institutional rhythm of registers and circulars, the macro calendar of crises and seasons.
- **The hunt** (hidden movement, evidence, hunters; C17, C05, C13, C16): Hunters never see you; they read records arriving on institutional clocks, hold weighted hypotheses advancing along the timetable you ride, cannot teleport, and cordon a city only where their men can be in time. Their notices move by cable, their bodies by train; the night train outruns tomorrow's newspaper but not tonight's wire. Your own trail is always visible as a self-forecast from your own acts; what they have concluded is not.
- **The life** (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends; C11, C04, C08): The diary is also the household's calendar — rent, remittances, club nights — and each kept appointment is a witness record. Health is spent by third class and long legs and recovered in weeks of world time that neither clock nor hunt pauses; ageing lengthens every timer; skills grow by use and rust by disuse; a year-end audit batches what accrued and stamps the coming year's known closures onto the calendar.

## A worked example of play
Berlin, late October 1905. A service's want (C18) pays for copied documents delivered to a handler in St Petersburg within six days; a three-month bill drawn under the same legend matures there on the seventh (C08). Before the evening Nord-Express: the bank closes at four, so the letter of credit is drawn first; the hotel's Meldezettel reaches the Polizeipräsidium tomorrow (C17), after the train has gone. The planner offers the sleeper — fast, rested, a berth under the legend's name — or second class to Eydtkuhnen, anonymous, a night sitting up: one cost vector (C02). The known-world overlay marks the Wirballen connection at high confidence from the October edition bought that morning; September's showed a withdrawn Sunday working. The evening edition carries strike rumours from the Russian lines — the disruption layer's forecast — so the plan buys a day of buffer and a fallback: a Stettin–Petersburg steamer edge learned from a shipping agent's cable, still open before the ice. At the frontier everyone changes trains; the passport hall records the name presented. The self-forecast (C13) shows the Berlin description reaching the post only if the Prussian police have cabled, and only in office hours. Then the scheduled Tier-0 event fires: the October general strike closes the Russian graph edge by edge. The sleeper is stranded; the steamer sails. The player who bought the buffer reaches Petersburg by sea, meets the bill and finds the handler's window shut by a day — the lapsed offer reports a rival delivered. The player who did not sees the bill protested and gazetted, and that name's heat rise in a city they are locked inside.

## Why it is captivating and addictive
The champion statements converge on one word: honest. "The scheduler is the game" (P02); the timetable was "the period's most pervasive real constraint on a spy" (P03); C07 is "a frame rather than a feature" (P04); "the night-train session end is the pool's most honest 'one more leg'" (P09). Zeigarnik tension: a provisionally booked departure is an open loop until the errands that justify it are done. Near-miss: a connection made or missed by eleven minutes, 80 Days' strongest reinforcement, and here every verb produces one. Goal gradient: effort rises as the visible departure nears, so the player rarely pauses. Mastery in the head: slack, buffers and the known network are skills the veteran feels, not stats. Approach–avoidance: the sleeper you want and the register you fear are one purchase. Information gap: the evening edition, the stale guide and the frontier's unknown demands must be closed. Emergent narrative: alignments of the three cadences — a deadline, a frontier closure and a cordon in one week — are set pieces nobody wrote. The natural stopping point is the hotel, and it has an honest cost, the register; the alternative is the 23:40 through train reaching Berlin before the bourse opens, so the player stops aboard it, on a cliffhanger the schedule wrote.

## Historical grounding
Accuracy is the rules. The master graph is built from period guides — Bradshaw's monthly Continental Guide, the Reichskursbuch, the Indicateur Chaix — whose editions change at seasonal changeovers (typically 1 May and 1 October) and were suspended in wartime. Times pass through local regimes: Russian railways run on St Petersburg time, Russian registers keep the Julian calendar until 31 January 1918, France keeps Paris time until 1911 — a forged date in the wrong calendar is a tell. At the gauge breaks (Eydtkuhnen/Wirballen, Alexandrowo; Irun, Port Bou) passengers change trains in a hall of passport desks, customs and a telegraph office. Registration is a row per jurisdiction and date: Meldezettel in Germany and Austria, innkeepers' registers in France, passport desks in Russian hotels; in Britain nobody is registered before August 1914 (aliens from 5 August 1914, British subjects from the National Registration Act 1915, general hotel registers from 1920). The network is the progression: Simplon 1906, Lötschberg 1913; Trans-Siberian through running 1903–04, the Baikal gap closed 1904, regular Circum-Baikal service 1905, the Amur line 1916; DELAG airships 1910–14, the London–Paris airline 1919; the Orient Express suspended 1914, the Simplon-Orient Express 1919. Disruption is dated: Baltic ice closes the Petersburg approach while Libau stays open; strikes in Russia (October 1905), France (1910) and Britain (1911, 1919); civilian trains suspended in Germany and France in early August 1914; the wartime Haparanda–Torneå route to Russia. Passport-free Western Europe before 1914, Russian and Ottoman passport regimes, universal controls from 1914 and the League's 1920 booklet are edge attributes with validity windows.

## Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies
One global priority queue of timestamped events with total ordering (time, priority, insertion id). Trains, mails, bank hours, register deliveries, maturities, liaison dates, crisis rungs, hunter moves and the player's intentions are all events; entities subscribe to event types; handlers are data. Every stochastic draw is seeded, so a save is seed plus input log and replay is exact — which makes C16's autopsy and C10's inquest computable and is the debugging tool. Timetables are monthly tables (edge, calendar bitmap, departure, arrival, class, fare, supplement); routing is connection-scan over the time-expanded graph; reachability sets are precomputed per timetable period so dozens of hunters cost microseconds. Level-of-detail scheduling runs the player's city and legs in flight at minute granularity and the rest at day granularity: a quiet winter passes in minutes, a closing cordon takes an hour. The known-world overlay is a sparse per-agent set of {edge, learned date, source, confidence}; the planner routes on it, the simulator executes the truth. Node dwell — the connection — emits records consumed by C17, C02 (exposure) and C08 (expense). The communications graph (C13) is a second edge table of latencies, office hours and jurisdiction gating over which records reach their readers — C17's lag model. The dated parameter layer (C10) supplies edge requirements, registration coverage, requisition and liaison as rows keyed by (edge or jurisdiction, date, tier); a new decade adds rows, not code. The other nineteen candidates attach as event types and handlers on this queue; none runs without it.

## Progression and endings (as systems)
What grows: the known network (edges learned, confidence raised, corridors saleable), slack mastery in the player's head, each legend's record-set and credit, skills exercised. What decays: editions at every changeover; unused skills toward a floor; unattended legends; health spent by cheap travel and recovered in world time. What accumulates: records in institutional ledgers, debts with maturities, dossiers per name, a file the alliance calendar may one day copy to a new reader. Endings are system states reached on the timetable (C11): retirement is reaching a non-extraditing haven with wealth in a currency that still converts before 1914, 1917 or 1923 slams a door; capture is a cordon closing on a city whose next departure you cannot make; ruin is a protested bill gazetted under the name you wear; succession keeps the world running and hands the next agent every debt and file; routes and codes persist only in the player's head.

## Risks and mitigations
- **"A skeleton, not a motive engine" (P07; echoed by P05, P06, P10).** True by design: C07 is the clock; the motive engines are arranged around it — C18's windowed, exclusive commissions and C08's burn and maturities are the reasons to leave the hotel, C01's reconciliation dates the reckonings. Rule: the slack must always have claimants.
- **Spreadsheet feel (P01.01's own risk).** Strong defaults — "next sensible departure", automatic edition purchase at stations, a porter — make planning depth opt-in; the diary, not a Gantt chart, is the face.
- **Punishing randomness (P04.02).** Delay distributions are shown before commitment; a forecast channel (editions, agents' cables, insiders) always exists, so buffer is priced.
- **Skipping one's life (P02.01).** The diary shows what will pass before time jumps; interrupts stop the jump; conversation and pursuit run on a nested clock.
- **Off-screen death (P09.01).** Deferred leg resolution never kills without warnings visible before boarding; the hotel pause stays cheap enough to be a real choice.
- **Unfair ghost connections (P04.01, P03.07).** Confidence cues on every known edge; staleness bites only at changeovers and wartime suspensions; recovery options always exist.
- **Dead time on long legs (P04.10).** Sealed legs carry an action budget — ciphers, forgery, letters, conversation — and news arrives only at ports or by wireless, so arrival is a reveal.
- **Data volume and accuracy.** Model the trunk network exactly from period guides and abstract minor lines to regional defaults; dates are table rows, cheap to audit and fix.

## Scope sketch
- **MVP (vertical slice):** One timetable period (a 1908 summer edition); the Western and Central European trunk network plus the Berlin–Petersburg corridor with its gauge break; six to eight cities at minute detail; three classes and sleepers; the diary and slot planner on the event queue with exact replay; one legend; one hunting service reading records on the same timetable (C05-lite); one commission type with windows (C18-lite); rent and one bill maturity (C08-lite).
