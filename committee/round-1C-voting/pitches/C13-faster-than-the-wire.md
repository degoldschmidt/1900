# Finalist Pitch — C13 Faster Than the Wire: The Information Front

*Rank 5 of 20 · Borda 145/200 · mean score 7.50 · Lead author P04, audited by P03 (history), P02 (engine) and P09 (hook honesty)*

## One-line pitch
A spy life-sim with two maps, the railway you ride and the wire that carries news of you, where every act is a race between your body and your description, and knowing a thing an hour before the bourse is both your income and the evidence against you.

## The hook — what is new
The period had two networks running at different speeds, and this game simulates both as graphs with real latencies. Every fact about an identity or an event is an entity travelling at its medium's speed: minutes between open telegraph offices, a night by mail train, five days by liner, an edition cycle through the agencies, a week for a photograph by post; the player's body travels on the timetable. The hunt, the newspaper, the bourse and the player's forecast of where their own description has reached are one propagation model read from different seats, so dread and opportunity are the same quantity, latency, and the player always sees their own front but never the hunters' minds.

## Core loop
- **Every 30 seconds:** choose how an act travels (in person, courier, letter, cable in commercial code) and read the planner's self-forecast beside the fare; buy or skip the edition; decide whether a headline without details is enough to act on.
- **Every 10 minutes:** a day of edition times, bourse sessions, office hours and mail collections: a fact acquired at 16:00 opens a window that closes when the agency wire lands at the bourse tomorrow; a visible act writes records that reach their readers hours or days later; a leg is built so the body arrives where the description has not; a register, frontier post or bank counter is a checkpoint against what has arrived there.
- **Every session (1–2 hours):** one commission generates two fronts, the secret's news and your own description, moving at different speeds through different channels; you route body, money and messages through the seams (offices that close at night, police who do not talk across a frontier, the sea beyond wireless range); the climax is a checkpoint the forecast rates at 40 per cent, and tomorrow's edition tells you whether you were right.
- **Over a campaign (years of game time):** the communications graph rewires on the world clock: cable redundancy after 1902, wireless pushing the wire to sea from the mid-1900s, censors and cut cables from August 1914, Russia going dark in 1917; mastery is knowing the latencies; your archive of office copies never shrinks; the prescience accumulator judges how often you were right too early.

## The systems and how they interlock
Describe each as rules/resources/states and, crucially, how it feeds the others:
- **Travel** (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): the transport graph is C07's time-expanded timetable; a leg costs hours, a fare and a class, and class sets trace fidelity (C02). C13 adds a second graph over the same places: telegraph and post offices, coast stations, agencies, bourses, banks, consulates and police posts, joined by channel edges with a latency distribution, office hours, capacity, a jurisdiction gate and a copy rule. Routing is a latency play: a night express beats tomorrow's edition but not tonight's wire; a wire needs open offices at both ends and, across a frontier, a liaison agreement (C03); a sea leg beyond wireless range is dark; in reverse, you route so that your letters of introduction precede you.
- **Deception / conversation / coding**: a wanted notice is a cable, a portrait-parlé description travelling as a fact with a confidence value, the photograph following by post days later, so the cheapest defence is to change the thing described (C04: switch legend where only the old name has arrived). Commercial code compresses at so much a word, private cipher conceals but attracts censors (C20), and every cable leaves an office copy whose readers list (C17) grows under warrant. Planted news is a provisional fact that reverts when contradicted; a poisoned liaison request is a fact with false authorship; room talk (C12) spreads as rumour at post speed across C04's gossip bridges.
- **Items / artifacts**: a telegram exists three times, handed-in form, office copy and delivered copy, each with its own readers; editions are dated objects whose value dies with the next edition; code books, a broker's book, ticker tape and a registration slip are items that are also records; goods in luggage and crates (C19) carry city prices that are themselves facts in transit.
- **Economy / business**: the news-latency market. Each bourse (London, Paris, Berlin, Vienna, St Petersburg, New York; Constantinople for Ottoman paper) holds a fact set and reprices an instrument at its next session only when the relevant fact has arrived; a fact known before the agency wire opens a window of hours. Profit is bounded emergently, not capped: depth (a position above a fraction of daily turnover moves the price against you and writes a notable-dealing record), evidence (every trade is a timestamped C17 record correlable with later news) and prescience (C10). City price vectors shaped by tariff walls and season run the luggage trade (C19); market income pays C08's burn, is laundered through C15's books when large, and never replaces commissions.
- **Politics / warfare**: the diplomatic calendar decides which police wires cross which frontiers (C03's matrix gates the edge); regime rows in C10's parameter layer switch censorship on, cut cables and reroute traffic through neutral copy points; crises (C09) swell cable volume so queues lengthen exactly when windows are richest. C10's prescience rule: moves correlated with an upcoming Tier 0 event, rather than with a fact whose acquisition is on record, raise a per-faction prescience score; thresholds trigger inquiry, frozen accounts or a recruitment approach; cover reasons discount it; Tier 1 jitter keeps the bet a bet.
- **Time pressure & the world clock**: every element is a scheduled event in C07's queue: edition times, bourse sessions, office hours, mail trains, the arrival of each fact at each reader. The world emits news with a propagation profile per event type, so the player always acts on information hours to weeks old; the lag is the pressure, and "wait for the evening edition" is a slot decision like any other.
- **The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters)**: C13 is the wire half of C05: a notice reaches a post when its fact arrives; hunters reweight hypotheses only on records that reached their institution and move watchers by train; checkpoints query local knowledge only. The player's instruments are the self-forecast, computed from their own act log and published channel parameters and never from faction stores, and C16's windows (editions, agony columns, the interrogation room, the autopsy). Tactics: outrun the front, slow it (a bribed clerk, a frontier crossed before the relay, a wartime cut), change the described thing, or feed it a false sighting that C17's scepticism learner may discount.
- **The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends)**: the life rides the same wires: references and remittances travel by post and telegraphic transfer at the same latencies, so a legend's record-set must arrive before its body; standing (C04) spreads by gossip and reaches a city before you; a telegraphic transfer (C08) is instant and copied; households read the editions too; staying ahead of the wire costs sleep on C02's dial; office copies left in 1903 wait for C03's archive reviews in 1919.

## A worked example of play
Constantinople, the second week of October 1908: Bulgaria's declaration of independence and the annexation of Bosnia have frozen police liaison between the Porte and Vienna. A want (C18): a Vienna bank will pay for the Porte's decision on a railway concession tender before it is gazetted. The player obtains it at 16:00 on day 0 and two fronts start. The decision's front: gazette tomorrow morning, the agency correspondent's cable about 10:00, Vienna by 11:00, evening editions, and the Vienna bourse repricing the concession company's shares at the day 1 session. The description's front begins only when the clerk's absence is noticed at 08:00 on day 1: an Ottoman circular reaches the Bulgarian frontier post by wire within two hours; nothing is asked of the Bulgarian police; the request to Vienna travels by embassy, which C03's matrix prices at two days this month. At 17:00 on day 0 the player hands in a fourteen-word commercial-code cable to a Vienna broker; its office copy is a C17 record whose readers are the cable company and, under an Ottoman warrant weeks away, the police. The broker buys at the opening of the day 1 session, under the depth threshold, two hours before the agency wire reprices. The body boards the Orient Express that evening: the Bulgarian frontier passes about 06:00 on day 1, four hours before the post is hot; Serbia has been asked nothing; Vienna is some forty hours out. The self-forecast puts the Vienna police's chance of holding the description on arrival at 15 per cent, rising past 60 for a second night, because a registration slip is read within a day and the embassy request lands on day 3. The player chooses: alight at Budapest, where the request arrives later still; take payment by telegraphic transfer (instant, copied) or a letter-of-credit drawing (a signature record); register the Vienna night under the second legend. On day 3 the Vienna evening edition carries an agency paragraph on a theft at the Porte with a description (C16's window); the prescience accumulator is untouched because the fact's acquisition is on record; the cable copy sleeps until a review (C03) reads it.

## Why it is captivating and addictive
The tension/relief cycle is the checkpoint: register, frontier, counter, each a query against what has arrived, resolved by institutions on their own clocks, so near-misses are earned rather than rolled (P09's ballot: "a complete, legible race ... near-misses are earned, not rolled"). Dread is legible without omniscience: the self-forecast turns anxiety into planning while the hunters' real holdings stay behind C16's windows, which P03 and P08 both name as what keeps the race honest. Information-gap curiosity runs the news half: a headline without detail is a gap the mind must close (P09.05), so the evening edition is irresistible; the market makes the gap pay in numbers while the hunt makes it cost in sleep. Mastery lives in the head: that a provincial office closes at eight, that a liner is dark for three days, is competence in the self-determination sense. Meaningful choice is the speed-permanence trade on every message: the cable instant and copied, the letter slow and private, the courier a body with a timetable. Emergent narrative is the record itself, reconstructed in the autopsy. Natural stopping points are the last edition and the bourse's close; players push through because a session ends aboard a train with the forecast showing where the description will be at arrival, and the window they are inside closes at a published hour. No variable-ratio schedule is used; every uncertainty is an institution's clock.

## Historical grounding
Accuracy is the latency table. The submarine cable network was dense by 1895 and redundant after the All Red Line closed in 1902; a transatlantic word cost about a shilling and companies kept office copies, readable under warrant; commercial codes (Bentley's from 1906) compressed meaning for anyone holding the edition. News moved through the Reuters–Havas–Wolff ring, each relaying its territory, into multi-edition dailies in the capitals and a day later into the provinces; Exchange Telegraph tickers had fed London since 1872. Wireless enters on the world clock: coast stations and shipboard sets from the first years of the century, liner news bulletins from 1904, and in 1910 a wanted notice overtook a transatlantic liner for the first time; before that a ship at sea was five dark days. Post ran at night-mail speed with several deliveries a day and pneumatic tubes in Paris, Berlin and Vienna; there was no transatlantic telephony until 1927. Police knowledge travelled by telegraphed circular, posted description cards and fingerprint cards from 1901, through liaison that followed treaties (C03). The rules change inside the period: the November 1905 Russian postal-telegraph strike blacks out the wire; on 5 August 1914 the Channel cables to Germany are cut and German traffic to the Americas passes through neutral copy points; censors read cables and admit only deposited codes; a Press Bureau filters editions; most bourses close at the end of July 1914, London from 31 July to 4 January 1915; Russian editions date in the Julian calendar until 31 January 1918 (C17's clock rule); the intercepted cable of January 1917, published on 1 March, is the archetype of an office copy that fights back.

## Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies
Two graphs over shared place nodes: C07's time-expanded timetable and a communications graph of institution nodes and channel edges, each edge a per-date parameter row (latency distribution, office hours, capacity, C03's jurisdiction gate, censorship state, copy retention) in C10's single parameter layer, written by macro-events. A fact is C17's tuple (subject, predicate, value, confidence, source, time, authorship, readers); propagation is not a second mechanism but C17's lag-and-reader model computed on this graph: an act or event emits a fact with a propagation profile (which channel types carry it, with what degradation) and the scheduler posts arrival events at reader institutions into C07's priority queue. Evaluation is lazy and hop-bounded: arrivals materialise only when a reader queries (a frontier post, a hunter reweighting, a bourse session), so cost scales with queries, not facts in flight; facts exist only for subjects of interest; institutions never read ground truth. The self-forecast reuses the propagation code on the player's own act log with published parameters and no faction state, so it is cheap and cannot leak. Markets are functions: a bourse's price is a deterministic function of the facts that have arrived there plus a seeded mean-reverting term and a depth response, with no per-tick simulation; trade records are C17 rows with broker and bourse as readers, and the who-dealt-before-the-news rule runs only when a service investigates. Every latency draw is seeded and every arrival totally ordered in C07's queue, so a save is seed plus input log and C16's autopsy replays the exact chain. Authoring is tabular: offices and hours, latencies by year, agency territories, edition times, censorship rows, propagation profiles per event type, structured headline templates; a decade adds rows. Scale: a few hundred institution nodes, thousands of facts per campaign, each with a handful of arrivals.

## Progression and endings (as systems)
What grows: latency knowledge held in the player's head; a network of institution-bound assets with reservation prices and leak risk (telegraph clerks, agency stringers, brokers, a cable address) that can be bought, burned or turned; cover reasons for being early, which discount prescience. What decays: facts' confidence and readers' attention; a cold description stops being queried but is never deleted. What accumulates: office copies and trade records in archives that C03's reviews may reopen; the prescience score; the market's depth response to a known name. End states are C11's, reached through this layer: retirement holds when no live front reaches the haven's readers; capture is a checkpoint querying a fact that arrived first; ruin is a frozen account after a prescience inquiry or an exchange closed on an open position; turning is C05's inverse mode run from the wire's side; the second agent inherits archives that remember the first.

## Risks and mitigations
- Collapse into flight (P01, 9th: "narrows the life toward flight and arbitrage"; P05, P06 and P10 agree). Mitigation: most fronts are opportunities, and the same layer is the player's only window on the world, so most play is reading and using latency; the self-forecast usually reports that no reader holds you yet, so standing still is the common right answer; running costs health (C02) and households (C04).
- The life is absent (P10, 14th: "after amendment its substrate is C17 and the life is absent"; P02: "a spine for the pressure half of the brief"). Honest answer: C13 is the pressure-and-opportunity spine and is pitched coupled to C04 and C11, whose legends, standings and remittances ride these wires; it does not replace them.
- A stock minigame (P02.09's own risk). Bounds are emergent (depth, trades as evidence, prescience) and income is tied to C08's burn and C15's laundering.
- Latency tuning: if the wire always wins the race is unwinnable; if offices never matter it is trivial. Office hours, liaison gates, crisis queues and the sea are the designed seams, and the MVP exists to tune the margins (P05.04: postal speeds near express speeds narrow races).
- The minimap exploit (P01.06's first risk): closed by A-P08-1; hunter inference appears only through C16.
- Replay foreknowledge (P03.08): Tier 0 dates are immovable, Tier 1 jitter and micro-events keep timing approximate, and C10 prices prescience as evidence.
- Teaching the network without a lecture (P02.02): editions and the autopsy show latencies; C05's inverse mode lets the player run the wire from the other chair.
- Procedural news text: structured headlines and tables, never articles.

## Scope sketch
- **MVP (vertical slice):** one corridor, Paris–Vienna–Constantinople, 1908–1912, about thirty place nodes; channels: cable, post, editions and one bourse pair (Vienna, Paris); facts about one identity and one instrument class; the self-forecast; C05's hunters at minimum strength; C16's editions and autopsy; tuning target: a night express beats the edition, the wire beats the body except across a closed office or an unagreed frontier.
- **Full game:** Europe with liners and the Trans-Siberian, 1895–1925; all channel types including wireless by date, censorship and cable cuts; six to eight bourses and the agency ring; archives and reviews; several legends, each with its own front; the parameter layer authored per year.
