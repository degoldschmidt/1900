# Finalist Pitch — C04 The Legend Portfolio: Several Lives at Once

*Rank 3 of 20 · Borda 181/200 · mean score 8.30 · Lead author P10, audited by P03 (history), P02 (engine) and P09 (hook honesty)*

## One-line pitch
Legends are the unit of account: each cover identity owns its own flat, bank account, debts, standing and police file, keeps running in its city whether or not you are wearing it, and the hunt advances only by linking two names.

## The hook — what is new
The player is never the unit the game accounts for; the name is. A legend is an entity owning a fact-graph of claims whose weights decay unless fed by acts under that name, plus a residence, accounts, obligations, a monthly burn, a standing per circle and one dossier per hunting service. Hunters never see a body, only records under names, so pursuit is a linkage problem rather than a chase, and the canonical link is decidable: two names whose records could have been produced by one body on the real timetable are a link candidate, while records that no connection could reconcile keep the names apart. Failure is therefore granular. You lose a name, not the game.

## Core loop
- **Every 30 seconds:** Choose which name acts, and how: sign the hotel slip as A or B; pay from A's letter of credit or in anonymous gold; post from B's accommodation address or by hand. A linkage-risk indicator computed from your own records warns before you arrive as A on a ticket B bought.
- **Every 10 minutes:** A day in one city under one name: feed its record-set (a bank drawing, a club lunch, a registration renewed), collect what piled up at its address, read the own-trail view of what this name has written lately, and decide whether it stays live, rests or retires.
- **Every session (1–2 hours):** A circuit. Plan by timetable so that unattended lives do not go stale (rent on the quarter-day, unanswered mail, a lapsed registration) and so that the last record of one name and the first record of the next are provably incompatible for a single body. Arrive and face what the simulation materialises: callers, fines, a creditor's letter, an offer at the club.
- **Over a campaign (years of game time):** Legends appreciate (record-set depth, credit, standing) and accumulate exposure (dossier size, links); August 1914 makes nationality a life-or-death attribute and February 1915 binds one face to every British name it is issued under; names are burnt, retired, sold or linked, the files thicken, and the campaign ends when one clean name reaches a haven (C11).

## The systems and how they interlock
- **Travel** (network, schedules, fares, classes, traces): the circuit is a timetable plan on C07's scheduler. Verification latency is routing: a Berlin employer answers a hunter's inquiry in a day, an overseas registry in weeks, so a claim is safe for as long as the mails take. The timetable is also the alibi: C14's habit profiles and a fastest-path query between two presence records decide whether two names can be one body. Class must match the legend (C02); frontiers test the presented name's papers (C06).
- **Deception / conversation / coding**: every assertion in a room (C12), on a hotel form or in a report (C01) is a claim written to the acting legend's fact-graph; claims and world records are one C17 tuple differing only in authorship. The legend ledger lists each name's claims, holders, verification cost and corroboration. Each name reports in its own cipher (C20); a captured cipher is a link.
- **Items / artifacts**: papers, visiting cards, letters of introduction, monogrammed luggage, tailors' labels and typewriters belong to a name and can betray it: one hand in two registers, one trunk under two names, one typeface in two correspondences. Folded options: a seizable notebook as the only private record in hard mode (P05.12), and a bounded donor identity, a vanished person's record-set plus a small hot set of acquaintances (P05.11).
- **Economy / business**: one ledger per legend (C08). Each name has a class with a monthly burn and a credibility that tracks spend against class; underfunded names decay, overfunded ones attract thieves. Credit is sized to record-set depth, so a thin name cannot borrow enough to make burning it pay, and a burned name's creditor becomes a budgeted private hunter seeking the link to your other names. Households anchor a legend: rent, staff with discretion and loyalty values, safe house, mail drop, cache, and a talk rate that writes witness records; a firm (C15) is a name's visible means of support.
- **Politics / warfare**: nationality is a legend attribute read by the regime layer (C10): internment of enemy-alien men of military age, call-up papers, property under custodians from 1914, expropriation from 1917. Liaison is a linkage source: when allied services compare files at a dated reconciliation (C01, C03) and know you under different names, the comparison itself can join them; 1917 spills a fallen service's archive.
- **Time pressure & the world clock**: records age and witnesses forget, so links decay; verification clocks run only while someone checks; rent and bills fall on calendars; registration slips reach the police on institutional lags; the document regime flips on fixed dates (August 1914, February and November 1915, 1920).
- **The hunt (hidden movement, evidence, hunters)**: each service holds one dossier per name as a filtered view of C17, and C05's hypotheses propagate per name. Linkage events: a witness who has seen both faces, a draft endorsed from one name to another, a photograph matched across files, gossip crossing a bridging institution where one person sits in two circles, a liaison reconciliation, or presence records the timetable can reconcile. Burning a legend zeroes its heat but forfeits its accounts, contracts, households and friendships and hands every asserted fact to the hunters; destroying the one linking record can sever a link instead.
- **The life (ageing, health, households, reputation, legends)**: standing is held per name per circle (a mess, a bourse, an émigré colony), decays toward neutral with time and distance, and gossip crosses bridges at a rate, so a scandal under one name surfaces elsewhere weeks later. Names do not age; the one body behind them does (C11): unchanging portrait-parlé fields (height, ears, scars) and wounds (C02) leak across names into every dossier at once. Retirement (C11) needs a name clean enough to live under.

## A worked example of play
Spring 1914. Name A is a British-papered textile traveller: a London accommodation address (nobody is registered in Britain before August 1914), a bank account with a letter of indication, a club, weekday second-class trips to manufacturing towns. Name B is a mining engineer on a second British passport from the lax pre-war declaration system, with a Meldezettel at the Berlin Polizeipräsidium, a flat, a housekeeper, a Berlin account and standing in a technical society; the Berlin police hold a thin dossier on B since an inquiry agency found no such engineer in any directory. A must draw its letter of credit in London on Wednesday; B was recorded at its Berlin bank on Tuesday afternoon. The fastest Berlin–London connection via the Hook of Holland night boat takes about a day, so the records are incompatible for one body and the engine keeps the names apart; the housekeeper also pays B's quarter-day rent in cash, a dated receipt placing B in Berlin again, at the price that she now knows the pattern and her loyalty becomes a target. In late July the papers and cable volumes signal the ladder (C09); before the civilian timetable collapses the player must retire B cleanly, closing flat and account over days the hunt does not pause, or burn it, handing the Berlin police B's specimen signature, Meldezettel and the housekeeper's testimony that B's mail went to a London address, now a link candidate to A. From August, B's nationality makes its flat and account enemy property under a custodian and its wearer liable to internment; from February 1915 renewing either passport binds the player's photograph to both names in one archive (C03), a link waiting for a review.

## Why it is captivating and addictive
The hook is honest in P09's sense: every pressure is an institution's real clock, never a manufactured timer. Endowment and the IKEA effect do the work: a legend built over months of registrations, references and trades is a genuine loss when burnt, one the hunt can inflict without ending the game, and choosing which name dies is rich in anticipated regret. Plate-spinning gives sustained engagement with legible failure: three to five lives, each with a rent day, a maturing claim and a lapsing registration, produce the appointment compulsion of "I must be in Petersburg before quarter-day". The double life is a fantasy made mechanical: trusted in one circle and dangerous in another under different names, juggling the player can see. Dread of linkage is an information gap, since only C16's windows say which records a service has read. A new legend offers the clean-slate appeal that makes roguelikes compulsive, inside one continuous career. Natural stopping points are arrivals, when a city's mail materialises, and the end of a circuit; players push through because a departure is already in sight (C07), a verification clock is running on yesterday's claim, and an unattended life is a day from going stale. In the panel's words: "identity is the join key of this entire game; failure is granular, lose a name, not the game, which is what lets forty hours of pursuit stay fair" (P08); "the life sim and the hunt become one object" (P09).

## Historical grounding
Accuracy is the rule set. Where a name can be invented is a property of the registration regime: Britain is where people are invented, Germany where inventions die. German and Austrian Meldewesen send a slip to the police within a day and keep resident cards; French innkeepers keep police registers; Russian hotels carry the passport to the police desk and yard-keepers report to the precinct; in Britain nobody is registered before August 1914 (aliens from the Aliens Restriction Order of 5 August 1914, British subjects from the National Registration Act 1915, general hotel registers from the Aliens Order 1920), so a British legend can be invented until 1915 and a foreign one only until August 1914. Hunters audit legends with the period's tools: inquiry agencies, professional and consular registers queried by post or cable, portrait-parlé cards, fingerprint files from 1901, typeface comparison, laundry marks and tailors' labels. The document regime changes inside the rules: passport-free Western Europe against Russian and Ottoman visas before 1914; German and French passports in the first days of August 1914; British photographs from February 1915 and compulsory passports from November 1915; the 1920 League booklet. Internment is staged (selective from August 1914, general in Germany from November 1914 and in Britain from May 1915), enemy property passes to custodians, and 1917 expropriates every Russian household and account. Per the Charter errata, alibi arithmetic uses a change of train at the gauge break and Trans-Siberian through running from 1903–04.

## Engine: dynamic events, decisions, actions, dependencies
The identity is an entity with components: a fact-graph of claims (C17 tuples with weight and timestamp), residences (household entities with staff, cache and talk rate), instruments and obligations (C08), registrations, a nationality, a standing vector per circle, papers (C06 items) and a derived heat per service. Every act emits identity-tagged records into the one append-only store; a dossier is an indexed filter over it, so hunters pay nothing to keep files. Linkage is a rule pass run only on new records against indexes: shared handwriting or typeface ids, shared object ids (a trunk, an endorsement chain), witness co-presence, photograph matches, circle-overlap propagation, liaison reconciliation, and timetable compatibility as a fastest-path query on C07's time-expanded graph, bounded and cached per city pair and month. Unattended identities run at low level of detail: their obligation events fire with aggregated outcomes, and on arrival the engine materialises specifics deterministically from the seed, so cost follows attention, not the number of lives. Verification clocks are lazy, spawned only when a hunter checks a claim, with latency from the communications and travel graphs. Burning cascades through a dependency graph (contacts, accounts and households bind to the name), and every link event carries provenance, so C16's autopsy replays exactly which record joined two names. Content is declarative: fact types, link rules, registration coverage and lag per jurisdiction and date (rows of C10's parameter layer), class burn tables, institution templates and donor record-set generators. Determinism is C07's: seed plus input log replays the portfolio exactly.

## Progression and endings (as systems)
What grows: record-set depth, credit, standing, households, and the player's knowledge of each country's disclosure texture. What decays: unfed claim weights, standing toward neutral, links as witnesses forget, registrations as they lapse. What never decays: dossiers, archives, the body's description fields. A legend has its own end states: rested (cheap, decaying), retired cleanly (slow, expensive), sold (a clean legend is an item with provenance), burnt (instant; every fact becomes evidence) or linked (its dossier merges into another's). The life's end states are C11's, each a query over the portfolio: retirement holds only for a name whose heat is below threshold in every service with reach into the chosen haven and whose assets sit in a stable currency; capture is a dossier that reconstructed the body behind the names; succession passes standings held by names an organisation knew.

## Risks and mitigations
- *Cognitive load.* Cap live identities at three to five; onboard with one life and add a second only when the first has a record-set worth protecting; the legend ledger must be the best interface in the game.
- *Upkeep as chores.* Maintenance must generate opportunities (callers, offers, introductions at a fed address) and be delegable to staff who leak; bookkeeping aggregates per name.
- *Legend hoarding and borrow-then-burn.* Burn by class, reactivation events for dormant names, credit sized to record-set depth, creditors who become hunters.
- *Hunter omniscience or arbitrariness.* Links come only from records, reachability is noisy, every capture has an autopsy, and pre-photograph face matching must stay a filing operation weaker than a photograph.
- *Pre-1914 may feel easy.* Russia and Ottoman lands are high-paper regions; archives make early carelessness expensive later (C03).
- *The panel ranked the clock first.* Rightly: this concept assumes C07 as the scheduler and C17 as the record store and attaches to both without modification.
- *Taste.* Donor identities stay institutional record-sets, never authored lives.

## Scope sketch
- **MVP (vertical slice):** two legends, three cities on one corridor (London, Berlin, a Low Countries port), 1912–1915 spanning the August 1914 flip and the 1915 photographs; one hunting service with per-name dossiers and the full link rules; three registration regimes; the legend ledger and linkage-risk indicator; burn and clean retirement; C07's timetable for the corridor; no donor identities or notebook.
- **Full game:** three to five live legends across the continent, all regimes 1895–1925, circles and gossip bridges, households with staff, name-bound instruments and creditors (C08), liaison linkage (C01, C03), the body and its tells (C02, C11), donor identities and the notebook as options.
